# The Demon to Hunt

> [!scene] [[Город Рэйкацу|Reikatsu]], 106 NE, spring

![[Assets/Images/The_demon_to_hunt_1.jpg]]

Rain had been falling since before dusk. It came down without anger, without thunder, without the dignity of a storm. It simply descended, thin and endless, as if the sky had forgotten some ancient grief and was trying, drop by drop, to remember it. The narrow street lay under it like a wounded thing: black mud between the stones, puddles trembling beneath the eaves, paper lanterns swollen with dampness and giving off a tired amber light.

[[Лосфер|Losfer]] sat beneath the wooden awning of a closed tea house, one knee raised, one hand resting loosely on the hilt of his sword.

The city around him was unfamiliar, though it belonged to his homeland. Such places always did. The same curved roofs. The same rain-darkened cedar. The same paper screens glowing faintly behind latticed windows. The same little charms hanging from doorways to ward off hungry spirits. And yet every town had a different silence, as every grave had a different name.

Water gathered at the edge of his hood and fell from it in slow drops. One slid down his cheek. Or perhaps it had begun beneath the blindfold. No one could tell. That was convenient.

![[Assets/Images/The_demon_to_hunt_2.jpg]]

For a long while, even the voice inside his head said nothing. Then, softly—almost playfully, almost afraid of frightening him—[[Лосфи|Losfy]] spoke. “What is wrong, my sweet? Why are you so sad again? Come on… let us imagine something more interesting instead… mm? Something warm. Something wicked. Something that makes your heart remember it is still alive.”

Losfer’s fingers tightened around the scabbard. He did not look up as he answered her without moving his lips. “That is precisely what I want least right now. Your ‘interesting’ things frighten me.”

For a moment, there was only rain. Then her voice returned, lower this time, velvet-dark and wounded at the edges. “But… I do not invent them by myself, darling. I only…”

“I know.” Losfer’s answer came too quickly. A few drops struck the back of his hand. He watched them break against his skin. “That is what frightens me even more.”

The silence inside him changed shape. Losfy did not answer at once. He could feel her there—only a voice behind his thoughts. A girl locked behind the wall of his own skull. A demon with no hands to touch him. A beloved with no arms to hold him.

“Do you hate me when I bring those things to the surface?”

Losfer breathed out through his nose. It was not a laugh. “No.” He swallowed. “I hate that they are there before you bring them.”

From somewhere down the street came the faint clack of wooden sandals through mud. A shutter closed. A dog barked once, then thought better of it. Above the rooftops, the sky was the color of old iron.

“Then hate the mirror, not the reflection.”

“I am tired of mirrors.”

“I know.” Her voice softened. “That is why you blindfolded yourself.”

Losfer lowered his head. The black cloth around his eyes was soaked through. It clung to him like a second sorrow. To others, it was a sign of his calling: his body was a prison for the demon, and the cloth allowed him to carry out the work entrusted to him by the goddess of souls and not frighten the common folk with those red eyes. For him, however, today was simpler. A mercy. No one could see his eyes.

Losfy asked, “Do you not like our adventures in dreams anymore?”

He gave a hollow sound, too bitter to be amusement. “In dreams? I am a fool there. A scarecrow in black robes dragged through someone else’s carnival.”

Losfy made a small offended noise inside him. “That is unfair.”

“Is it? There are demonesses, Masters, dragons, cat-girls, a blonde woman with a frankly shocking bust, a strange drink called ‘coffee’, flying journeys, strange glowing rectangles, and somehow I am always writing absurd reports about all of it.”

“But you wanted to study demons.”

“I wanted to understand possession. Curses. Souls that cling to the wrong body. Grief that turns into hunger.” He lifted his head slightly. Rain glittered along the edge of his jaw. “Not to discover that every demon in existence apparently wants to sleep with me.”

Losfy was quiet. When she answered, the flirtation in her voice had become fragile. “I… I thought… you would like being wanted.”

The words hurt him more than they should have. He closed his eyes beneath the wet cloth, though the gesture changed nothing. “That is the problem, Losfy.”

A cart passed at the far end of the street, wheels sinking into the mud. The man pulling it did not look at him. Perhaps he recognized the robes and thought it wiser not to trouble a [[Онмёдзи|servant of spirits]]. Perhaps he recognized sadness and knew it to be more dangerous than any spirit.

Losfer went on. “I do like it. Sometimes. In the dreams, when you laugh, when everyone reaches for you, when the world becomes bright and shameless because of your voice… I almost believe I belong there.”

“You do belong.”

“No. You do.” He rubbed his thumb slowly over the binding of his sword. “They are your friends. Not mine.”

“That is not true.”

“Who would want friendship with a tired man who cannot even explain why he is sad?” His voice dropped almost to a whisper. “They want you. The living one. The indecent one. The little nightmare who smiles and makes people feel brave enough to desire something. Without you, I am only the man behind the curtain. The one who apologizes for the show.”

Losfy’s voice came closer, as if she were leaning against the inside of his chest. “You wanted friends so badly.”

“I wanted someone to share this darkness with.”

“Is that not the same thing?”

He did not answer. Somewhere nearby, a wind chime rang once. The sound moved through the rain, thin and mournful, as if the chime itself regretted having been struck.

Losfer broke the silence. “They are too far from this world. Too bright. Too strange. I do not know them.”

“They are closer than you think.”

“I frighten them.”

“You do not know that.”

“I showed them too much.” His hand left the sword and curled against his own chest, where the fabric was dark with rain. “I thought I was being honest. I thought my darkness was ordinary because it lives inside me every day. Then I saw them turn still. Felt their voices become polite. That little distance people make when they do not wish to wound you, but have already stepped away.”

The rain thickened. Losfy said nothing. Perhaps even dreams did not know how to comfort the waking. Losfer spoke again. “I do not want them to know the real me.”

“And yet, in the last dream, you tied me up so that I would not interfere while you were speaking to them.”

His mouth tightened. “I immediately regretted it.”

“You were afraid I would scare them.”

“I was afraid I would.”

The footsteps came nearer before either of them noticed. A city guard stood at the edge of the awning, his straw cloak dripping steadily. He was not young, not old. His face bore the weathered calm of a man who had seen drunken merchants, dead children, runaway brides, and even oni. At his hip hung a plain sword. In one hand he held a paper lantern, its light trembling in the rain.

He bowed, not deeply, but with respect. 「<span lang="ja">お怪我はございませんか、陰陽師様。</span>」 <span class="astaria-dialogue-translation">(Are you hurt, honoured Onmyoji?)</span>

Losfer turned his face toward him. The guard could not see his eyes. That, too, was convenient. He answered aloud. 「<span lang="ja">怪我はある。だが、それは癒えることのない傷なのです。</span>」 <span class="astaria-dialogue-translation">(I am wounded. But it is a wound that will never heal.)</span>

The guard did not ask the foolish questions that kind men often ask when they are afraid of silence. He only stood there, rain ticking softly against his straw cloak. Then he sighed. It was not pity. Not quite. It was the sound of a man accepting that there were wounds which did not belong to his hands. He reached into the fold of his cloak and drew out a small rice ball wrapped in a leaf. Simple. Poorly shaped. Probably his own supper.

He placed it on the bench beside Losfer. Then he bowed again. Not to a hero. Not to a priest. Perhaps only to another man sitting in the rain. Without another word, he turned and walked away down the street.

Losfer stared at the onigiri. A ridiculous thing. A little lump of rice. A whole accusation. His throat tightened so suddenly he almost hated the guard for it.

Losfy’s voice returned within him. “He was kind.”

He answered silently, “Yes.”

“That hurt you.”

Losfer’s hand hovered over the leaf but did not touch it. “Yes.”

“Why?”

The question was so innocent that it became cruel. He bowed his head. “Because the world keeps giving me things.”

Rain drummed against the roof. “A home. Love. Food. Work. Art. A child’s voice. A woman’s hands. A friend’s message from a place I cannot reach. A stranger’s supper in the rain.” He let out a breath that broke halfway.

“And still I sit here like a man robbed of everything.”

Losfy’s voice trembled. “My love…”

“What kind of ingratitude is that?”

No answer came.

Losfer finally picked up the onigiri. It was still warm. That made it worse. The guard was already far down the street, his lantern reduced to a small trembling star between curtains of rain. Losfer murmured after the departing guard:

「<span lang="ja">ありがとう。</span>」 <span class="astaria-dialogue-translation">(Thank you.)</span>

The words did not reach him. Perhaps that was easier. He placed the rice back beside him and covered his face with one hand. For a while, Losfy allowed the silence to cover what the rain could not.

The city breathed around them. Water ran from gutters in silver cords. The mud accepted every footprint without judgment. Somewhere behind the paper walls, a woman laughed softly, then hushed herself, as if laughter too were improper beneath such a sky.

Losfy asked, “Do you want me to disappear?” The question was very small. Losfer’s hand fell from his face. He went still. “If I am the one who brings shame… if I frighten you… if I make your desires visible and your loneliness louder… do you want me gone?”

The rain seemed to pause, though it did not. Losfer’s fingers closed around the onigiri until the leaf wrinkled.

When he spoke, his voice was hoarse. “What would remain of me then?” Inside him, Losfy drew a silent breath.

He went on, “You think you are the demon. Because you wear horns. Because you whisper at night. Because you know the shape of every forbidden thought before I confess it.”

He looked toward the street, though he saw nothing but the inner dark behind cloth. “But you are the part of me that still reaches. Still laughs. Still desires. Still believes closeness can save something.”

His shoulders sank. “I need you to help me do my duty and hunt demons.”

Losfy’s voice became quiet. Not seductive now. Not playful. Only frightened. “For what?”

Losfer lifted his face toward the rain. For a moment, the water traced the black edge of the blindfold, slipped beneath it, and vanished where no one could follow.

“So that one day I can defeat the most terrible one.”

Losfy brightened at once, too quickly, desperately eager to be useful. Her fear turned into motion, into hope, into that stubborn little flame inside him which still refused to die.

“Then let us go. We need to find him, right? I will help you. I will give you my sight, and… and my dream-magic. My teeth, my horns, every wicked little trick I know. We have survived labyrinths, mirrors, queens, shadows, and worse. We will defeat him together, my beloved. We must.”

Her voice trembled, but she forced herself to sound brave. “Tell me his name, then. This frightening demon we have to defeat… what is he called?”

The paper lanterns flickered. The rain fell. Losfer slowly rose from the bench. Mud sucked at the soles of his boots as he stepped from beneath the awning. He tucked the guard’s onigiri into his robe with the care one might give a relic.

Then he stood in the open street, under the full weight of the rain. For a long time, he said nothing. At last, with his hand resting on the sword he had never drawn, he answered.

“Losfer.”

And after that, he walked on through the mud and the rain, while the city behind him closed its shutters one by one, as if even the world wished not to witness what the sky had so carefully hidden.
