# Smoke Thicket

![[Assets/Images/Smoky_Thicket.png]]

## Main text

The **Smoke Thicket** is a forest encircling the [[Призрачные горы|Ghost Mountains]] on [[Туманный остров|Mist Island]]. In the language of the [[Эдзо|Edzo]], it is called **Kasumi-no-mori** (霞の森, かすみのもり), the ‘forest of mist’.

The forest extends across the lands of [[Обитель|Obitelj]] and [[Амато|Amato]]. Its northern reaches are denser and wilder; the southern woodland within Amato belongs to the same forest. Mist lingers between the trees.

## Southern woodland

The south is covered by old, damp mixed woodland. Smoke pines grow beside large, spreading trees; moss covers trunks and fallen timber, and thick undergrowth gathers beneath parts of the canopy. Broad branches and fallen trunks offer routes above the ground while fog conceals the lower stretches of the paths.

Ravines and streams cut through the forest in places. Roots are exposed above the water, and rocky ledges emerge among the trees. Paths wind around slopes and converge at convenient crossings; those who know the alternative routes can approach such a place unnoticed from another direction.

## Northern and western forests

Dense coniferous woodland predominates in the north and west. Closely packed spruce stands and mossy lowlands cover the lower ground; beneath the forest canopy lie hidden valleys crossed by old animal trails.

At the foot of the Ghost Mountains, forest paths climb towards slopes and passes. Higher up, trees give way to rocky ridges, patches of snow and exposed, windswept heights.

## Inhabitants

[[Менада|Maenads]] inhabit the southern thicket. When hunting, they use branches, fallen trees and ledges to approach their prey unseen and choose a place for a short leap. Ravines and narrow crossings provide opportunities for ambush.

Their communities build simple platforms and shelters of branches in the forks of large trees. Here maenads rest above the ground and watch the forest from higher up.

Maenads communicate through vocal signals, postures and gestures; they do not understand Edzo speech. In rare cases, a human may gradually teach a maenad to communicate through agreed gestures or drawings.

Maenads are cautious, rather lazy and sometimes even timid. They fear open confrontation with humans and try to avoid fighting strong groups. For people, the southern forests are considerably less dangerous than the northern werewolf territories.

Maenads defend their communities and young, as well as the hunting grounds entered by loggers and hunters. They may regard a lone, wounded or straggling person as easy prey and attack from ambush.

Packs of [[Вервольф|werewolves]] inhabit the northern and western forests and the Ghost Mountains: large, exceptionally intelligent predators capable of coordinated attacks on humans. They lived here before human settlements appeared; the expansion of human activity drives them deeper into the forest and higher into the mountains. Hunger, cold and the loss of hunting grounds make the packs increasingly hostile towards people.

Werewolf howls can be heard even along the coast of Mist Island. The danger of encountering a pack existed long before the recent unsettling rumours from the forest.

## Edzo woodland trades

For the Edzo, the southern thicket is a wild and dangerous forest. They enter it chiefly to cut timber and hunt. Building timber is needed for houses, boats and new shrines to [[Ицунэ|Itsune]]; game supplements food from the rice fields, while pelts are sold. Herbs and berries are plentiful in the [[Луга Тамано|Tamano Meadows]], where gathering them is more familiar and safer.

Most loggers and hunters are townspeople. Their visits to the forest are brief. Loggers work by day and try to return home by nightfall. An armed escort is useful when entering the woods.

Scattered temporary cabins used by woodsmen and hunters stand in the southern thicket. They provide shelter for a midday meal or an unavoidable overnight stay.

Timber is brought to landings on [[Озеро Шинда|Lake Shinda]] and the [[Река Кумокава|Kumokawa River]]. It is needed in [[Город Касумито|Kasumito]] and other southern island settlements, including [[Город Рэйкацу|Reikatsu]] and [[Город Кири|Kiri]]. Onward deliveries to these coastal towns travel by cart or sea.

## Disturbing news

For many centuries, Mist Island was regarded as the safest island in Amato where possession was concerned. Now reports of disappearances and the appearance of oni are arriving from the forests and mountains.

Loggers found a mortally wounded [[Онмёдзи|Onmyoji]] who, before dying, told them of an ambush by companions of an [[Они|oni]] he had been pursuing. The loggers brought the hunter's body to the temple in Kasumito. This news gave local people reason to suspect that the possessed had begun acting together.

Following these alarming accounts, timber harvesting has slowed: few now dare to enter the forest alone. The daimyo of Kasumito and the local temple are asking the capital and other temples for assistance. For the ruler, the forest's wealth is the chief economic advantage of his domain, so removing the threat matters both to him and to the priests.

Rumours circulate in the southern settlements about **[[Нурари-химэ|Nurari-hime]]** (ぬらり姫, ぬらりひめ), a young ‘Queen of Demons’ supposedly glimpsed from afar among oni. Her identity is unknown.

One account is repeated especially often: a hunter managed to escape from the possessed. According to him, his pursuers abandoned their chase after a girl appeared.

Not everyone believes these stories. Earlier tales of bloodthirsty demons from the forests and mountains often arose from encounters with wolves, maenads or werewolves; sometimes the unsettling sounds were merely the wind. Some residents are frightened by the new rumours, while others consider them another woodland tall tale.
