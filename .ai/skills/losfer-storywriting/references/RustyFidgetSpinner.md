"We were going to use Imaren's Light to end the war, but not like this!"

Kaeda sneered derisively, "Did you honestly believe wishing upon a fallen star would end a war that's burned for a millennia?"

Imaren's Light was, in truth, a star plucked from the sky, but Rand didn't answer.

"Your naïveté was charming three months ago."

Kaeda held her hand out to him, her eyes fixated on the pouch around his neck.

"Give it over, Rand. I won't ask twice."

Rand grasped hold of the pouch and closed his eyes. It only contained a few useless pebbles, after all. He hardly trusted himself with the relic and certainly not these people. Days ago, he trusted a friend with it and now he prayed she would never be found.

He drew a deep breath in and tried to calm himself. Nestled behind his eyelids was a visage, a memory, a fleeting moment of youthful, innocent bliss. The woman he loved — her hazel eyes, her mousy brown hair. They tumbled together into a pile of leaves, giggling in delight, and they held each other there as autumn rain began to fall. It was frigidly cold and she was warm in his arms. Life was so much simpler then.

Rand knew before stepping within this place that the relic should not fall into the hands of his former cadre, but now the truth was laid bare. The choice was clear.

"I won't let you do this."

"I will pry the relic from your dead hands if I must, lover." she paused and cocked her head, "But you didn't bring it here, did you? Do you take me for a fool? Do you think I won't find it?"

Their exchange began to catch the attention of the others. A few crept closer, curious and wary.

"It isn't too late, Kaeda. Please." Rand begged, "There has to be another way!"

"There is no other recourse, not for the Empire and not for a traitor!" Kaeda snarled. She gestured and several men drew their blades. Rand held his ground with his arms out, desperate to change the course of the situation.

"Need I remind you of what the Empire took from all of us?"

Kaeda clenched her fists as the fury in her boiled out. "Your fiancée! My daughters, Rand! My DAUGHTERS!!! The families of everyone here! The Empire stole away everything we ever loved! EVERYTHING! And now! Now, of all times, you betray us?!"

Emotions were building too fast and there was nothing Rand could say or do to stop it. They closed upon him, weapons drawn.

"Sarna..." he said aloud the name of his fiancée, uttering it like a sacred word. A strange and serene calm overtook him. There was no turning back, there was nowhere to run, there was no other choice.

"Shirak." he intoned a simple cantrip. It was peasant magic, the sort of spell used to light a candle at night. In the dark of his mind, he saw his lost love's face bathed in warm amber light, he saw her hazel eyes wild with delight because he used a trick to light their evening. She had such wonder; to her, the world was magical and innocent.

She threw her arms around his neck.

"Show me how you do it and I promise you... I promise that I'll run away with you."

Spinning on that dizzy edge, he kissed her face and kissed her head. He dreamed of all the different ways he had to make her glow.

"Why are you so far away?" she said.

"Why won't you ever know that I'm in love with you? I'm in love with you!"

Rand flicked a hand and a bright spark danced from his fingertips and glittered through the air, bouncing once, twice, and a third time towards the barrels of gunpowder.

"Sorry, Kaeda." he murmured dreamily, "I am finally going home."

Kaeda's eyes widened in horror. She whipped her hands about to pull threads of ley into a defensive spell. It was almost too late for her.

Black on white, their silhouettes stood for the blink of an eye in the wake of a spectacular explosion. In an instant, everything and everyone within the warehouse was pulverized and reduced to ash. Nearby buildings buckled and every window shattered. The thunderous shockwave was heard for miles around.

That night, despite Rand's sacrifice, Dubhaven caught fire.
