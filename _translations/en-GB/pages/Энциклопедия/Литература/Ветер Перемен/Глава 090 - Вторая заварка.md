# Chapter 090 — The Second Infusion

*[[Сурадж Ка Гхар|Suraj Ka Ghar]], 106 NE, summer*

The travellers reached the gates of [[Город Ванпур|Vanpur]] towards dawn. The city greeted them with high walls, open gates and guards as courteous as suspicion permits. After a brief conversation, [[Гилья Кангарлавехотри|Gilja]] learnt where they might eat and rest. The finest choice was a central tavern called the Second Infusion.

The landlady, Nadila Tiwari, and her daughter Tanushri found the guests the last available room. The travellers slept until midday and woke hungry. Nadila tried to keep them upstairs: conflict was brewing in the common room between local priests and the [[Тхаги|Thuggee]], and foreigners would be wiser not to interfere. Urist's hunger and Gilja's powers of persuasion prevailed.

Two bald priests were arguing with two Thuggee. The priests seethed while the Thuggee answered with dry composure. One approached [[Томас Робинсон|Tom]] and spoke in flawless Lunaar. Tom later relayed the message: the Thuggee asked him to tell [[Мерката|Mercate]] that their task was complete and they would await her at the next full moon.

Listening to the dispute, Gilja realised that the priests had lost a relic. The townspeople were suffering as a result, and the priests demanded aid. The Thuggee refused to intervene.

Before they left, Urist tried to sell the Thuggee some crystallised dragon blood. They declined, but advised him to travel to [[Город Награкшаса|Nagrakshasa]], where such a rarity would command a better price.

The priests then approached Gilja. They complained of the city's misfortunes since the relic vanished, and Gilja promised to visit the local hospital. A Raksha named Samir Thakur directed its affairs, and Gilja already had questions for him.

After lunch, the friends went to the market. Gilja warned his companions to be watchful, but Vanpur immediately demonstrated its own rules: [[Чори Марджари|Chori Marjari]] cut three of Mercate's feathers from Tom and disappeared.

Urist remembered his lost tiger skin and decided to buy another, fashion a new cloak and tell everyone it was the original. The seller was a fifteen-year-old boy proud to represent the fourth generation of his family in the trade. A mercantile battle over every coin unfolded between him and Urist.

Urist ultimately prevailed. The cloak barely touched the common purse and would be ready in a few days. Vanpur received the travellers not with battle, but with commerce, arguments and stolen feathers. Sometimes that is precisely how a city bares its teeth.
