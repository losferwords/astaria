# Chapter 005 — The Red River

*Gilas, 103 NE, summer*

Leaving [[Город Мистрас|Mystras]], the travellers crossed [[Регион Элессия|Elessia's]] fields towards the [[Река Тавропос|Tavropos]]. Central [[Гилас|Gilas]] seemed endless: dry golden grass, heavy summer air, farm roads and sparse belts of woodland where any undocumented traveller might become a guard's quarry.

The road led into a forest, where a boar-man and a whole sounder attacked. The battle was ugly and bloody. [[Урист Китин|Urist]] held the charge through strength, [[Томас Робинсон|Thomas]] searched for openings to shoot, and [[Хонсу-Намун|Khonsu]] and [[Мэй Ву|Helena]] did what they could amid broken branches, hooves and screaming animals. Victory came through bruises, blood and exhaustion rather than glory.

Thomas's woodland knowledge then led them to the river, where they found a boat. The Tavropos, flowing from [[Озеро Митра|Lake Mitra]] to [[Город Аргос|Argos]], offered a faster, quieter road than land.

Near Argos, three [[Гарпия|harpies]] fell upon them from the sky. Helena's knife struck one wing; Thomas fired between beating feathers and his companions' bodies. A harpy seized Urist by the head and tried to lift him. Thomas's arrow forced it to release him, but he plunged into the river with a terrible wound across his face.

Khonsu helped drag him back while the others called to the city walls. Arrows finally drove the creatures away. Urist lost consciousness, fever came quickly, and the fugitives needed a doctor in a capital where they might be arrested at any moment.

They found a peculiar physician called Philoctetes in a market tent. He was truly [[Данис ибн Синай|Danis ibn Sinai]], a Qadian scholar living under another name. He had been studying tiny creatures upon glass and solemnly calling them ‘little cows’, yet put aside his observations, cleaned Urist's wound and revived him.

Fever returned Urist to consciousness in confusion. Insisting he needed his own boat, he ran into the city, struck a boatman and drew the guards' attention. His companions had to save him from the consequences of his delirious obstinacy.

At last they hid in an inn called the Red River, where they ate and considered Ramat's letter. Nikandros, the fruit merchant meant to open their road to a ship, still lay ahead. Argos had already made one truth clear: help in Gilas's capital could cost far more than it first appeared.
