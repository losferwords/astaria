# Inhabited scenes: groups with shared activities

## User preference and scope

On 2026-10-21 the user requested a reusable rule for settlements and other scenes containing many inhabitants: replace scattered generic idle figures with small groups engaged in household work, walking, conversation and other visible everyday activities. This followed stiff residents in the Trella and Badrakali results and an incorrectly depicted ox-cart connection.

Apply this where human activity belongs in the requested scene and is readable at the chosen scale. Do not turn a landscape into a crowd scene or make villagers foreground character portraits. Grouping is an artistic planning method, not a guaranteed ChatGPT Images control.

## Build a few readable activity groups

Use the pattern **place + participants + shared action + visible interaction or support**. A few representative groups are usually more useful than individual instructions for every resident; adjust their number to the composition rather than enforcing a fixed count.

- Anchor groups to places that explain their actions: water carriers at a well, porters between a cart and storehouse, buyers at a stall, neighbours on a veranda. Keep walking routes and working space open.
- Use concrete actions instead of only `busy`, `lively`, `dynamic` or `no idle poses`. Specify who offers, receives, carries, pulls, reaches, kneels, bends or turns toward whom or what.
- Make an interaction shared rather than a list of unrelated poses: a seller scoops grain into a buyer's bowl; two workers carry one crate together; seated travellers turn toward each other while talking.
- Choose one readable instant for each group. Do not make the same person perform several sequential actions at once, and do not import video timing, sound or animation instructions into a still prompt.
- Suggest plausible weight and contact through selected cues: a loaded shoulder, a bent knee on a stair, a hand steadying a jar, a torso leaning toward a work surface. Avoid exhaustive limb choreography at panoramic scale.
- Vary orientation, spacing and rhythm. People attend to companions, tools and routes rather than all facing the viewer. Groups can overlap naturally, but avoid cloned poses, parade rows and equal gaps between isolated upright figures.
- Mix working, walking and restful social groups. A person seated sideways at a shared meal or resting against a post can feel alive; do not require everyone to run, gesture dramatically or look excited.

## Match the viewing distance

At a moderate village panorama, describe a few shared tasks and characteristic silhouettes. Faces and fine hand anatomy remain secondary. In a high aerial view, use broad signals such as moving pairs, shared loads, clustered stalls and seating arrangements; distant residents may remain simple unresolved traces. Do not demand close-up anatomical clarity while also making people tiny.

Keep enough quiet space to read the environment. Sparse inhabitants can still feel occupied if their relationships are clear; adding more people is not the automatic remedy for lifeless staging.

Small is a scale requirement, not an instruction to flatten people into cutouts. Following the author's 2026-10-26 Amanohama feedback, readable figures should share the scenery's dimensional form shading, reflected colors and contact shadows. Heads, sleeves and clothing can read as rounded forms without detailed faces or enlarged foreground characters. Truly distant figures may stay unresolved; avoid forcing close-up anatomy into a panorama or imposing a uniform black outline and two-tone fill on every person.

## People, animals and connected props

Avoid relying on a noun alone when physical relationships matter. A cart, its load, draught animals and a handler must share coherent direction, scale and attachment. If a moving team matters, state simply that the animals are ahead of the cart, face the travel direction and pull through a coherent yoke and drawpole arrangement.

If complex harnessing is incidental and has already failed, simplify the staged activity rather than repeatedly adding mechanical detail: a parked unhitched cart can be unloaded while animals rest separately by the stable. Explain that adjustment in the Russian introduction when revising the user's scene; do not silently discard an explicitly important requested action.

Likewise, distinguish carried game from a living animal through its supported horizontal position, rather than merely putting hunters beside a deer. Inspect the resulting contact, weight and orientation instead of assuming that the prompt guarantees them.
