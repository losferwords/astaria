# Lang-An

## Main text

Lang-An is the God of Fire and central deity of the [[Джу|Dju]], founder of [[Империя Ланг-Ан|the Empire of Fire]] and its eternal dragon-emperor. He is portrayed as a long, red-scaled serpent with four clawed legs, dorsal spines, whiskers and fangs. In temple art he is almost invariably wreathed in flame, yet this fire signifies more than destruction: it warms the home, tempers metal and transforms raw matter into finished form.

Legend holds that the Great Dragon saved the ancestors of the Dju from destruction, leading them out of the western deserts of [[Архея|Archaea]] and into the fertile valley of the [[Река Тяо Хэ|Tiao He]]. The new state was to renounce slavery, endless war and the arbitrary cruelties of the old world. That covenant became the foundation of imperial law and of the Dju understanding of harmony: strength is permissible only when its bearer can contain it within the bounds of duty.

Tradition describes Lang-An as ardent and impetuous. The first empress was a mortal woman who mastered his temper not with a weapon, but with reason. Ever since, the Dragon has ruled through chosen wives: the god lends power to the throne, while the empress must remind him of the cost of a hasty judgement. To the Dju, their union is a sacred image of balance between flame and vessel, authority and restraint.

Most inhabitants of the empire have never seen their patron. They recognise his presence in the heat of temple braziers, in the empress's dreams and in wonders attributed to the Dragon's will. Any long silence from the god unsettles the country: courtiers begin to argue, provinces test the strength of old obligations and common people wonder where the promised harmony has been broken.

Lang-An's chief dwelling is the [[Храм Дракона|Temple of the Dragon]] in the capital, [[Город Джу-Суо|Dju-Suo]]. His repose is guarded by the [[Аватар|Avatars]], chosen warriors able to channel a portion of divine power. The people call them the emperor's living sparks. They are charged with defending the weak and preserving order, yet, like every flame, remain dangerous whenever will proves weaker than the gift.
