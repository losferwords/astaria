## Realm of smiths and wanderers

Wildlands are the young realm of the [[Авгарцы|Avgars]], stretching along the rivers, steppes and mountain passes of central [[Архея|Archaea]]. Foreigners see a country of stern nomads and smiths, but the land is far more varied than its own name suggests. Wandering arbats, mining settlements, crowded ports and cities grown around the roads that once supplied the [[Великая Кузня|Great Forge]] all stand side by side. Most travellers follow these roads on foot. Horses are valued companions on a long journey, but Avgar life does not revolve around the saddle.

## The march that became a homeland

In 678 ChE, the young [[Вактары|Vaktar]] [[Аксель Хан|Axel Khan]] travelled south in search of glory and new lands. His company encountered [[Хангор|Khangor]] and, mistaking the Iron God for a monster, attacked him without hesitation. Khangor admired their courage and offered strength, knowledge of craft and a new destiny in exchange for help building the Great Forge. Thus an expedition slowly became a migration, and the northerners, together with the people of Archaea who joined them, became a new nation.

Khangor's first followers called themselves [[Мектиг|Mektigs]]—the Mighty. The Forge demanded immeasurable quantities of ore, stone and timber, and the nascent realm took them through conquest. Campaigns along the [[Горный хребет Шафар|Shafar Range]] led to the [[Раскол Кадира|Schism of Qadir]] and centuries of enmity with [[Империя Ланг-Ан|the Lang-An Empire]]. Their neighbours named the Mektigs Barbarians; they themselves regard the name as a foreign insult.

## Clans, arbats and personal responsibility

An Avgar family is rarely confined to a single home. Kin may live across several arbats and cities, yet remain bound by mutual defence, shared debts and a common reputation. On the steppe, camps move with the herds and seasonal work; in the [[Хангорская долина|Hangor Valley]], permanent forges, markets and river landings have grown. Even a settled craftsperson keeps a travelling cauldron, sound boots and a weapon as though the household might depart again tomorrow.

Order rests upon the principle of personal responsibility. An Avgar may act according to their own will, but must answer to the injured person, their family and their own clan. A wrong may be repaid in silver, labour, public reconciliation or a duel when no other accord can be reached. The ability to speak and bargain is therefore valued no less than strength; anyone unable to explain their own deed soon finds themselves without support.

Craft is treated as proof of maturity. A respected warrior can repair their equipment, make camp and distribute a burden on a long march, while a good ruler must understand the cost of metal and labour. Cities quarrel over which lineage forges the finest blades, yet despise a maker who conceals a dangerous flaw for profit.

## After Khangor's silence

In 30 NE, work upon the Great Forge was completed, and Khangor sealed himself inside his creation. A country that had lived for centuries in service to one task was left without a new command. Some clans demanded further conquests; others turned to trade and the cultivation of the lands already taken.

The present Great Khan, [[Батар Саг|Batar Sag]], is attempting to turn a military confederacy of clans into an enduring state. Under his rule, writing, agriculture and peaceful trade have developed, and for the first time in generations the army marched to aid a former enemy, Lang-An. Old warriors call such policy weakness. To the younger generation, it is a chance to earn renown not by destruction, but through what remains afterwards.

Wildlands greet a traveller with candour and austere hospitality. Empty titles carry little weight; respect goes to one who keeps their word, works with their hands and does not shelter behind another's back. A mistake may be forgiven. Refusing responsibility for it almost never is.
