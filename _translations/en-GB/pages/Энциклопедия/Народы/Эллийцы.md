# Ellians

![[Assets/Images/Ellians.jpg]]

## Main text

The **Ellians** are a proud and energetic people of western [[Архея|Archaea]] who form the principal population of [[Гилас|Gilas]]. Their ancestors were [[Хтониды|Chthonids]] who settled [[Регион Элессия|Elessia]] approximately a thousand years ago. Life far from the island metropolis, constant frontier wars and laws belonging to their own cities gradually made the settlers a new people.

After the [[Падение Хтона|Fall of Chthon]], the Ellians finally separated themselves from their island ancestors and built an independent state beneath [[Гиперион I|Hyperion I]]. Their kinship with the fallen civilisation remains an uncomfortable subject. Ellians are proud of the antiquity of their cities, but prefer to regard tenacity, martial honour and political independence as achievements entirely their own.

Ellian culture prizes composure, physical discipline and responsibility for one's word. Many know how to wield a spear and what to do during a siege, yet valour is not confined to war. An artist, physician, philosopher or architect may bring a city no less glory than a victor of the arena. In any contest, respect belongs not only to the winner, but to one who obeys the rules, refuses to humiliate the weak and keeps their dignity in defeat.

The cities of Gilas live by strict codes, but each preserves customs, festivals and rivalries of its own. Public squares host arguments about campaigns and philosophy; workshops paint pottery and walls; news is debated in the public baths; veterans and farmers strike bargains in the markets. Ellian hospitality demands that a guest be fed and allowed their say, even when heated argument around the table is inevitable. The arenas serve at once as festival grounds, instruments of punishment and the path to the title of [[Идеал|Paragon]]. This cruel part of society belongs to its contradictory understanding of freedom: the citizen's right to take part in the destiny of their city, rather than a gift equally available to all.

Ellians regard magic with suspicion. The memory of the Fall of Chthon has made prophecy and uncontrolled power almost synonymous with a future catastrophe. They may respect a formidable enemy or a foreign Imithei, but such a person must earn trust through deeds rather than miracles.
