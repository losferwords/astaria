# Chapter 004 — Helena's Flame

*Gilas, 103 NE, summer*

Every approach to [[Город Мистрас|Mystras]] was watched. After the escape from Achaeus and alarm in Phibas, guards might remember the travellers' faces. [[Мэй Ву|Helena]] therefore chose a path requiring neither trust nor an open road: they entered through a sewer.

The passage was foul and exhausting. Water sucked beneath their feet and stone walls returned every sound as though Mystras itself were listening. [[Урист Китин|Urist]], [[Хонсу-Намун|Khonsu]] and Helena climbed into a small tower, where Urist broke a lock loudly enough to attract two guards. Helena's voice, expression and confidence formed so natural a deception that the guards saw an ordinary civic mishap rather than fugitives. The three vanished into the crowd.

At night they reached the refuge of [[Рамат-Хат-Су|Ramat-Hat-Su]], an officer of [[Жаждущие|the Thirsty]] for whom freeing slaves was dangerous daily work. He offered passage out of Gilas in exchange for delivering a letter in [[Город Аргос|Argos]] to fruit merchant [[Никандр Иеропонтид|Nikandros Hieropontid]]. Nikandros would secure berths to [[Город Уасет|Waset]] in [[Амон-Астат|Amon-Astat]]. The road had lengthened, but finally possessed a clear end.

Morning brought an unexpected meeting. They found [[Томас Робинсон|Thomas]], thought lost in Elessia, in the hands of wealthy aristocrat [[Вилас Контос|Vilas Kontos]], an arena master who now employed the hunter as a bodyguard—another kind of cage.

Their secret attempt to free him quickly ceased to be secret. Guards spotted them, confusion filled the estate, and Vilas ran for the city gates to summon aid. Helena caught him first.

Her anger ignited faster than words. Before her stood a man for whom another's bondage formed part of a convenient order. Fire engulfed her hands and leapt to his clothes when she seized him. The guards recoiled. This was no longer a slave escape or household fight, but something beyond Gilas's familiar rules.

Urist, Khonsu and Thomas watched her with alarm. The woman who had saved them through cunning in Phibas had shown a wholly different power. Mystras fell behind, but beside Ramat's letter they carried a new question: who was the young woman calling herself Helena?
