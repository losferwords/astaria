## Main text

Obitelj is the country of the [[Венды|Wends]] in eastern [[Кельн|Keln]], a land of grain plains, birch groves, coniferous forests and shallow rivers. Foreigners sometimes mistake its name for a promise of undisturbed refuge. To the Wends, it means not a place where misfortune never comes, but a home people preserve for one another despite frost, hunger and war.

The ancestors of the Wends came from [[Вактар-Йорден|Vaktar-Jorden]]. They brought runes, heavy clothing, songs beside the fire and an austere respect for endurance, yet a different way of life gradually took shape upon their new soil. Under the protection of [[Велисса|Velissa]], the highest virtue became not glory won, but life preserved: a household raised again after famine, a family reconciled, a traveller brought safely home from a winter road.

## From hatred to love

The first tales of a ‘beautiful maiden’ spread among Vaktar settlers near the [[Девичьи Холмы|Maiden Hills]], which took their name from these encounters. Hunters spoke of a stranger who offered care, a smile and a blessing upon a long marriage. Yet upon returning home, they forgot their own wives and children, then left again to search for her.

Fearful for their families and filled with anger, the Vaktar women led the settlements farther east towards [[Сребролесье|Silverwood]]. The encounters continued there. Hunters and woodcutters came home consumed by longing for the maiden in the forest. The strange calamity was accompanied by a wonder: the more people fell beneath her influence, the richer the soil became, the fuller the harvests and the healthier the children.

Several women whose husbands had abandoned them chose revenge over another flight. They found Velissa beside the [[Река Красивая|Krasivaya River]], at the place where [[Город Мирград|Mirgrad]] would later stand. Like their husbands, they fell in love with the goddess at first sight, but still found the strength to speak. Velissa was horrified by the grief she had caused. She had never wished to break families and had hidden from mortals for millennia because she knew that her beauty stole their will.

In 1236 ChE, the encounter ended not in vengeance but in the [[Основание Обители|foundation of Obitelj]]. The women accepted her apology and became the first Beregini: bearers of her will and a living veil between the goddess and mortals. They vowed to protect people from chance encounters with Velissa while carrying her blessings, gifts and remembrance of the lives she had unwittingly broken. Velissa in turn took the settlers under her protection. From their compact grew Mirgrad, the Wendish people, and a society in which love became not merely a virtue, but a power.

## Land and livelihood

Obitelj's brief summer must feed it for the entire year. Grain and flax grow upon the plains, livestock graze in the valleys, and forest settlements live by hunting, beekeeping and gathering mushrooms, berries and medicinal herbs. Stone and ore come from the northern foothills. Timber, honey, cloth, pottery and sacks of the new harvest travel down the [[Река Красивая|Krasivaya River]] and other waterways to the capital.

Winter cuts many roads for months, and so each village maintains common stores of grain, salt and dry wood. Opening such a store without the elders' consent is a grave offence—unless another household faces hunger. In that case, refusing to share brings greater shame than breaking the seal. This simple custom has saved entire districts after early snow or flooding.

[[Веданский лес|Vedan Forest]] and [[Сребролесье|Silverwood]] provide timber and game, but the Wends do not regard the wild as an ownerless storehouse. Old trees are marked with protective runes, saplings are planted beside a felling, and any great hunt begins by seeking leave from the keepers of the groves and the priestesses of Velissa. Iron, coloured stone and translucent crystals are mined in the [[Горный хребет Северный Венец|Northern Crown]], the latter revered as an earthly sign of the goddess.

## Mirgrad and the crown

Obitelj's capital, [[Город Мирград|Mirgrad]], stands upon the Krasivaya and joins river trade to the roads of the farming regions. The fortified royal court occupies the high bank, with quays, forges, weaving rows and markets below. Most of the city is built of timber, and its broad squares serve both as festival grounds and as barriers against fire.

The king or queen negotiates with neighbouring realms, musters the levy and answers for the common stores. Daily matters fall to town and village elders, master artisans and the Beregini—priestesses of Velissa who mediate family quarrels and disputes over land. Such government is seldom swift. The Wends prefer a decision with which a community can live through a long winter to an elegant decree forgotten by spring.

After the fall of [[Ивар Свирепый|Ivar the Fierce]], the throne passed to [[Мафка|Mavka]], an unknown girl who appeared about seven years old and was named Velissa's chosen. A council of elders, Beregini and [[Страж|Guardians]] formed around her. They turn the young queen's simple wishes—to feed the hungry, bring people home and reconcile neighbours—into laws, supplies and treaties.

## Velissa and the turning year

Velissa is revered as Goddess of Earth, Love and Beauty. Her temples are modest and often surrounded by gardens, springs or fields. The first grain, ribbons, flowers and objects made for a loved one are left in her honour, yet the finest offering is care given without hope of reward.

The spring thaw is the country's greatest festival. Doors are opened to the sun, wreaths set upon the rivers, winter-worn possessions mended in the squares, and the first fresh bread divided among neighbours. In autumn, families remember those who did not return to the hearth; in winter, they gather for long evenings of song and story. The year thus reminds the Wends that love consists not only of the joy of meeting, but also of the labour of waiting.

## Love as a power

In Obitelj, love is not merely symbolic: it is one of the active forces of the world. Wendish runes can heighten mutual desire, warm fading tenderness or help spouses hear one another. They are embroidered upon wedding belts, carved into the headboards of marriage beds and written upon ribbons exchanged privately by lovers. Such magic feeds upon freely shared feeling. It cannot create love from nothing, and an attempt to overrule another person's will often turns the rite into a painful obsession.

Sexuality is regarded as a life-giving gift of Velissa rather than a source of shame. Virginity can also sustain magic when it is kept as a freely chosen vow: it represents undivided longing and a choice not yet made. Neither marital intimacy nor a virgin vow makes anyone purer or more worthy than another; only fidelity to one's own word gives either path power.

Marriage vows are therefore sometimes bound with runes. They strengthen fidelity, but punish deliberate betrayal with equal force. A faithless spouse may find the warmth leave the house, suffer another person's dreams and longing, or taste bitterness whenever they speak a lie. The curse can be lifted through an honest confession, reconciliation or a truthful dissolution of the union. Not every couple binds a marriage with magic, and the Beregini warn that no rune can replace trust.

## Ivar's war and the Guardians

For many years Velissa lay in an unfathomable sleep whose cause remains unknown. In her absence, Ivar the Fierce turned Obitelj's powerful army into an instrument of personal glory. He marched against Vaktar-Jorden and sought to conquer [[Туманный остров|Mist Island]], where the Wends lived beside the [[Эдзо|Edzo]] of [[Амато|Amato]]. The war of 104 NE took countless lives and divided Obitelj itself: some feared to disobey their king, while others could no longer name his campaign a defence of the homeland.

Upon awakening, Velissa chose the first Guardians from among those who opposed the tyranny. She granted them the strength of stone and kinship with the land, but charged them to defend rather than conquer. After months of fighting, the rebels drove Ivar from Mirgrad and ended the war. Victory did not heal the country at once. Burnt villages, orphaned families, refugees and distrust of the crown remained.

Today the Guardians protect the roads and help rebuild what was destroyed, while Obitelj returns prisoners, searches for the missing and reopens old trade routes. Peace with Amato remains fragile because of Mist Island, yet the bond with Vaktar-Jorden slowly strengthens through the affection between Mavka and Queen [[Сигрид Дракендоттир|Sigrid Drakendottir]]. The Wends know well that ending a battle is easier than learning to await a guest without fear. That is what the whole country is now attempting—slowly, stubbornly and together.
