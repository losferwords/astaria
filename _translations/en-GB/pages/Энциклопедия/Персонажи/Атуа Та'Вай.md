# Atua Ta'Wai

![[Assets/Images/Atua Ta'Wai.jpg]]

## Main text

**Atua Ta'Wai** is the present chief of [[Хамоа|Hamoa]] and the First [[Хранитель|Warden]], eldest among the seven chosen servants of [[Икатерра|Ikaterra]]. He speaks quietly, seldom raises his voice and possesses the rare gift of making those in dispute listen not only to him, but to one another.

Atua became an [[Имитей|Imithei]] late in life, at the age of forty-six. By then, he was already an accomplished helmsman, healer and father of a large family. Those years taught him to understand Ikaterra's power not as dominion over water, but as a duty to preserve life. He had earned the respect of his people long before receiving the goddess's gift, and several years later was chosen as their chief.

His new station required Atua to commune more often with the Sea Goddess and to make decisions affecting every village. In time, Ikaterra raised him to the rank of First Warden. He does not regard the title as a reward: the eldest Hiku is the first to answer for another Warden's mistake and the last to leave the site of a disaster.

Atua has a devoted wife, eleven children and four grandchildren. His home in [[Деревня Хамоа Хо|Hamoa Ho]] is almost never quiet. Kin, pupils, petitioners and passing travellers gather at the same table, while important counsel must often be conducted amid children's laughter and the sound of a fishing net being mended.

As a ruler, Atua prefers patient conversation to command. He journeys between the villages, listens to fishers and healers, then brings the matter before the common council. This deliberation frustrates those awaiting an immediate answer. Yet the people know that once the First Warden orders them to leave the shore or prepare its defence, the time for doubt has already passed.

In recent years, Atua has been especially troubled by Ikaterra's silence, the changes around [[Вулкан Ротонуи|Rotonui]] and the dangers beyond the [[Перешеек Мост Хранителей|Wardens' Bridge]]. He strives not to burden his people with his own fear and remains, to the Manai, the image of a tranquillity that does not deny the storm, but helps everyone face it together.
