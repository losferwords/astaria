# Chapter 001 — The Key to the Cage

*Gilas, 103 NE, spring*

In [[Город Ахей|Achaeus]], a border city of [[Гилас|Gilas]] beside [[Озеро Митра|Lake Mitra]], strangers received little kindness. Here, where [[Эллийцы|Ellian]] valour was measured by endurance in the arena and every martial yard preserved the memory of the [[Идеал|Ideals]], three people from distant corners of [[Астария|Astaria]] found themselves behind the same bars: [[Урист Китин|Urist Kitin]], [[Томас Робинсон|Thomas Robinson]] and [[Хонсу-Намун|Khonsu-Namun]].

The cell was cramped, with one wooden bunk for three and air steeped in sweat, iron and arena dust. Urist, an Avgar from Wildlands, held himself with the heavy stubbornness of a man accustomed to arguing with the world through his shoulders. Thomas, a fugitive hunter from Lunaar, was quieter; his gaze searched the corners as though hoping for a forest path where there was only stone and iron. Khonsu, an archivist's pupil from Tiza, seemed most out of place: a man of books and ancient maps among people destined to be tested by weapons.

A fourth prisoner sat with them, a silent youth little more than a boy. He sought no conversation and looked as if he had already decided to belong neither to the cage nor to those beside him. His silence was not peace, but the taut stillness before a blow.

Their keeper was [[Диомед Ахейский|Diomedes the Achaean]], the elderly curator of the city's gladiators. Once an [[Идеал|Ideal]] of [[Гиперион I|Hyperion I]], he now trained those whom Achaeus threw onto the sand. He possessed neither an overseer's cheap cruelty nor a gaoler's indifference: he honoured fair combat, pitied his fighters in his own fashion and had not entirely forgotten how to see prisoners as people.

Diomedes asked what each could do—not like a trader pricing living goods, but like an old warrior who could already hear where a life might end tomorrow. Urist offered a smith's strength, Thomas a hunter's eye, Khonsu cautious learning, and the youth only stubborn silence. The following day, Diomedes told them, they would fight.

Morning light struck their eyes as they entered the arena. Each was allowed a weapon. Urist chose a one-handed hammer, close to his craft. Thomas took an old bow, poor but familiar. Khonsu selected a long staff, more support and means of survival than victor's weapon. The silent youth refused everything.

When the fighting began, the youth rushed first into its heart. Despair drove him more than judgement. A terrible wound felled him almost at once, his death the first blow against those who had hoped the arena might grant them time to learn its rules.

Then all became shouting, sand, hard breath and brief flashes of pain. Urist seized one opponent so fiercely that the man lost consciousness in his grip. Thomas stepped back and loosed a dreadful, precise shot: his arrow found a young archer's head before she could decide the battle herself. Khonsu endured as best he could, but the arena spared no unwilling scholar. A grave wound to his thigh nearly brought him down.

This was no glorious duel from a song, nor ritual [[Танатомахия|Thanatomachy]] deciding the fate of a future Ideal. It was the dirty, bewildered first fight of people forced to survive before a foreign city. Yet they survived.

Victory brought no freedom, only exhaustion, bloodied clothes and the knowledge that tomorrow might be worse. Later Diomedes spoke to them briefly of gladiators' lot, Achaeus's laws and the little mercy left once a person became spectacle.

Before leaving, he gave them food. Hidden within it was a key to the cell—a thing too small to be called hope, yet heavy enough to alter fate.

Soon a fire broke out in the street. Cries, running feet and alarm bells filled Achaeus, and the prison received the kind of moment that seldom comes twice. The captives unlocked their cell and escaped. Freedom's first moments were blind; they ran without knowing where flight led or where the city ended.

The guards quickly noticed them. Khonsu and Urist attempted a clumsy performance to misdirect pursuit, but Achaeus was no place where a fugitive gladiator's word was readily believed. The deception collapsed into a true chase.

Still, the bars remained behind that day. Three prisoners who had recently been strangers became more than accidental cellmates: they became companions. Their road had only begun, but the first key had turned in the lock.
