# Goidair

![[Assets/Images/Goidair.jpg]]

## Main text

The **Goidair** are a northern people living among the windswept hills and stony valleys of the [[Предгорья Битхорн|Bithorn Foothills]], beside the [[Горный хребет Грань Тьмы|Edge of Darkness]]. They descend from ancient Dair who left the forests of [[Иомар|Iomar]]. Even now they recognise familiar melodies in the songs of the [[Надаир|Nadair]], though the words and customs of the two peoples have long since parted.

Goidair life is built around the clan. Kinship means more than blood: it is a shared oath, a winter endured together and the right to sit beside the same fire. Every clan preserves its own colours, the pattern of its woven sash and an ancient song that must never be performed in full before outsiders. A chief must be a generous host and the first to meet danger. One who hoards stores while others hunger swiftly loses respect, however great their wealth.

The chief deity of the Goidair is [[Тарун|Tarun]], lord of thunder. Tradition says he saved the first settlers from stone giants and showed them a country where lightning strikes bare summits more often than human dwellings. Before a long journey, the Goidair cast a sprig of heather into the fire and watch the direction of the smoke. A peal of thunder after this rite is taken as the god's answer.

A child born during a storm is called sky-marked. Such children are not always expected to win glory in war: singers, judges, herders and future [[Горец|Highlanders]] are found among them. What matters is the belief that every sky-marked child must one day make a choice upon which the whole clan will depend.

The Goidair are known for dense woollen cloth, smoked cheeses and stone houses able to withstand mountain winds. Their hospitality is loud and direct: a traveller is fed first, questioned afterwards, and only then judged a friend or a possible difficulty. An insult is remembered for years, yet someone who admits their fault honestly before the common hearth may still earn forgiveness.
