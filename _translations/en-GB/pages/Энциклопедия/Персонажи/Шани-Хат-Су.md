# Shani-Hat-Su

![[Assets/Images/Shani.jpg]]

## Main text

**Shani-Hat-Su** is a priestess of the Sun from the [[Хефат|Khefat]] people who found herself at the heart of the [[Кризис веры в Амон-Астате|Crisis of Faith in Amon-Astat]]. She played an important part in the events recounted in the saga [[Зов Бури|*Call of Thunder*]].

Shani was born to a family of farmers who cultivated reeds in the lower reaches of the [[Река Хапи|Hapi River]]. She was the youngest child, raised alongside two sisters and her elder brother, [[Рамат-Хат-Су|Ramat]]. Despite the family's poverty, Shani's early childhood was cheerful and carefree.

Her life changed abruptly at the age of twelve, when the Great Drought began in [[Амон-Астат|Amon-Astat]] in 94 NE. The Hat-Su family, never prosperous, lost its only source of food and income as the Hapi almost completely dried up. During the second year of the drought, Shani's mother died of hunger. Fearing that his children would share her fate, their father abandoned the farm and set out for the port city of [[Город Уасет|Waset]] without any true plan. He knew perfectly well that the city was already crowded with the poor and starving.

Life in Waset became slightly easier, although the struggle against nature gave way to a struggle with other people. The family settled in the docks. Shani's father and [[Рамат-Хат-Су|brother]] fished upon the open sea, while the sisters sold their catch in the market. Hunger and unrest, however, drove the authorities to desperate measures: part of every merchant's goods was seized, including the fishers' catch. Fishing could just keep the family alive, but seldom earned enough for fresh water or shelter.

One day, Shani gave two fish to a hungry boy of about seven and refused payment. Her sisters immediately reproached her generosity, for their own survival was precarious. A passing priest of the Sun overheard the dispute. Impressed by the girl's selflessness and kindness, he visited her father that evening and offered Shani a place in the priesthood at Waset's temple of the Sun. Her father was reluctant to part with his beloved youngest daughter for years of training, but understood that the temple could give her a roof, fresh water and at least a little food.

During her three years at the temple, Shani rarely saw her sisters and glimpsed her father and [[Рамат-Хат-Су|brother]] only once, through the window of her cell as their fishing boat passed at sea. The priesthood revealed to her the full horror of the drought in the desert provinces of Amon-Astat. Her duties included tending the sick and dying, offering the daily prayers for the dead and preparing their bodies for burial or cremation according to the means of each family. More than anything, Shani feared recognising one of her own kin among the countless victims.

In 99 NE, at the height of the [[Кризис веры в Амон-Астате|Crisis of Faith]], Shani left Waset with the other priests for the capital, [[Город Тиза|Tiza]], where they hoped to find the protection of [[Рамхотеп II|Pharaoh Ramhotep II]]. Their caravan never reached its destination. Near [[Город Хесус|Khesus]], a rebel company attacked, killed almost all the priests and took the women and children—including Shani—captive.

Shani learnt that the band was travelling west to join the rest of the rebel army in its attempt to seize [[Город Ахей|Achaeus]] from neighbouring [[Гилас|Gilas]]. She understood with horror that despair had brought her people to the brink of war with a realm famed for military might and ruthlessness. Several weeks later, the army reached Achaeus and, to everyone's astonishment, [[Вторжение Амон-Астата в город Ахей|the city fell]] within hours.

Shani was confined in the old city barracks with other prisoners, now including citizens of Achaeus. There she noticed a gravely wounded man and a woman bound more securely than the rest, blindfolded and gagged in addition to having her hands and feet tied. Even in captivity, the young priestess retained her compassion and her faith in [[Аст|Ast, the God of Light]]. For several days she tended the dying warrior until he finally gave his name: [[Кеннет мак Рейн|Kenneth]].

The prisoners spent about a week in the barracks under a small watch that changed twice each day. On the seventh day, a young soldier entered to count the captives as usual. Shani looked into his face and recognised her elder brother, [[Рамат-Хат-Су|Ramat]]. He was at once overjoyed and afraid: he had at last found his sister, but she was a prisoner of his commander. After a long conversation, Ramat promised to arrange her escape from Achaeus. Shani insisted that she could never reach Tiza alone and persuaded him to free [[Кеннет мак Рейн|Kenneth]] and [[Кассиопея Атанатос|Cassia]] as well.

The following evening, Ramat set fire to a granary near the barracks. Amid the panic, he released Shani and her new companions and told them where they could find provisions and camels for the journey across the desert. The company crossed the [[Пустыня Сехет|Sekhet Desert]] with great difficulty and reached [[Регион Аментир|Amentir]]. In Tiza, their paths parted: Kenneth and Cassia continued towards [[Город Атафет|Atafet]], while Shani resumed her training at the capital's temple of the Sun.
