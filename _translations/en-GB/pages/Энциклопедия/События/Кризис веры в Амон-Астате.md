# Crisis of Faith in Amon-Astat

![[Assets/Images/Faith_Crysis_Amon-Astat.jpg]]

The Great Drought began in 94 NE and, year by year, deprived [[Амон-Астат|the Empire of the Sun]] of water and harvests. Wells and mighty rivers failed, temple granaries emptied, and relief ceased to reach the distant cities. The light of [[Аст|Ast]], once the very image of life, became to many a symbol of merciless heat.

The first protests demanded water, bread and an accounting from the priests of [[Хат-Астат|Hat-Astat]]. People asked why the god of Light would not save his realm, and why his servants continued to speak of a trial while families starved. As answers failed to come, mistrust hardened into fury.

Open revolt broke out across the cities in 98 NE. Crowds demolished Ast's temples, drove out his priests and, in some places, put them to death. Merchants, soldiers and nobles who had lost kin or estates joined the insurgents. The scattered bands began to call themselves the [[Жаждущие|Thirsty]] — people who had been promised saving light and left with nothing but thirst.

Pharaoh [[Рамхотеп II|Ramhotep II]] attempted at once to defend the temples and keep the country from civil war. Yet the Crisis had ceased to be a dispute over religion. It exposed unequal access to water, the weakness of ancient institutions and the provinces' anger towards the capital. When some among the Thirsty sought to capture more prosperous lands, the result was the [[Вторжение Амон-Астата в город Ахей|invasion of Achaeus]] and the transformation of a domestic uprising into an international war.

Faith never returned to its former shape, even after the conflict ended. Temples were rebuilt, but priests and rulers could no longer demand trust merely by invoking Ast's will. To modern Amon-Astat, the Crisis remains a warning: a light that refuses to see human suffering will one day be mistaken for another form of darkness.
