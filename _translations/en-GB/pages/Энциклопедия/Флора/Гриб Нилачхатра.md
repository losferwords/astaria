# Nilachhatra Mushroom

![[Assets/Images/Nilachhatra_Mushroom.png]]

**Nilachhatra** are mushrooms with dark blue caps, growing upon damp timber in the jungles of [[Сурадж Ка Гхар|Suraj Ka Ghar]]. Their name means ‘blue parasol’ in the [[Раджати|Rajati]] tongue. After a night of rain, the mushrooms open in dense tiers like tiny awnings raised above the moss.

## Habitat and Gathering

Gatherers set out at dawn, before the heat softens the caps. In the [[Джунгли Шанкари|Shankari Jungle]], this work has become especially perilous because of the [[Ветал|vetalas]], so a basket of fresh mushrooms may cost more than meat. At market, such nilachhatra are sold together with the gatherer's name. The buyer pays not only for the food, but for a journey from which someone managed to return.

## Colour and Properties

Nilachhatra flesh changes colour according to its surroundings: in an acidic mixture it turns violet, and in salted stock almost black. Cooks know this property well and use the hue to judge whether a dish contains enough acidity. A raw cut surface briefly stains the tongue blue; children are sometimes allowed merely to touch it with the tongue, but swallowing an untreated mushroom is strictly forbidden.

Fresh flesh tastes bitter and may cause severe nausea. Nilachhatra become edible after preparation, so gathered mushrooms are not eaten straight from the forest.

## Use as Food

The mushrooms are boiled twice in fresh water before being fried with oil, soured milk and spices. The flesh remains firm and develops a deep flavour recalling both nuts and smoke. Poor villages add it to lentils and grain; wealthy households serve it upon thin flatbreads with a hot sauce.
