# Chapter 093 — Black Tears

*[[Сурадж Ка Гхар|Suraj Ka Ghar]], 106 NE, summer*

The city hospital occupied an old villa built long ago for a very different life. Heavy heat entered the wards through carved shutters, flies circled lazily beneath the ceiling, and the scents of iron and sweat mingled with medicinal herbs and cloying incense. Patients suffering from [[Ракта-Доша|Rakta-Dosha]] lay crowded beside people carried in from the jungle with fevers, bites and wounds. The attendants hurried from bed to bed as though hoping to outrun death.

The vial containing the blood of the eight-year-old boy and [[Томас Робинсон|Tom]] still stood upon the table. Sediment lay between the incompatible layers, striking through their hope of a simple cure. [[Гилья Кангарлавехотри|Gilja]] and [[Самир Тхакур|Samir]] left to examine the result, leaving Tom, [[Урист Китин|Urist]] and [[Мэй Ву|Mei]] in the hospital.

Soon a side door flew open, and two bearers carried in a woman found in the city drains. They handed her hurriedly to the attendants and vanished before anyone could ask precisely where she had lain or who might have thrown her there. A deep puncture darkened the flesh beneath her ribs. Blood was already spreading inside her, and her breathing grew fainter. Earrings set with black lotuses in gold shone at her ears, identical to those Mei had recently bought for [[Ниса|Nisa]].

The attendants meant to bind the wound and await Samir's return, but Mei understood that the woman would not live so long. The heroes recognised the missing [[Савитри Эклунд|Savitri]] and attempted something for which none of them was truly prepared.

Tom entered her dream. Rather than a nightmare, he found an utterly ordinary morning: Savitri bade farewell to her husband, the Avgar merchant [[Бату Эклунд|Batu]], and went to the market. There she showed the lotus coin from stall to stall. A boy carrying a basket passed too close, and a long thread caught upon her dress. Savitri followed it as it unravelled.

The thread led into an alley where the boy spoke with a hooded young woman. The stranger saw Savitri and firmly ordered her to leave. Savitri picked up the last of the thread and had just turned when red light flooded the dream. The blow hurled Tom out before he could speak to her.

Yet he had seen enough. Beneath blood, fear and pain, Savitri retained one simple desire: to come home once more after an ordinary day at the market. Tom held that memory beside her while Urist began an old Avgar chant of healing.

To the local attendants, his voice sounded more like a battle cry. Several elderly women immediately tried to stop the incomprehensible rite, and Urist had to drive them away with a directness taught in no hospital. The women retreated, the song continued, and the Avgar earned an entirely unexpected new title: **Lord of Grannies**.

Savitri's breathing steadied. They stopped the bleeding, secured the wound and cauterised it. When the smell of hot metal and burnt cloth dispersed, the woman opened her eyes.

The young woman from the dream was [[Чори Марджари|Chori Marjari]]. She had helped Savitri after the first attack, but rescue swiftly became a debt. Chori knew something of the woman's dark past and had held her close for two weeks through that old secret. Together they acted against people calling themselves the [[Чёрный Лотос|Black Lotus]], though Savitri never learnt how far their network extended.

Savitri was to meet Chori that night. She never reached the appointed place: agents of the Lotus caught her in an alley and stabbed her beneath the ribs. She had told her husband nothing. Savitri knew Batu too well—had he learnt the truth, he would have taken the heaviest weapon in his shop and ended either in a grave or a city cell.

As she spoke, Tom caught a familiar metallic scent. The sounds of the hospital receded, his body grew heavy and black tears ran from his eyes. The darkness within him was no longer an obedient power: it dissolved the boundary between the man and the [[Тень|Shadow]]. With his remaining strength, Tom told his friends that they must follow the raven.

He could have remained at the hospital. Mercate had issued no command, and the raven demanded nothing. Yet Tom loved his goddess and freely chose the path after her. Urist and Mei helped him stand, and the three followed the black bird.

The raven led them to [[Вирадж Сомани|Viraj's]] abandoned incense shop. The sharp scents of spices and traces of hurried flight still lingered in its rooms. The bird settled upon the carpet, the shadows around it turned away from the moonlight, and [[Мерката|Mercate]] emerged from the darkness.

At the sight of Tom's condition, the Goddess of Night forgot the stones. She came close, rested her brow against his, listened to his breathing and held her Shadow tightly. Mercate did not know the precise cause of the fit, but promised to discover it and ordered Tom to rest.

Only then did she examine the black shards from Viraj's cache. The stone was obsidian tied to caves known to Mercate and to the recent mineral shipments entering Vanpur. Part of the cargo had come from the country around [[Деревня Улехей|Ulehey]]. No ordinary merchant would break such material into thin, sharp pieces without purpose. Mercate believed they might be fashioning needles and small blades able to conduct the power of unending sleep into another body. Whence that power came and how the weapons were imbued remained unknown.

The black casket found beside the stones offered no answer either. It resembled the coffer that had once held the [[Сердце Огня|Heart of Fire]], but the likeness was only superficial.

Mercate connected Tom's fit to the return of the Shadows to her power. Servants such as Tom and [[Селина Эллиот|Selina Elliot]] had retained their own will—and they were the ones who suffered these states. Shadows in perfect submission felt no such pain. The goddess did not conceal how little she knew, but promised to come if the fit returned. Then she kissed Tom upon the cheek and dissolved into the night mist.

Despite all they had endured, the travellers did not abandon the meeting with Chori. Night had already fallen over Vanpur when they entered a narrow alley. Two black-clad figures appeared ahead, while two more closed the way behind. Curved shards upon their gloves darkened like short claws. The heroes knew enough to understand that a single puncture might condemn them to eternal sleep.

The attackers gave neither names nor reason for the ambush. Tom loosed an arrow before the nearest could approach, and the first man fell. Mei met the others at close quarters, allowing them no room for a precise strike. Urist brought his hammer down upon the Lotus agents with the full weight of Avgar fury.

Three attackers died. Realising he would be taken alive and questioned, the last drove his black claws into his own neck. Death did not come: he fell instantly into the very sleep from which, the heroes believed, no one ever returned.

The attackers carried identical lotus rings, thread, another black casket and gloves fitted with shards. The heroes carefully removed every claw and placed them inside the casket. They looked just like the stones in Viraj's stores, but no one yet knew whether they were the same substance or what transformed obsidian into a weapon of eternal sleep. Even Savitri's and Mei's earrings remained an ambiguous sign: ornament, token of entry or mere coincidence.

It was about three in the morning. The heroes deliberately left the bodies in the alley and took everything they had found. They never reached Chori. The meeting still lay ahead, while behind them remained four bodies and a question the Black Lotus would surely answer before the city guard.
