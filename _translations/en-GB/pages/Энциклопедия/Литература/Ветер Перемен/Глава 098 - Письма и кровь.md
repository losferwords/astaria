# Chapter 098 — Letters and Blood

*[[Сурадж Ка Гхар|Suraj Ka Ghar]], 106 NE, autumn*

While the travellers remained in [[Город Ванпур|Vanpur]], the pigeon post brought them troubles from other cities. Amid temple secrets, missing people and misfortunes of their own, they had to find time for wages, supplies and visitors who interpreted hospitality rather too freely. [[Город Бахара|Bahara]] wrote about [[Бордель Халык-Бындыр|Halyk-Byndir]], [[Город Джу-Суо|Dju-Suo]] about [[Бордель Уй-Джан|Ui-Djan]]. Sending an answer was easier than appearing in person, but the running of both establishments now depended on a few written lines.

The first matter began with contradictory instructions about money. A supplier had received an advance payment quite properly, yet an order to freeze payments followed it. Someone was trying to set the owners against the people who kept the house supplied. After examining the notes, they identified the storekeeper as the culprit.

[[Гилья Кангарлавехотри|Gilja]] remembered the kitchen at Halyk-Byndir. The Avgar woman working there kept her spices neatly arranged on shelves; this had impressed him more than any assurances of trustworthiness. In his opinion, someone like that could also keep track of incoming goods. They decided to appoint the cook, whose name was Padmi, chief bookkeeper and give her authority to check deliveries. She was to keep an eye on the storekeeper as well.

[[Навчин Урт|Navchin]] was considered too unreliable for the position. The storekeeper was not dismissed, but his wages were cut by a quarter. The supplier kept the silver due to him, and Gilja took responsibility for overseeing financial matters of this kind.

They also decided to safeguard their correspondence. The final paragraph of a letter was to contain a sentence beginning with the first letter of the sender's name. For [[Урист Китин|Urist]], the letter was U; for Gilja, G; for [[Мэй Ву|Mei]], M; for [[Томас Робинсон|Tom]], T. The simple sign would help distinguish a genuine instruction from a note someone had written in the owners' names.

The next message from Bahara already carried the agreed mark. Dockside thugs had appeared at Halyk-Byndir's entrance. Calling themselves guards, they drove away guests, obstructed deliveries and demanded a fifth of the week's takings. The new bookkeeper awaited an answer.

Mei refused to pay. The house had fewer guards than there were extortionists, but she ordered a public show of resistance: single out one of the newcomers, put him firmly in his place and make the others understand that the establishment could defend itself. Word was to spread through the harbour that reinforcements had already left Ui-Djan. Anyone who continued demanding payment was threatened with torture in the Ju manner.

Mei did not rely on rumours alone. A second letter went to [[Рон Эллиот|Ron Elliot]], who watched over Ui-Djan's security. He was asked to send several imposing men to Bahara, providing visible support for the talk of numerous guards. The plan worked. The extortionists withdrew, and Halyk-Byndir could return to its business.

In Dju-Suo, the difficulty was of another kind. An influential merchant and his retinue had again occupied Ui-Djan's best parlour and showed no intention of paying. [[Сяо Ша|Xiao Sha]] was no longer willing to let his debt grow without the owners' consent. The guest promised to settle it once he had rebuilt his burnt estate.

The merchant was [[Унгол Баят|Ungol Bayat]]. Urist had once owed him money, and later the travellers had rescued him from the nomads. Ungol, however, chiefly remembered the fire at his villa and considered that old grievance sufficient grounds for further indulgence.

Urist dictated a letter to Xiao. He reminded the merchant that his life had been saved and asked him not to give Urist cause to regret his mercy. The fire had happened long ago; a man of the Wildlands ought not to shelter behind a burnt house for so many years. Besides, Ungol was in a city where Urist was honoured as a Hero. If that argument proved insufficient, there was still the option of appealing to the Empress.

In a postscript, the owner gave the manager a simpler instruction: hit Ungol properly, since an Avgar might otherwise fail to understand.

Xiao Sha read the letter with an impassive face, attempting to convey Urist's manner. When she reached the end, she swung her fist into the merchant's face. Ungol paid his entire debt and left an extra bag of silver. The mention of the Empress and the postscript, already carried out, had been enough; his battered face would remind him of the correspondence for some time.

Then Ron reported thefts. Over three evenings, three purses had disappeared during performances at Ui-Djan. Angry visitors demanded that everyone be searched, but Xiao objected: many came to the house precisely for privacy and had no wish to attract attention. The thief had to be caught without turning the evening hall into a guardroom.

Tom suggested watching for repeated behaviour among the guests, noting those who hurried away after a performance, and preparing bait. [[Чень Ляньши|Chen Lianshi]] was chosen to play a wealthy, absent-minded visitor. They dressed him up, left his purse in plain sight and set aside a bag of silver for his drinking. Chen did not drink in small quantities; there was little point in economising on a convincing performance.

The thief took the bait. He failed to recognise his former drinking companion in the rich clothes and reached for the purse. When the theft was spotted, he tried to run, but outside he was knocked down by one of the horses Tom had once rescued in Chang-Sha.

Knowing Chen had previously helped the pickpocket gain entry to establishments. Now Chen had stopped buying him drinks, and the former companion had decided to find money for himself.

With the establishments' affairs settled, Urist wrote to [[Деревня Улехей|Ulehey]]. Along with word that he was well, a cactus brooch — the emblem of the Kitin family — was sent to [[Ли Сан|Li San]]. The family news was followed by a business proposal. The income from Halyk-Byndir and Ui-Djan could be invested in smelting metal: Suraj Ka Ghar was preparing for change and needed more than ore alone. Urist considered seven chests of spoils enough for a beginning.

Li San's reply was warm. [[Аргея Китин|Argeya]] and [[Лимар Китин|Limar]] were growing, she wore the brooch, and she supported her husband's idea. The smelting should be organised in Ulehey itself, with help from local craftspeople and her mother, [[Чани Сан|Chani]]. First, however, they needed to know which metal was required, how much was expected and by when. Li San proposed keeping part of the establishments' income in reserve for wages and repairs.

Finished furnaces and the first smelting were still some way off. For now, Urist sent a separate reminder of his promise to [[Рахман Саркар|Rahman Sarkar]]: a shipment of copper ore was to reach Vanpur within the agreed two weeks. Messengers were to travel ahead of the caravan. If the delivery was delayed, the elder should at least hear of it in advance.

In Vanpur itself, concern for other people's wages gave way to the question of how much of the travellers' own bodies would remain by morning.

At the Second Brew, Gilja was still trying to explain the vanished part of his right arm as a consequence of the moonshine. Urist, whose buttocks had disappeared, found that explanation inadequate. Too much recalled the invisible, tooth-filled creatures they had already encountered and the drunken rite beside the statue.

Urist decided to test another idea immediately. He punched Gilja in the nose, collected the blood in a cup and tasted it. Gilja found this a compelling argument for urgently attending to the reversal of the ritual.

One of Urist's buttocks began to reappear. Then the invisibility receded altogether, restoring his cherished part of the body. His relief was incomplete, however: after the mouthful of blood, he wanted more.

The opposite was happening to Gilja. The emptiness crept further up his arm and reached his chest. The invisible flesh remained with him, but watching his own body disappear was growing harder. Urist went to wash, trying to make sense of the new hunger.

That night, Tom dreamt of a swamp.

He stood knee-deep in warm water. Silt clung beneath his feet, wet roots and vines intertwined overhead, and pale mist hung between the trunks. An arrow passed beside his temple. A second struck a tree, its black fletching still quivering when the [[Ракша|Reapers]] emerged from the gloom.

Scarlet marks glowed on bare skin. The Rakshas climbed over roots with raised raktakataras, cried the name of [[Шубханкари|Shubhankari]] and charged towards the arrows. [[Тень|Mercate's Shadows]] moved among the branches and trunks. Black feathers flashed above the water; archers fell back, shot again and fell beneath the blades.

A wounded Reaper reached towards one of them. Blood rose in fine threads and soaked into the red marks, and the Raksha stood again. Then more arrows struck him. The water received the blood of both sides, cries broke off amid prayers, and the mist concealed where the battle ended.

Stone steps rose from the swamp. Beyond them lay the broken statue of a six-armed woman, tangled in roots. A raven sat upon the stone crown. Below stood [[Селина Эллиот|Selina]], holding a small vial. Dried blood darkened its base.

Above the noise of battle came [[Мерката|Mercate's]] familiar voice: she commanded that what belonged to her should not be given to the other. A second woman's voice followed, demanding the return of what had been taken. Selina searched Tom's face. She ought to have remembered him, but recognition slipped away; he too could not hold the fragments of roads, faces and touches surfacing within him.

Selina held out the vial and asked his name.

The dream was no memory. Tom remembered Morogrim's warning: what he saw that night was to come true. Somewhere ahead lay the swamp, Shadows and Reapers fighting one another, and Selina with a vial of dried blood. The four samples found in the hiding place also needed explaining. The travellers needed someone who could make sense of them.

In the morning, Mei discovered that Gita was gone. During the night, she had asked the girl to wake her before the meeting with Kiran's intermediary so that they could go together. Gita had gone alone.

Mei went downstairs to Nadila. [[Чори Марджари|Chori]] stood at the counter. She returned the Lotus ring and the small-share bowl the girl had taken to arrange the meeting. The good news was that Chori knew where to find the mentor. The bad news was that Gita had been put into the black sleep.

Her real name was [[Тара Саркар|Tara]]. She was the daughter of Vanpur's elder.

Mei heard this without breaking down. Only recently, the girl had held her arm and asked not to be left alone. Now Chori was offering to show them where she lay.

They went beyond the city limits to a stream. Tara lay motionless beneath a bush, hidden from the crows. She was breathing, but neither voice nor touch brought her back to the world.

Chori told them about her pursuit. She had seen Tara leave the city and followed her. A broad-shouldered Rajati in a mask had appeared, one of the network's senior intermediaries. His figure reminded her of [[Шанкар Марджари|Shankar]], though she had not seen his face. After that meeting, Tara had been left asleep.

According to Chori's information, the people of the [[Чёрный Лотос|Black Lotus]] had already left Vanpur. There had been several trails, and she could not follow all of them at once. But Kiran remained in the laboratory behind the waterfall and, as far as she had discovered, intended to spend several days there.

They decided to take Tara to the hospital. Mei would not let any of the men carry her. She lifted her herself and bore her away, though the unfamiliar weight strained her back. The girl did not wake on the way.

They found [[Савитри Эклунд|Savitri]] with [[Самир Тхакур|Samir]]. She had almost recovered from her wound and intended to return to her husband. The physician recognised the elder's daughter; she had been seen at the hospital before. The name Gita could no longer conceal whom the travellers had brought in.

They found a needle mark on Tara's neck. It confirmed Chori's warning: the girl had been put to sleep with the Lotus's substance. This was no ordinary rest.

Among other people, Urist had to restrain himself. The thirst had not passed since he drank Gilja's blood, but he would not attack the living. Chori stayed to tell Samir what had happened by the stream. Savitri went home to Batu.

The travellers would not wait for another arranged meeting. They now knew where to find Kiran, and Gilja's transformation was continuing. Having finished speaking with Samir, Chori led them to the waterfall and showed them the secret descent. She did not want to appear before the mentor herself.

Beyond the roar of the water, the familiar underground rooms opened before them again. [[Киран Варма|Kiran Varma]] sat in the hall with four rooms, where they had previously found records and water. He wore a Raksha's ritual robes. He greeted the newcomers calmly, as though he had avoided them merely because there was so much to do, and expressed his pleasure at finally seeing a colleague.

Gilja rejected the greeting. In his view, someone who had abandoned Shubhankari's Harvest had no right to call himself a Raksha.

Kiran showed him the scrolls and replied that these were his daily Harvest.

Gilja accused him and Samir of obstructing the succession of the Cycles of Destruction and Creation. The mentor heard the accusation without a burst of anger. Samir, he acknowledged, had indeed ceased to fulfil his duty. But Kiran himself continued giving every drop to the goddess. If Gilja doubted him, he was ready to show the records.
