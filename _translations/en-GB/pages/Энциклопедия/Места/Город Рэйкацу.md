# Reikatsu

![[Assets/Images/Reikatsu_City.png]]

## Main text

Reikatsu (霊霞津, れいかつ) is a major port of [[Амато|Amato]] on the southern coast of [[Туманный остров|Mist Island]], within the [[Луга Тамано|Tamano Meadows]]. Ships arriving from the direction of the [[Белый пролив|White Strait]] make a stop here. Rice from the surrounding fields is brought to the city for shipment to other islands. Reikatsu is also known for sake and rice vinegar; trade in the harvest goes hand in hand with rice processing. Cups and vessels for local workshops come in part from [[Город Кири|Kiri]], to the north-east.

The name combines the characters for spirit, haze and harbour; its poetic interpretation is “the misty harbour of spirits”.

Passing ships bring work to merchants, dockworkers and the keepers of local establishments. Smugglers and pirates follow, while drinking and dubious dealings sustain Reikatsu's ill repute. Good money can be made here, though another person's good fortune can easily lead to a dangerous acquaintance. There are fewer pirates than in [[Город Бахара|Bahara]], and imperial authority sets limits to the port's freedoms.

Sea winds carry mist towards the city, quickly concealing its coastal streets. In damp weather the cedar walls darken, water runs from the curved roofs and passers-by seek shelter beneath awnings. The glow of paper windows and lanterns shows through the veil.

## The street of taverns and gambling houses

Reikatsu owes much of its notoriety to a street of taverns and gambling houses. In the evenings, paper lanterns are lit above the wet paving. Their light trembles in the puddles while sailors, merchants and people whose occupations are seldom discussed before strangers shelter beneath the eaves. Voices, laughter and invitations to try the local sake drift through open doors.

People drink, gamble and strike dubious bargains here. For a newcomer, this street can easily become a first introduction to Reikatsu's wealth and dangers: money changes hands, and a chance encounter may bring a profitable offer or trouble.

White lanterns burn at the doors of some establishments. Their light promises hot rice and tea even to a traveller without money. Amid the noise of the night street, this is a familiar sign of help for someone who cannot pay.

In gambling houses, visitors customarily leave their weapons at the entrance, in the owner's keeping. The owner is responsible for order within.

## The daimyo's rule

Reikatsu is governed by a daimyo, a samurai subject to Empress and Shogun [[Аматсу Рин|Amatsu Rin]]. The daimyo answers to her for order in the city and holds enough authority to keep the port from falling into open piracy. Local dealers know the limits: while their business stays within the bounds set by the daimyo, they may make their fortunes, but crossing that boundary brings the ruler's intervention.

The imperial court lies across the sea, yet the orders of the shogun and her bakufu are binding here too. The daimyo's independence in local affairs rests upon service to Amato's supreme sovereign.

## The watch and the Onmyoji

The city watch is accustomed to working with the [[Онмёдзи|Onmyoji]]: hunters are regularly called upon when possession is suspected. They detect the presence of another will and perform purification rites, seeking to save [[Они|those who are possessed]].

The inhabitants respect the hunters, yet fear their closeness to the spirit world. A blindfold conceals an Onmyoji's red eyes, but meeting one still makes many people lower their voices. To the watch, a hunter's arrival means help with a task for which an ordinary sword may not suffice.
