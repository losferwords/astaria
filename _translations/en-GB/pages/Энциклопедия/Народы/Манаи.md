# Manai

![[Assets/Images/Manai.jpg]]

## Main text

The **Manai** are a brown-skinned seafaring people of eastern [[Архея|Archaea]], inhabiting the [[Полуостров Туануку|Tu'anuku Peninsula]] and the coastal villages of [[Хамоа|Hamoa]]. Written sources reveal almost nothing of their distant past: the domains of the [[Хтониды|Chthonids]] lay too far away, while the Manai entrusted memory to songs, genealogies and the tales of helmsmen.

Tradition says that the Manai ancestors fled a great calamity by sea. The goddess [[Икатерра|Ikaterra]] led them to a safe shore, where they founded [[Деревня Хамоа Хо|Hamoa Ho]]. They call themselves the Wanderers not because they lack a home, but in remembrance of the voyage after which every shelter came to be received as a gift.

The Manai live by fishing, gardening, gathering and building light boats. They read the sea in the shape of waves, the colour of the water, clouds, birds and the behaviour of reef-dwelling creatures. An experienced helmsman can recognise an approaching storm long before the sky darkens, yet rarely attributes such knowledge to a single sign: the way ahead is assembled from dozens of small observations.

The Manai relationship with marine life runs deeper than fishing. They learn the habits of great fish and sea beasts, free entangled animals, leave breeding grounds undisturbed and sometimes accept their aid during a voyage. The Manai never claim to have mastered the sea. A fine swimmer, they say, is someone whom the water and its inhabitants have consented to receive.

Healing holds a special place within their culture. Families preserve methods of purifying water, staunching blood, easing fever and making splints from flexible wood. A healer is never separate from the community: while they care for the sick, neighbours feed their family, mend the roof and take their place at sea.

Manai peaceableness is founded not upon fear of conflict, but upon the high value of human life. A dispute is resolved where possible through the counsel of elders, restitution and labour performed together. When danger threatens a village, however, its inhabitants act as one and need few commands. The seven [[Хранитель|Wardens]] remain the people's mightiest defenders, but every fisher knows which oar will serve as a club and where children must be taken during an attack.

The Manai favour bright cloth, woven ornaments, flowers and patterns recalling waves, fish scales and ancestral routes. Songs accompany rowing, work and evening meals: one voice begins the tale and the others take it up. Thus the past remains alive not in a sealed archive, but among people who remember when to join the common chorus.
