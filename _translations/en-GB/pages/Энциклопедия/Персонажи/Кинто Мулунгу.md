# Kinto Mulungu

![[Assets/Images/Kinto_Mulungu.jpg]]

## Main text

**Kinto Mulungu** is the High [[Шаман|Shaman]] of [[Руфу|Rufu]] and spiritual leader of the [[Вакумара|Vakumara]] confederation. He wears no royal crown and cannot command its tribes with a single decree. His authority rests upon the chiefs' trust, his knowledge of ritual and the Vaku belief that the Dead God can speak through him.

The Mulungu lineage has preserved its priestly tradition since the confederation was founded. Kinto's ancestor helped unite the grief-stricken tribes around Rufu's necropolis, and each succeeding generation taught its children the arts of healing, poison, burial and communion with the ancestors. To the family, service has never been an honourable title: it is a duty to safeguard the memory of an entire people.

At the age of twelve, Kinto survived an epidemic from which his father's rites and medical skill saved those closest to him. The boy then resolved to master everything that had allowed his family to live. He soon displayed an uncommon attentiveness to plants, the human body and the dreams of the sick, and later became the youngest Shaman known to Vaku history.

After his father's death, Kinto performed one of the most demanding rites of his lineage and received his soul, inheriting a portion of his knowledge and memories. To an outsider, such a practice may appear terrifying. Kinto himself describes it as a final lesson, freely passed from father to son. Since then, his speech has sometimes taken on turns of phrase once favoured by older generations of the Mulungu family.

Kinto appears frail beside the tribes' warriors, yet age and composure lend weight to his presence. He seldom raises his voice, listens patiently to those in dispute and then asks a question that forces both parties to acknowledge their own fault. When reconciliation proves impossible, the High Shaman can be merciless: preserving the confederation matters more to him than the pride of any single lineage.

In [[Деревня Намибуа|Namibua]], Kinto receives pilgrims, settles disputes between chiefs and watches over the necropolis. His greatest concern is preventing the memory of Rufu from becoming nothing more than a thirst for vengeance. The Vaku must remember their defeat and still continue to live; otherwise, the “deathless” confederation will become a people that never escaped its own grave.
