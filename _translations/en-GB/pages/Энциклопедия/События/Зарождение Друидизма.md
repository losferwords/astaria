# Birth of Druidism

![[Assets/Images/Druidism.jpg]]

The birth of Druidism is associated with the meeting of the [[Надаир|Nadair]] and the goddess of life, [[Церунна|Cerunna]], beside [[Озеро Сильвиан|Lake Silvian]] in 1797 ChE. Her teachings gave rise to the [[Друид|Druid]] tradition and the [[Круг Друидов|Druid Circle]], a community that would become a foundation of religious and political life in [[Иомар|Iomar]].

## The Meeting at Silvian

The ancestors of the Nadair reached central [[Кельн|Keln]] as settlers seeking land for a new life. From the [[Даирские луга|Dair Meadows]], they ventured into [[Лес Иомар|Iomar Forest]]. Tradition holds that, on Silvian's shores, they encountered a tall woman with red hair, branching antlers and the legs of a deer.

Cerunna taught them to see the forest as a living whole. Trees, animals, water and human settlements depended upon one another; any life taken required care in return. This teaching changed their relationship with their new homeland: a community's prosperity could not be separated from the health of its land.

## The First Keepers

The goddess's closest pupils became the first Druids, while other attendants were known as the children of Cerunna. Their duties joined healing and herbal knowledge with observation of animals and care for woods and waters. Noticing a sick tree or tending a wounded creature mattered as much as fidelity to ritual.

In time, these keepers began to settle disputes between communities and establish limits upon hunting, felling and farming. A religious teaching became a way of living together: those who governed were obliged to consider the forest's future.

## A Living Tradition

Druidism retained its connection to its birthplace. Silvian remains a sacred lake, and the Nadair believe that the goddess still walks its shores. Cerunna's temples and altars often stand beside springs, in clearings or amongst ancient trees. The encounter at the lake is remembered as the beginning of a lasting covenant between the settlers and the land they learned to cherish.
