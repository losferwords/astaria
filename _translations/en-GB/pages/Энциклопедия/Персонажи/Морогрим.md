# Morogrim

![[Assets/Images/Morogrim.png]]

Morogrim is a black raven, [[Мерката|Mercate's]] messenger and longtime companion. The goddess entrusts him with messages for her servants and, in quieter hours, lets him settle close beside her. Those who have seen them together notice how attentively the bird follows her every movement.

## Appearance and Habits

He is a large raven with a strong beak, black eyes and dense plumage that catches a cool sheen. Morogrim will sit motionless for a long time, turning his head towards whoever is speaking. He knows how to wait until a person has calmed down or finished an argument before drawing their attention with a brief tap of his beak.

His behaviour reveals a long familiarity with people. He remembers faces, listens closely to conversations and seldom wastes his energy on needless fuss. Anyone he beckons to follow may have to make rather more effort: when choosing a route, Morogrim does not always consider how easily a person can keep up with a bird.

## The Goddess's Messenger

Morogrim understands human speech, but answers through caws, movements of his head, taps of his beak and his own actions. He can indicate a direction, persistently beckon someone to follow or repeat a gesture until his meaning becomes clear. A person familiar with birds finds such messages easier to understand; a stranger may mistake a warning for ordinary raven mischief.

The raven carries small objects and knows how to lead the recipient somewhere a message can be delivered without unwanted witnesses. His appearance is associated with Mercate's will, yet a black silhouette above a rooftop gives little clue to the news he brings.

## Beside Mercate

With the goddess, Morogrim is quite at home. He preens and tidies her hair with his beak, caws crossly when she wears herself out with work and insistently demands her attention if he thinks she ought to rest. Mercate is accustomed to this grumbling and allows him to remain beside her even when she wants no other company.

Morogrim is quick to notice her weariness and changes of mood. In difficult hours, he settles closer and stays for a long time. He faithfully carries out distant errands, but finds a prolonged separation hard to bear: on his return, he scarcely leaves Mercate's side for a while and watches her with particular care. His concern can be tiring, and persuading the old raven to give her a little peace takes patience.
