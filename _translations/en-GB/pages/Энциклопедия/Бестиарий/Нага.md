# Naga

![[Assets/Images/Naga.jpg]]

**Nagas** are a predatory, intelligent species inhabiting the marshes, bogs and humid jungles of [[Сурадж Ка Гхар|Suraj Ka Ghar]]. They are encountered most often in the [[Болота Кхали Дарти|Khali Dharti Marshes]], the [[Джунгли Сундари|Sundari Jungle]], the [[Джунгли Шанкари|Shankari Jungle]], the [[Топи Нирагхат|Niraghat Bogs]] and the region of [[Ладвакхара|Ladvakhara]]. There they excavate subterranean nests or occupy old caves, abandoned temples and damp fissures in the rock.

A naga resembles a woman whose legs have been replaced by a long serpent's body. Her upper body appears almost human, but only at first glance: the skin bears a cold, moist sheen; the pupils are vertical; the teeth are sharp and curve slightly inwards; the tongue is forked; and every movement is too smooth, too gliding, to possess a human being's angularity.

The species' defining feature is its six arms, arranged in three pairs along the torso. With them, a naga may wield several weapons, cling to a wall, move stones, weave nets, constrict prey and strike from different directions at once.

## Behaviour

Nagas are not mere beasts. They can reason, communicate and organise, though the people of Suraj Ka Ghar long regarded them simply as monsters of cave and marsh. For the [[Раджати|Rajati]] who dwell near their territories, an encounter with a naga usually promises danger: nagas are strong, stealthy, keenly attuned to humid tunnels and deadly at close quarters.

Recent discoveries, however, reveal that they cannot be reduced to the image of a savage monster. They are capable of intricate choices and negotiation, and aspire to a civilisation of their own. This revelation profoundly unsettled [[Шубханкари|Shubhankari]], who had long regarded them first and foremost as enemies and beasts.

## Relations with humankind

Nagas now stand at the heart of Shubhankari's religious and historical conflict. One of their kind, [[Шаша|Shasha]], became an emissary between her people and the inhabitants of [[Сурадж Ка Гхар|Suraj Ka Ghar]]. Through her, it became clear that the disappearances and violence surrounding the Great Harvests were connected not only to bloodshed, but also to concealed attempts at contact between two cultures.

For Shubhankari herself, meeting the nagas became a painful yet vital turning point. Their longing for civilisation forced the goddess to question both her former judgement of them and her own conception of what makes a monster.
