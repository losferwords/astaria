## A homeland found beneath the trees

The first Dair arrived from [[Остров Хтон|Chthon]] upon the shores of [[Кельн|Keln]] in 1866 ChE. The settlers needed a year to establish themselves. In 1865 ChE they founded [[Город Муир|Muir]] beside the [[Даирские луга|Dair Meadows]], and named their new state for the city. Yet the newcomers found a true homeland only after meeting [[Церунна|Cerunna]] deep within the great forest. In the god's honour, they renamed their country Iomar and accepted that a state upon this land must be nature's guest and guardian, never its master.

## A land of two faces

Southern Iomar opens towards the sea and the wider world. Herds graze upon the meadows, grain and medicinal herbs grow in cultivated fields, and Muir's wharves receive merchants, pilgrims and wandering musicians. Markets echo with spirited argument, seasonal festivals fill the roads, and honey, dried herbs, cloth and carved wood pass from hand to hand.

The north begins where the road enters beneath the canopy of [[Лес Иомар|Iomar Forest]]. Settlements grow smaller and paths narrower; any tree to be felled requires the leave of a local keeper. Within the deep wood, humankind has no right to claim mastery. A herd may halt a caravan, roots may turn a road, and a [[Друид|Druid]] may overrule a sovereign's decree when it threatens the forest's balance.

## Rule of the Circle

Iomar is governed by the [[Круг Друидов|Druid Circle]]. Most of its elder servants are women, leading outsiders to call the country a matriarchy, though authority is not inherited through blood. The High Druidess is chosen in an assembly of the Circle and acknowledged only so long as her decisions serve land and people alike. The present ruler, [[Аластриона Растущая|Alastriona the Growing]], received her office at an unusually young age, but has proven that years and maturity are not always the same.

Four times each year, at solstice and equinox, representatives of the temples gather to exchange tidings. They decide where a road may be opened, which part of the forest must remain untouched, who should receive aid after a failed harvest, and when a settlement ought to yield before a river that has outgrown its banks. Such councils proceed slowly, but a decision reached there seldom needs to be imposed by force.

## Custom and the traveller's path

For the [[Надаир|Nadair]], care for nature is not an ornamental rite but the basis of daily life. A hunter thanks the quarry, a beekeeper leaves honey for the hive, and a builder plants a sapling before touching an old tree. Even the largest settlements try not to choke a watercourse or raise a wall across the path animals have followed to drink for centuries.

Iomar seems hospitable to a traveller so long as its unspoken laws are respected. Here one may find a rare remedy, a wise guide or a path to a forgotten sanctuary, but no one may purchase the right to harm a living thing without consequence. The forest remembers discourtesy far longer than people do.
