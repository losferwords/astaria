# Kasumito

![[Assets/Images/Kasumito_City.png]]

## Main text

**Kasumito** is a town of [[Амато|Amato]] on the shore of [[Озеро Шинда|Lake Shinda]], beside the outflow of the [[Река Кумокава|Kumokawa]]. The river connects the town with [[Деревня Аманохама|Amanohama]] and the coast of the [[Закатное море|Sunset Sea]].

## The townscape

Approaching along the Kumokawa, travellers see busy landings, storehouses and covered galleries beside the water. Cargo is constantly carried between the boats and trading yards.

New wooden houses and workshops stand among the older neighbourhoods, and temples are under construction. Kasumito's appearance bears the marks of the post-war reforms of [[Мориока Масанори|Morioka Masanori]].

## Government and trade

Kasumito is home to the residence of the island daimyo, [[Мориока Масанори|Morioka Masanori]], who governs Amato's holdings on Mist Island under Empress and Shogun [[Аматсу Рин|Amatsu Rin]]. Envoys are received here, and the affairs of the lake district are administered. Masanori is respected for his noble birth and the reforms carried out in the town and neighbouring settlements after the war with [[Обитель|Obitelj]].

The town serves as a centre of lake trade. Rice arrives from the fields of [[Луга Тамано|Tamano]], fish from Lake Shinda, and timber and game from the southern reaches of the [[Дымовая Чаща|Smoke Thicket]]. Some supplies and goods are sold in the town; others are sent farther down the Kumokawa. The landings connect the town's markets with lakeside villages and river settlements.

The wealth of the Smoke Thicket is the chief economic advantage of the local daimyo's domain. Timber deliveries to the island's towns strengthen his standing among Amato's other governors. The safety of those working in the forest is therefore of particular importance to him.

## Kasumi-don

Kasumito is renowned for its refined preparation of **[[Касуми-дон|Kasumi-don]]** (霞丼, かすみどん): river fish grilled over charcoal and served on hot rice beneath a thick mushroom gravy. The dish has become one of the reasons visitors travel upriver to the town along the [[Река Кумокава|Kumokawa]].

Cooks pay particular attention to grilling the fish, preserving its juiciness and developing the flavour of the gravy. They arrange the fish carefully on the rice, considering the presentation of the whole bowl. Admirers of the town's eating houses debate who handles the charcoal best, prepares the finest mushroom gravy and combines it most skilfully with the fish.

In [[Деревня Аманохама|Amanohama]], Kasumi-don is simple, home-style fare. From there, connoisseurs and lovers of fine cooking are directed north along the river to Kasumito's cooks. The town's cooks also take part in the annual river cooking competition, where residents and visitors choose the best ‘bowl of mist’.

## Post-war rebuilding

Under Masanori's leadership, the landings and storehouses were restored and river transport along the Kumokawa was re-established. The daimyo helped returning soldiers and families who had lost their homes in the war rebuild their houses and livelihoods. These efforts strengthened his reputation among the people of the district.

At the daimyo's invitation, architects have come from the capital, [[Город Кайто|Kaito]], to teach local craftspeople how to build temples. There are still few local architects and artisans skilled in temple building in the lake district.

The building of new temples, torii and shrines to [[Ицунэ|Itsune]] goes hand in hand with the passing on of the craft. The visiting masters work alongside apprentices, gradually training those who will be able to continue building on the island.

Timber for these projects is felled in the Smoke Thicket. The demand for building timber connects the town's craftspeople with those working in the dangerous southern forests.

## The princess's visit

[[Аматсу Ами|Amatsu Ami]] came to Kasumito on an official visit to the daimyo. The princess disappeared after visiting Masanori. Her disappearance placed the ruler in an extremely difficult position before the empress.

Masanori retained his office and leads the search, reporting personally to Rin. A bakufu official has arrived in the town to investigate the disappearance, and the daimyo now acts under his close scrutiny.

Passengers have already been checked and boat crews questioned at the landings of Lake Shinda and the Kumokawa, but no information about the princess has been found. Small armed parties search the banks and forest paths. Deep in the thicket, they face the threat of [[Менада|maenads]] and oni, while the approaching winter makes the search still more difficult.

A reward has been offered for reliable information about Ami. It attracts both willing helpers and people hoping for easy money.

## Word from the thicket

Loggers found a mortally wounded [[Онмёдзи|Onmyoji]] in the Smoke Thicket. He managed to tell them that he had been pursuing an [[Они|oni]], but had been unexpectedly wounded by the possessed one's companions. The hunter died there in the forest. The loggers hurried to Kasumito with the alarming news and brought his body to a local temple.

The dying hunter's words gave rise to fears that the possessed come to one another's aid and are capable of acting together. The loggers did not witness the fight themselves: the attack is known only through the Onmyoji's final account.

The temple and the daimyo sent urgent messages to the capital and other temples, asking for experienced Onmyoji to be sent. The ruler is as keen to remove the threat as the priests are: the economy of his domain and its timber deliveries depend on work in the forest.

Logging has slowed. Following the accounts of the hunter's death, few are willing to enter the thicket alone, and the workers' fear is disrupting the usual course of their work.

Rumours circulate through the town about [[Нурари-химэ|Nurari-hime]], a young ‘Queen of Demons’ supposedly seen from afar among the oni. There is no reliable information about her identity. Some find these stories frightening; others recall earlier tales of forest and mountain demons, which often proved to be wolves, maenads, werewolves or the sounds of the wind.

The name became known through a hunter's account: he claims that he escaped when the possessed pursuing him abandoned the chase after a girl appeared.
