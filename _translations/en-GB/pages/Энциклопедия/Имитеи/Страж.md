# Guardian

When the earth beneath a home trembles and authority demands that cruelty be called duty, the Wends turn to those who have sworn to stand between danger and another person's hearth.

## Main text

The **Guardians** are the Imithei of [[Велисса|Velissa]] and protectors of [[Обитель|Obitelj]]. The goddess created their path during the war begun by [[Ивар Свирепый|Ivar the Fierce]], choosing the first Guardians from among the Wends who refused to take part in his conquests. Their uprising helped liberate [[Город Мирград|Mirgrad]], overthrow the king and end the bloodshed.

The Guardian's path did not vanish with the war. Its purpose is broader than service to the crown: a Guardian protects people, the land and the possibility of returning to peaceful life. Today these Imithei escort caravans, search for the missing, reinforce bridges and riverbanks, clear ruins, and mediate wherever old grievances threaten new violence.

## Choosing and oath

The path cannot be inherited, nor can the title be earned through martial glory. Velissa chooses someone who has already shown that they can place another person's safety before their own pride. Her chosen include men and women, farmers, artisans, hunters and former soldiers. Skill with a weapon is useful, but cannot replace patience and compassion.

Initiation takes place upon open ground. The future Guardian lays aside shoes and weapons, presses both palms to the soil and vows to protect without turning protection into a right to command. When the goddess answers, crystals and luminous runes may emerge upon the chosen person's skin, clothing or equipment—different for each Guardian, like veins within stone.

## Abilities

Guardians can sense movement and hollows beneath the ground, strengthen stone, hold a collapsing slope and seal dangerous objects within a dense shell. Their bodies possess immense strength and endurance, especially when shielding another person or defending familiar soil. An experienced Guardian may take upon a shield a blow that would break a city gate, or become almost immovable for a few moments, as though rooted like a boulder.

The gift does not make them masters of all the earth. The further a Guardian's deed strays from defence and approaches a hunger for power, the harder it becomes for the ground to answer. They therefore fear not weakness, but the certainty that divine strength makes every one of their decisions righteous.

## The power of love

A Guardian's bond with the earth is nourished by the bonds for which they stand in defence: love of a spouse, family, friends or an entire community. Some wear beneath their armour a rune embroidered by a beloved. Others keep a vow of virginity and offer the goddess a love not yet shared. The two paths are equal. Magic answers not to the state of the body, but to the honesty of the vow.

Their best-known battle enchantment is called the ‘unraised weapon’. When an enemy prepares to strike a Guardian, luminous runes awaken either the memory of someone they love or a blinding tenderness and attraction towards the defender. For several moments, the attacker cannot raise their weapon. The enchantment acts upon people of any sex, but may fail against a being incapable of attachment or a will sustained by other magic.

Guardians also carve paired runes upon shields, strengthening the courage of companions who defend one another. Yet seduction used for humiliation, violence or private advantage profanes the oath and weakens the bond with Velissa. Guardians are therefore taught to wield enchantment only to end a battle, preserve a life or shield another person.

## Attributes

Guardians carry round wooden shields bound in metal and set with Velissa's crystals. A heavy hammer serves both as weapon and tool: it may break down a tyrant's gate, drive the pile of a new bridge or clear stone from a fallen home. The red cloak recalls the blood shed in Ivar's war, while white clothing represents the peace for which a Guardian takes up arms at all.

Embroidery from the Guardian's own household and a woven headband are often worn over the armour. To the Wends, these matter more than a uniform. A Guardian must remember that they defend not an abstract realm, but countless particular hearths, fields and people.
