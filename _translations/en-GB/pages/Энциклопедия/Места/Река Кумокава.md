# Kumokawa River

![[Assets/Images/Kumokawa_River.png]]

## Main text

The **Kumokawa** is a river of [[Амато|Amato]] in the southern part of [[Туманный остров|Mist Island]]. It flows out of [[Озеро Шинда|Lake Shinda]] beside [[Город Касумито|Kasumito]], runs south and enters the [[Закатное море|Sunset Sea]] at [[Деревня Аманохама|Amanohama]].

Its gentle current allows small boats to travel from the sea mouth to the lake. The river is a familiar route into the island's interior, connecting Amanohama with Kasumito and the paths to hunting and logging grounds in the [[Дымовая Чаща|Smoke Thicket]].

Timber felled in the southern thicket and game taken by hunters are carried along the river. Settlements throughout the south of the island need building timber; onward deliveries to [[Город Рэйкацу|Reikatsu]] and [[Город Кири|Kiri]] travel by cart or sea.

Together with Lake Shinda, the Kumokawa feeds irrigation canals that supply the surrounding fields of the [[Луга Тамано|Tamano Meadows]].

## Navigation and weather

Boat crews keep close to the familiar bank and navigate by marker poles. When thick fog conceals even these landmarks, they stop travelling and wait at the nearest landing. No one sets out in such fog.

River stopping places have sheltered landings where travellers and boat crews can warm themselves, drink hot tea and dry their clothes. Here they wait out bad weather in the company of other travellers before continuing their journey.

Winters in southern Mist Island are damp and maritime, with rain, sleet and strong winds. The Kumokawa remains largely free of ice. Boats continue to travel in winter when the weather permits; in bad conditions, journeys are suspended until the weather improves.

## River cuisine

The Kumokawa's eating houses are famous for [[Касуми-дон|Kasumi-don]] (霞丼, かすみどん), the ‘bowl of mist’: river fish grilled over charcoal and served on hot rice beneath a thick mushroom gravy. Its name recalls the steam rising from hot rice and the island's mists. Visitors come from elsewhere to taste it, and local cooks maintain a friendly rivalry over the grilling of the fish and the preparation of the sauce.

In [[Деревня Аманохама|Amanohama]], Kasumi-don is simple, home-style fare. Those seeking especially skilful cooking and refined presentation are advised to travel north along the river to [[Город Касумито|Kasumito]], renowned for its refined version of the dish.

Proprietors praise their fish and gravy, while boat crews champion their favourite cooks and recommend them to travellers. Once a year, the cooks gather for a river cooking competition with a communal tasting: residents and visitors sample Kasumi-don from different eating houses and choose the best ‘bowl of mist’.

## Forest rumours

In the river settlements, people tell stories of [[Нурари-химэ|Nurari-hime]], a young ‘Queen of Demons’ supposedly seen from afar surrounded by [[Они|oni]]. Her identity is unknown. Some residents believe the unsettling rumours; others regard them as another tall tale, recalling that stories of forest and mountain demons have often been explained by encounters with wolves, maenads and werewolves, or by the sounds of the wind.

One account passes from mouth to mouth: a hunter escaped from the possessed. According to him, the oni abandoned their pursuit after a girl appeared. She is now called Nurari-hime.

## The search for the princess

After [[Аматсу Ами|Amatsu Ami]] disappeared, boat crews were questioned and passengers checked at river and lake landings. These checks have already been completed, but they yielded no information about the princess's whereabouts.

Small armed search parties operate along the banks and forest paths. Advancing into the thicket is dangerous because of [[Менада|maenads]] and [[Они|oni]], while the approaching winter makes the search still more difficult.

A reward has been offered for reliable information about Ami. It attracts both willing helpers and people hoping for easy money.
