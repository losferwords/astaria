# Chapter 099 — The Price of Reprieve

*[[Сурадж Ка Гхар|Suraj Ka Ghar]], 106 NE, autumn*

The waterfall rumbled beyond the stone wall. Its roar filled the pauses between words; in the underground hall, it was hard to tell how much time had passed above. Lamplight fell across [[Киран Варма|Kiran Varma's]] red robes, the edge of the table and the scrolls the mentor had unrolled before [[Гилья Кангарлавехотри|Gilya]]. He was still offering them his records to examine.

Gilya bent over the paper. Through the unfamiliar handwriting, the work of a healer gradually emerged: days of fever, the patient's condition, the amount of blood taken. Columns of figures stood beside names. Several bore the same note: ‘transferred to the wards for continued care’.

Gilya remembered the wards. That morning, he had woken in one of them beside [[Бату Эклунд|Batu]], among scattered spices and the remains of a drunken rite. Now the names on the paper matched the people he had seen in the beds: old men, a woman, patients with bandaged elbows. Only the dark-haired boy who had lain feverish in the cell opposite was missing.

The further Gilya read, the clearer the course of treatment became. But he could not accept these records as evidence of the Great Harvest. He reminded Kiran of the words of the rite, then of [[Шубханкари|Shubhankari's]] own words. The goddess had said there was no blood. The Cycle was not turning. The mentor could record as many vials as he pleased; Shubhankari's answer would remain the same.

Kiran no longer insisted that his reckoning was beyond fault. He had hoped the blood still reached the goddess. He had compromised knowingly: taking what was due while leaving people alive. Now he had to allow that the Harvest had stood still all those years. For the transition to Creation, he said, everyone who had ever held Shubhankari's raktapatras would be needed — faithful servants and those who had turned from their duty alike.

At Dadu's name, the conversation grew heavier still. According to the mentor, the old man was neither a [[Ракша|Raksha]] nor even a Rajati. His predictions had come true far too precisely; Kiran had once wanted to kill him, but had not done so. He now believed the old man had deceived him.

The travellers heard this without much reverence for the stranger's gift. After Lang-An, the village and the dragon, anyone good at gathering rumours might have guessed they were coming. Something else mattered more: Kiran named Dadu as the head of the [[Чёрный Лотос|Black Lotus]]. The old man had already left [[Город Ванпур|Vanpur]] with the network's people. The mentor had followed them through the jungle, tried to hold their trail, and lost it.

Kiran's gaze lingered on Gilya's arm. Or rather, on the place where his arm should have been. The hall showed through it; the invisibility had already reached his chest.

‘Did you close the circle at the statue?’

Kiran recognised the signs of the rite at once. Gilya had to return to the events of the drunken night, while [[Урист Китин|Urist]] had to explain how he had restored his buttocks that morning.

The blood from Gilya's broken nose had indeed helped. But while Kiran prepared the counter-rite, that hard-won wellbeing began to disappear again. Urist wanted more blood. The vial nearby held his attention far more strongly than scrolls or temple arguments.

Kiran stopped him. Another portion might briefly restore his flesh, but the hunger would grow worse. The morning's experiment had already shown Urist how easily the first mouthful could lead to wanting a second.

For the rite, they took the blood of [[Рон Эллиот|Ron Elliot]], which Gilya had kept with him. The mentor attended to Urist first. The vanishing flesh regained its outline and stayed in place. This time, as it returned, the disappearance ended in one man and began in another: Kiran lost the same part of his body that he had just restored to the Avgar.

He explained that he had taken the transformation upon himself. According to him, the completed transfer could no longer be reversed. He had made the choice himself. Until the goddess returned, he would become a speechless [[Ветал|vetala]].

The mentor could still speak, give instructions and offer medical advice. He forbade Urist to eat anything containing blood for the next couple of days: meat, game or raw fish. Nor should he test the new treatment with another mouthful.

Gilya still had the emptiness beneath his sleeve. Kiran had not finished helping them. Already beginning to vanish himself, he continued answering questions.

What he knew of the black stone came from the Lotus. Genuine needles put a person to sleep. Ordinary obsidian looked much the same, and [[Вирадж Сомани|Viraj Somani]] had turned that resemblance into a profitable trade: ordering stone from the north, chipping it into pieces and selling the shards for the price of a genuine injection. When the Lotus discovered the counterfeit, Viraj had to flee.

The travellers remembered the abandoned house, the crates in the cellar and [[Мерката|Mercate]], whom they had summoned there because of the black shards. The cache's contents were beginning to make sense. Waking someone from a genuine needle remained as much a mystery as before.

Kiran had seen patients die of [[Ракта-Доша|Rakta-Dosha]] before the black injections appeared. He remembered the last days of fever, when there was no treatment left to prescribe. So, when he gained a means of halting the dying, he began carrying sleepers into the hidden cells. He took blood, hoping to give it to the goddess, and waited for a cure.

The people in the beds went on breathing. Their names remained in the scrolls. The mentor did not know how to wake them.

[[Самир Тхакур|Samir]] had made no progress towards that answer either. Dadu seemed to know more, but Kiran had obtained nothing from him. According to the mentor, the Lotus profited from fear of the disease and cared nothing for awakening. They decided to discuss the stones with Velimir as well.

There was still the chest in the temple guest house. The black blade the size of a raktakatara, the Lotus rings, the small-share bowls — all had lain beneath a false bottom in Kiran's room. The mentor confirmed that he had slept there, but knew nothing of the cache. More than five years earlier, he had arrived from the capital, been given a cell and asked no questions about what the previous occupants had done there. The Lotus, he believed, had been there before him.

His predecessor's name was familiar: [[Савитри Эклунд|Savitri]]. Batu's rescued wife had once lived in that very house. The travellers were already preparing to leave Vanpur, and another question had just been added to their conversation with the leatherworker.

Kiran was speaking of the road too. He believed that Shubhankari's will would draw the vetalas to the Flesh Forge: that was where the Cycle would turn. He intended to send Samir there as well. The physician had not overcome the disease and no longer performed the Harvest; the mentor saw no reason for him to remain in the city. While the Heroes passed through [[Тхаги|Thuggee]] lands, they meant to leave Samir needles and instructions to continue giving the dying sleep instead of death, then set out for the Forge. In a few weeks, they all expected to be in [[Город Сангалла|Sangalla]]. That decision would leave Vanpur without either Raksha.

The scrolls still lay on the table, and the argument returned to blood. Urist remembered the goddess drawing an entire wave of it into herself. Kiran now sought the mistake elsewhere: Shubhankari might also need the soul, and a sleeper's soul remained in the body. That was his explanation for why his Harvest had brought Creation no closer.

Gilya repeated the order of the rite. Neither healer nor patient appointed the sacrifice. The choice belonged to the goddess; the Raksha carried out her will. Kiran, in turn, offered a different explanation even for Shubhankari's earlier appearance after [[Мэй Ву|Mei's]] blood. In his view, a starving goddess had tried to take form after several years without offerings; to call that a completed Harvest had been a mistake.

The voices took turns, while the rumble of water remained unchanged. No agreement came. Kiran had already paid with his own flesh to help Urist, and was still arguing with Gilya over what ought to be given to the goddess.

[[Томас Робинсон|Tom]] listened like a man shown the middle of someone else's story. Names and old events ought to have meant something, but memory left too many gaps. They reminded him of his scar, of being rescued from inside a worm, of the road the Heroes of the Dragon had travelled. He could not have taken it alone. Mei, who remained beside them, confirmed it: all of this had really happened to them.

Behind the stories, familiar faces slowly returned. Urist, Gilya and Mei became his companions again, rather than people claiming to have known him once. Details slipped away; the memory of his friends returned before the memory of everything they had endured together.

Before they parted, Kiran offered to finish helping Gilya. The vial of Ron's blood had already been used up. For the second rite, they took the blood they had kept from [[Артур Бомейн|Arthur Beaumain]].

Gilya saw his own arm again. Skin emerged where the wall had shown through moments before. The invisibility retreated from his chest, and the Black Mark faded from the returning flesh. Meanwhile, Kiran's own arm was disappearing.

The mentor had accepted this transformation as well. He expected to meet them at the Flesh Forge — if he could still recognise them by then. Gilya was left with his restored body and an old curse unexpectedly gone. What had happened to the mark would have to wait until they could ask Velimir.

A dark-haired boy appeared in the passage behind the statue. He came from the familiar cells, and one look at his face was enough to recall the bed opposite. The fever had not passed. Illness had worn him down, but his first question was about [[Тара Саркар|Tara]] — was she here, had she come with them?

He was [[Вивек Ашма|Vivek]], the seventeen-year-old son of the jeweller [[Джевахар Ашма|Djevahar Ashma]]. His father had asked the travellers to keep him alive. Now the boy stood before them, and his [[Ракта-Доша|Rakta-Dosha]] had already reached its final stage. There was a simple reason his name had been missing from the list of sleepers: he had never received a black injection. Until now he had lain ill in the cell, and had only just woken.

Vivek still had time to speak of Tara and how much he loved her. Helping him could not wait.

Samir had spoken before about replacing blood. A complete replacement with compatible blood might, he hoped, free someone from the disease; a smaller amount merely bought time until another attack. Kiran immediately ruled out Gilya's and Urist's blood. In his judgement, it was still too close to vetala blood after the rites they had just undergone.

Kiran turned to Tom.

They mixed a sample with the boy's blood. They waited for a precipitate that would mean this way was closed. The blood remained uniform. It had been the same when compared with the queen's blood; now Tom could help a Vanpur jeweller's son as well.

For the duration of the transfusion, temple disagreements receded. The medical work required attention to a living person who was growing worse. Tom gave his blood. After the procedure, Vivek's fever began to subside; the boy survived the crisis.

No one could promise that the disease would not return. But he could give thanks again, make plans and promise gifts. His father would make the finest piece of jewellery for Tom — Vivek was certain of that. Now he called them blood brothers. The transfusion created no actual kinship, but for the boy it was reason enough to count his rescuer as one of his own.

When they told him about Tara, Vivek could not understand why the Lotus had put its own messenger to sleep. He knew of her work for the network. Now she lay in Samir's hospital and could answer neither him nor anyone else.

Kiran repeated that the Lotus knew how to sell hope. They had never learnt to wake people.

The boy, however, knew what the mentor had failed to discover in the jungle. Tara had spoken to him of [[Город Шантинат|Shantinat]], a city on the road to Sangalla: the Lotus intended to make camp there. The lost trail had a direction once more.

Vivek wanted to go with the travellers. He knew how to carry instructions; he could be a messenger. After the transfusion, he wanted to repay his rescuers and do something for Tara himself.

‘Rest for a day,’ Gilya told him. ‘We’ll finish our business and go to Nagrakshasa. You can help us there.’

For now, the boy could visit Tara and stay beside the sleeper. Kiran remained to watch over him and give him something to drink; afterwards, he meant to go to Samir and then on to the Flesh Forge.

The Second Brew smelt of tea and hot spices. Here the noise was different: crockery clinked at the counter, and customers talked at their tables. [[Амрит Наир|Amrit Nair]] was already waiting for the travellers. The caravan would leave from the western gates at sunrise — four horses and a load of goods. Their agreement still stood; they could go with him.

Amrit intended to set off from the teahouse. Asked about the bearded northerner and the horned girl, he pointed upstairs: both were in their room again.

A bucket stood in [[Велимир Сажин|Velimir]] and [[Лиарин Ветреная|Liarin's]] room. Sliced pieces of blue [[Гриб Нилачхатра|nilachhatra]] dropped into it. Both had dilated pupils, but this time the conversation required no slaps and no long wait for words to begin forming sentences.

After the cave, they had spent several days eating mushrooms without restraint and had finally found the limit. Now they were drying a supply for the road: the dried ones were less potent. Over this task, it emerged that they would be travelling separately from here. Velimir and Liarin had chosen the main road to Sangalla; the Heroes meant to make a detour through Thuggee lands.

[[Чори Марджари|Chori]] had visited them the evening before. She had offered to guide the foreigners to the capital. Velimir knew that her father had fallen asleep in the village, and understood why she too needed a cure. The Guardian himself continued his search for [[Велисса|Velissa's]] [[Чёрное Сердце|Black Heart]]. They agreed to meet again in Sangalla.

The Heroes told them about the black stones. Velimir had already seen similar shards and still did not consider them the relic he sought: a resemblance in appearance and properties offered no certainty. Then the conversation turned to needles obtained from the blood of sick elephants.

Liarin immediately brightened. Elephants still lived in the jungle, but encountering them was about as likely as finding a wolf in a city park. Yet the travellers had seen a herd approached by traders with a cart. If the animals' disease and the black shards were connected to the goddess's crystal, there were places to search beyond the city's rumours. On the journey with Chori, Velimir intended to venture deeper into the jungle, listen to the earth and look for traces. For now, it remained a conjecture worth leaving the road to pursue.

Gilya showed him his restored arm. Velimir could no longer sense the old punishment upon him, and explained what the Black Mark had been. The goddess of love's curse forbade carnal pleasures on pain of death. In his judgement, Shubhankari's rite had carried it away with the vanishing flesh; when the body returned, the sign had not followed.

Urist had been less fortunate. The rune remained on him. He explained that he had received it from [[Вигдис Меткая|Vigdis the Unerring]], rather than from the goddess herself. Vigdis was about forty-six and had no intention of seeking a cure. As Urist explained it, the curse troubled those who wanted a family, and she did not.

Velimir had not seen anything like it outside books for a long time. Urist advised him, the next time he spoke to the goddess, to ask her to replace the punishment with something less idiotic.

Amid talk of the capital and stones, their plan for [[Город Награкшаса|Nagrakshasa]] took shape. There they hoped to sell the box of red crystal — dragon blood they were still carrying — and make sense of the evidence they had gathered. The Thuggee archives might hold records about Sangalla, the Flesh Forge and the unending sleep. Whoever could read them would have to do the reading.

They discussed Vivek too, though they were not yet prepared to trust him completely. If he ran off, he could at least carry a message to Velimir. If he stayed with the Thuggee, he might eventually become an apprentice, giving the Heroes a contact among the keepers of records. They were already finding uses for the boy years into the future.

They spent the night at the teahouse. In the morning, Nadila handed out dry provisions: dried meat and flatbreads, all generously seasoned with hot spices. They ate breakfast near Amrit, each at their own table. The guide left first and arranged to meet them at the gates in an hour. Their paid lodging had not yet run out, but they were already packing for the road.

Before they left, [[Викрам Саркар|Vikram]], the familiar scribe with the missing teeth, came running. His master had sent him to search for his missing daughter.

The travellers explained that Tara was in the hospital. She was alive; her sleep was long, but they had no intention of calling it eternal. The Lotus members who had put her to sleep had already left the city. Djevahar's son remained beside her; without further ceremony, they called him her bridegroom. Vikram was told to visit the hospital and keep an eye on their farewell. They added a joke that the seventeen-year-old should take no liberties with the sleeping girl.

Four laden horses waited at the western gates. The caravan left Vanpur the morning after the conversation with Vivek. The day's rest promised to the boy was not yet over. Before departing, the travellers never remembered that they had told him to wait. He remained in the city, recovering from the transfusion and tending to Tara.

Towards evening, one of the horses shied sideways. A driver clutched his throat. Blood poured between his fingers.

Beside him, the outline of a body began to emerge. The blood made visible what had been almost impossible to discern: an adult male vetala, as tall and heavily built as Urist. Two more predators were already springing at the Avgar and Tom.

Gilya drew his raktapatra. Shouting over the attack, he cried that the Harvest was over, that no more blood was needed, and waved towards the capital. The vetala went straight onto the blade. The weapon pierced its body, but the creature seized the Raksha's arm and held it fast along with the weapon.

Urist and Tom were thrown to the ground. Caught in his attacker's grip, Urist reached for its throat, clenched his hands around it and began to strangle it.
