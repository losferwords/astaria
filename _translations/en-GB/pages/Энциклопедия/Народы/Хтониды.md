# Chthonids

![[Assets/Images/Chthtonid.jpg]]

The **Chthonids** are an ancient and nearly vanished people whose island civilisation was destroyed by the [[Профитис|Profitis]] during the [[Падение Хтона|Fall of Chthon]]. Before the catastrophe, their cities were centres of learning: stars were observed, maps drawn, archives kept across centuries and the workings of the world investigated. Among their kindred Ellians and Thalassians, the Chthonids were regarded as the most refined. Beauty of speech, clothing, home and thought itself was not a luxury to them, but evidence of inner order. The Chthonids became ancestors to several younger peoples, including the [[Эллийцы|Ellians]] and [[Талассийцы|Thalassians]].

The few survivors lost not only a homeland, but their accustomed understanding of their own purpose. Everything in which they had once taken pride—knowledge, prophecy and the ability to see beyond the evident—became a reminder of Chthon's destruction. Most Chthonids now live in [[Катахтонос|Katachthonos]], where open study of magic inspires fear and the name of the ancient god [[Тиресий|Tiresias]] is seldom spoken without need.

Modern Chthonids value quiet labour, family memory and the making of things whose use is plain to everyone. Farmers, sailors, copyists and merchants are common among them. Even a learned person will often emphasise the practical side of their work, lest curiosity be mistaken for a dangerous obsession with mysteries. Yet the taste of their ancestors survives in graceful calligraphy, delicate jewellery, a love of deep philosophical conversation and the custom of arranging a home so that even a modest object has true proportions and a place of its own.

## Clothing

Their clothing retains echoes of island culture: long togas, loose gowns and hooded mantles. Ancient Chthonids favoured flowing silhouettes, fine cloth, intricate embroidery and jewellery in which geometric beauty mattered more than the size of a stone. Modern dress is usually humbler, but even a poor family tries to preserve a clean fall of fabric and careful finish. Garments that survived Chthon are rarely worn in daily life; they pass from hand to hand as proof that a lineage existed before the New Era began.

The Chthonids live with a heavy question to which no generation shares one answer: can respect for knowledge be restored without repeating the arrogance of their ancestors? Elders more often choose caution and silence, while the young try to study history without worshipping it. From that dispute, a new culture is gradually emerging—no longer imperial, yet still mindful of how magnificent and terrible its past once was.
