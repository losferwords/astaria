# Sigrid Drakendottir

![[Assets/Images/Sigrid_Drakendottir.jpg]]

## Main text

**Sigrid Drakendottir** is the reigning queen of [[Вактар-Йорден|Vaktar-Jorden]], a warrior and one of [[Винтра|Wintra's]] [[Вознесённый|Ascended]]. Her reign is young, but long before she took the throne Sigrid was known for sea voyages, raids and a habit of being the first to set foot upon a dangerous shore.

Sigrid's father, King [[Фрости Дракенсон|Frosti Drakenson]], raised his daughter alone from the day of her birth. No one knows her mother's name, although the [[Вактары|Vaktars]] whisper that she was a [[Талассийцы|Thalassian]] sailor. Sigrid's detractors therefore call her a half-blood and no true northerner. Few dare say so openly: she joined her father's raids from an early age and knows how to answer an insult with steel.

In 104 NE, Sigrid fought in the bloody struggle to overthrow [[Ивар Свирепый|Ivar the Fierce]] from the throne of [[Обитель|Obitelj]]. Frosti succumbed to his wounds and, having once before been numbered among the Ascended, departed life for ever. Wintra herself came for Sigrid upon the battlefield and bore her away to [[Храм Винтерхейм|Winterheim]].

Several months later, Sigrid returned to [[Город Трелла|Trella]] as an Einherjar. By Wintra's will, she inherited her father's throne. The divine choice ended the open dispute over the succession, but did not silence every doubt: the old houses watch closely to see whether the young queen will become a sovereign in her own right or merely continue Frosti's unfinished wars.

The new queen of Obitelj, a girl named [[Мафка|Mavka]], holds Sigrid in great esteem and calls her an elder sister, although the two share no blood.

Sigrid answers the child's affection with unexpected tenderness. She does not intrude upon Obitelj's affairs without invitation, but regards Mavka's safety as a personal duty. Their bond has become a firmer link between the two realms than many formal treaties: the Wends remember the northerners' aid, while the Vaktars see in the young queen the person for whom their former king gave his life.

Sigrid is less certain of herself in peace than in battle. She must settle quarrels between ports, apportion winter stores and listen to counsellors who cannot be defeated by a well-cast axe. The queen is learning to accept this work as another kind of struggle—slow, thankless and necessary if she truly wishes to protect her people rather than merely glorify her own name.
