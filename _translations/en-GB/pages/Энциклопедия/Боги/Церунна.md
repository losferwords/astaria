# Cerunna

## Main text

Cerunna is the chief goddess of [[Иомар|Iomar]], patron of life, fertility and the natural renewal of the world. Together with [[Тарун|Tarun]] and [[Мерката|Mercate]], she forms the great triad of the ancient Dair pantheon. Among the [[Надаир|Nadair]], however, Cerunna holds a place apart: she is no distant sovereign, but the very power of the earth to bring forth life after winter, fire or human folly.

Tradition names Cerunna as the first deity to meet the Dair settlers of central [[Кельн|Keln]]. Having made their homes upon the [[Даирские луга|Dair Meadows]], the newcomers ventured deeper into [[Лес Иомар|Iomar Forest]] and reached [[Озеро Сильвиан|Lake Silvian]]. There a tall woman appeared before them, with a pair of slender deer-like legs, cloven hooves, red hair and branching antlers. She taught them to see the forest not as a store of timber and game, but as a living whole in which every life taken must be repaid with care.

Cerunna's first disciples became [[Друид|Druids]]. In time, their teaching formed the foundation of the [[Круг Друидов|Druid Circle]] and of Iomar itself. The goddess does not choose her servants for ancestry or family rank. Her notice is said to fall upon those who can hear the pain of a wounded animal, recognise sickness in a tree before its leaves wither and refuse an easy answer for the sake of the land's future.

Many temples and open-air altars have been raised in the goddess's honour. Stone walls seldom divide them from nature: a spring, a circle of ancient trees or a glade to which the same herd returns every spring may all become sacred ground. [[Храм Ишнаалар|Ishnalaar]], deep within Iomar Forest, is considered the greatest centre of her worship.

Cerunna appears comparatively often among the Nadair as she walks her domain. Her arrival is not always miraculous or solemn. A traveller may notice only the track of an immense deer, flowers blooming out of season or a red-haired stranger binding the wound of an animal beside the road. In Iomar, meeting the goddess does not make a person chosen; it merely tests whether they can show mercy when no reward has been promised.
