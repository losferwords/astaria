# Amanohama

![[Assets/Images/Amanohama_Village.png]]

## Main text

**Amanohama** is a village of [[Амато|Amato]] on the coast of [[Туманный остров|Mist Island]], at the mouth of the [[Река Кумокава|Kumokawa]], where it enters the [[Закатное море|Sunset Sea]]. From here, small boats travel upriver to [[Город Касумито|Kasumito]] and [[Озеро Шинда|Lake Shinda]].

The houses of Amanohama stand in close groups around courtyards sheltered from the wind. Narrow passages lead down to the water between these clusters of buildings.

## Fishing and crafts

Fishing is the foundation of village life. The inhabitants fish both at sea and on the river, dry their nets and preserve their catch. Alongside their fishing, they tend to everyday household needs.

River transport and the transfer of goods between the coast and the lake provide additional income. Some villagers carry goods along the Kumokawa to Kasumito and back.

Timber from the [[Дымовая Чаща|Smoke Thicket]] comes downriver to the mouth. Here, loads of timber are transferred for onward transport by sea to coastal towns in the south of the island.

Net makers weave and repair nets, while rope makers produce cordage for fishing tackle and boats. The flax they use is brought from the [[Луга Тамано|Tamano Meadows]]. Nets and coils of cord hang beneath awnings; villagers come here with torn gear and orders to be filled before their next trip onto the water.

Coopers make tubs, buckets and small barrels for fish, household stores and the transport of goods. Basket weavers supply their neighbours with catch baskets, mats and woven fish traps.

Wood smoke rises above the smokehouses. Preserved fish is kept for household supplies, sold and taken on river journeys. The work of the fish smokers connects the fishers with boat crews and the craftspeople whose containers are needed to store and transport the catch.

## The riverside yard

Amanohama's main meeting place is a riverside yard with an inn and a tea house. Visitors stay the night, boat crews eat their meals, and people gather while waiting for better weather or a boat travelling their way. Over food, they discuss the catch, transport arrangements and news from the lake.

A covered landing offers shelter from bad weather. At the riverside yard, travellers can warm themselves, drink hot tea and dry their clothes before continuing their journey.

Here, a traveller can find a guide to the Kumokawa, arrange a place in a boat and learn who has recently returned from Kasumito or from hunting and logging in the forest. News arrives with the river crews and passes from table to table.

## River cuisine

Amanohama's eating houses serve **[[Касуми-дон|Kasumi-don]]** (霞丼, かすみどん), the ‘bowl of mist’: river fish grilled over charcoal and served on hot rice beneath a thick mushroom gravy. Here it is simple, home-style fare, served to boat crews and travellers at the riverside tea house among other places.

For especially skilful cooking and refined presentation, proprietors and river crews recommend travelling north along the Kumokawa to [[Город Касумито|Kasumito]]. The town is renowned for its refined version of the dish.

Proprietors maintain a friendly rivalry with their neighbours over the best grilling of the fish and preparation of the gravy. Boat crews champion their favourite cooks and recommend their dishes to visitors. Local cooks take part in an annual river cooking competition with a communal tasting, where residents and guests choose the best ‘bowl of mist’.

## Unsettling news

Reports of people disappearing in the [[Дымовая Чаща|thicket]] and the [[Призрачные горы|mountains]] have become especially frightening to the villagers since a much-loved boatman went missing. His boat was found without him; gruesome details of the discovery are discussed at the riverside yard and in neighbouring homes. There is no clear explanation for what happened.

Parents keep their children at home. In the evenings, people try to gather with relatives and friends so that they can spend time together and avoid being alone. This one disappearance was enough to fill familiar life beside the river with unease.
