# Raksha

Those who take the path of the Raksha choose a destiny painted in the crimson shades of life and death. In the common tongue they are often called Reapers, but in [[Сурадж Ка Гхар|Suraj Ka Ghar]] they remain the Raksha: heirs to an ancient tradition of the [[Раджати|Rajati]] and faithful servants of the goddess [[Шубханкари|Shubhankari]].

Their lives move with the Goddess's Great Cycle. One breath is filled with music and the feast of life; the next, with blood and destruction.

In Suraj Ka Ghar, the Raksha drink a [[Настой красного корня|red-root infusion]] in hope of seeing Shubhankari or hearing her voice. Such encounters are always indistinct and easily confused with hallucination. The draught opens no road to the gods by itself: without a singular bond to the patron, a supplicant receives only fever, intoxication and disquieting visions.

## Cycle of Creation

During the Cycle of Creation, the Raksha become teachers and healers, wise counsellors and protectors. Their dances kindle the heart, their hands raise temples and their songs inspire whole peoples.

In this season, service may appear almost peaceful: tending children, the sick and the old; giving aid; teaching; practising temple medicine; and preserving the life of the community.

## Cycle of Destruction

During the Cycle of Destruction, the Raksha become incarnations of the Great Harvest. Every step is then the sweep of a blade severing another fate for the sake of renewal yet to come.

Shubhankari's blessing bears two faces. These Imithei carry both the joyous feast of life's celebration and an all-consuming thirst for blood. The unfortunate few who have witnessed a Raksha in battle speak of slaughter so dreadful that the hair rises upon the skin and blood runs like a river.

The long Cycle of Destruction has revealed the fragility of the tradition. When some Raksha ceased to perform the Harvest and foreign rites began diverting blood from the goddess, their service entered a crisis. To restore Shubhankari's stolen strength and set the order of the Cycle aright, the Raksha must gather at the [[Кузница Плоти|Furnace of Flesh]].

## Art of the Raksha

The Raksha are masters of close combat, joining dance, ritual warfare and blood magic. Their red tattoos are no mere ornament, but sacred signs through which the currents of life may be governed.

Every movement belongs to a rite. The Raksha cuts, offers sacrifice, drinks the enemy's vitality and turns that stolen life against its former owner.

## Attributes

The symbol of the Raksha is a pair of Raktakatar blades, born through the will of the goddess herself. Every novice faces an arduous trial. One of Shubhankari's six legendary blades—the [[Рактапатр|Raktapatra]]—is set into the initiate's hand and will not release its grasp. It demands sacrifice, lengthening with every drop of blood until it reaches its limit. The Raksha then breaks the blade and preserves the fragment for a future weapon.

Only after gathering two such fragments and seeking a raktarajit smith does the initiate receive a personal pair of Raktakatars. The weapons are unique and bound to their bearer for ever: they drink blood, hunger for more and feed the enemy's life not only to their master, but to Shubhankari herself.
