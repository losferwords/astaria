# Suraj Ka Ghar

![[Assets/Images/Crests/suradj_ka_ghar_crest.png]]

![[Assets/Images/Suradj_Ka_Ghar.jpg]]

Suraj Ka Ghar is the ancient kingdom of the [[Раджати|Rajati]] in south-eastern [[Архея|Archaea]]. Its cities rise among humid jungles, red cliffs and broad rivers, while merchant vessels enter [[Багровый залив|Crimson Bay]] by the golden spires of [[Город Сангалла|Sangalla]]. A traveller remembers this land by the scent of spice and incense, the music of bracelets, its brilliant cloth—and the uneasy sense that any celebration might become a sacred execution without warning.

The kingdom's history reaches back thousands of years, although the Rajati preserve it only in fragments. The Cycles of Creation and Destruction belonging to [[Шубханкари|Shubhankari]] have remade the country more thoroughly than any war. Old palaces were dismantled for new temples, chronicles vanished, and families surviving the Harvest began their reckoning anew. The past of Suraj Ka Ghar therefore lives less in dates than in songs, ancestral jewellery, temple dances and ruins swallowed by roots.

## A land of rain and spice

The country's heart lies between the [[Джунгли Шанкари|Shankari Jungle]], the [[Болота Кхали Дарти|Khali Dharti Marshes]] and fertile river valleys. Seasonal downpours turn roads into torrents, but feed the rice fields, orchards and thickets of medicinal plants. Timber, fruit and baskets of spice travel downriver to the coast; salt, metal and the wares of overseas merchants make the return journey inland.

Nature is generous here, but rarely safe. Healers know how to turn snake venom into medicine and a bitter root into a draught capable of holding someone at the threshold of death. The same knowledge can produce a silent poison. Travellers avoid leaving the lit roads after dusk: the coming of the [[Ветал|Vetals]] has made the jungle perilous even for seasoned hunters and [[Имитей|Imithei]].

## Two faces of the goddess

During the Cycle of Creation, Shubhankari is worshipped as patron of birth, music and craft. Cities are dressed in garlands, public squares dance until dawn, and the [[Ракша|Rakshas]] care for the sick, elderly and orphaned. This is a time of building, marriage and plenty, when the Rajati seek to live every joy as fully as though there might be no morning.

The Cycle of Destruction is called the Great Harvest. The goddess demands the return of life's strength, and the Rakshas take up [[Рактапатр|raktakatars]] grown from their own blood. To neighbouring peoples the Harvest remains an atrocity and the cause of ancient distrust. To the Rajati it is a terrible yet inseparable part of the world's order: destruction must make room for new life. Not every inhabitant accepts this faith without doubt, but openly defying the goddess's will requires almost unimaginable courage.

## People, power and daily life

Beyond the temples, life rests upon large families, craft communities and mutual aid between neighbours. Kin tend a household, assemble dowries, raise children and answer for one another's debts together. A guest is fed before being questioned, but trust must be earned: the people are accustomed to a new Cycle overturning yesterday's laws and alliances.

Queen [[Индира Раштра|Indira Rashtra]] rules from her palace in Sangalla. Her power relies upon the courtly houses, the temple and the Rakshas, but her present position is precarious. A prolonged Cycle of Destruction has coincided with the spread of [[Ракта-Доша|Rakta-Dosha]] and the appearance of the Vetals. Disease empties settlements, trade roads grow more dangerous, and the queen herself struggles for the future of both her heir and her country.

Suraj Ka Ghar remains a land of extremes: splendour and terror, medicine and poison, the tenderness of dance and the upraised blade. Those who can look upon both its faces will find, behind the frightening rites, a people who believe desperately that even after the longest night the world may yet be born anew.
