# Tiresias

Tiresias was the central god of the [[Хтониды|Chthonid]] empire and embodied wisdom, prophecy and law. He was portrayed as a stern old man with enormous goat horns, a book and a set of scales. The horns were so vast that temple reliefs raised them above his head like a heavy crown. An open eye was often carved beside the god: a sign of divination, hidden knowledge and the power to perceive the secret causes of events. The scales warned that knowledge without measure may prove as dangerous as ignorance.

No reliable account survives from anyone who saw Tiresias with their own eyes. Nevertheless, in the Chthonic Era his cult extended far beyond [[Остров Хтон|Chthon]]. Temples housed archives, schools devoted to the interpretation of law and gardens where scholars could debate without fear of the authorities. The [[Профитис|Profitis]] were regarded as the god's most gifted servants and occupied an exceptional place in society.

After the [[Падение Хтона|Fall of Chthon]], the name of Tiresias became inseparable from the catastrophe. His priests and followers were exiled, while former worshippers destroyed altars and records once held sacred. The reaction was fiercest among the [[Эллийцы|Ellians]] and [[Талассийцы|Thalassians]], whose cultures descended from the Chthonids. [[Гилас|Gilas]] and [[Талассия|Thalassia]] began to hunt the Profitis who had sacrificed thousands of lives for an incomprehensible prophecy.

Today the worship of Tiresias is forbidden almost everywhere, including [[Катахтонос|Katachthonos]], home to descendants of the surviving Chthonids. A newly discovered image of the open eye may be shattered as the emblem of an accursed cult—or hidden by someone who still believes that the priests, rather than the god, bore the guilt.

Tiresias remains one of the most troubling figures in Astaria's religious history. He embodied the pursuit of truth, yet the memory of his cult leaves a question: can knowledge remain pure when one who sees the future ceases to hold another life beyond price?
