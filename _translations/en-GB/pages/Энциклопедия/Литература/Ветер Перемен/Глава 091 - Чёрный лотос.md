# Chapter 091 — Black Lotus

*[[Сурадж Ка Гхар|Suraj Ka Ghar]], 106 NE, summer*

As Vanpur gradually revealed its markets, fragrances and dangerous alleys, [[Мэй Ву|Mei]] went in search of jewellery. She wanted a gift for [[Ниса|Nisa]], and amongst the inexpensive trinkets she found earrings set with black lotus-shaped stones in gold. They cost little, but as the merchant wrapped them, her husband approached and whispered urgently in her ear. The woman became visibly uneasy despite trying to conceal it from her customer. Mei left with the earrings, not yet knowing that the lotus sign was more than an ornament in this city.

That evening, when the friends returned to the Second Infusion, Nadila gave [[Томас Робинсон|Tom]] one of [[Мерката|Mercate's]] feathers. It came with a note bearing no words, only a drawing of a lotus. The courier had left the parcel with Nadila for “those people” and promised that the remaining two feathers would be returned later.

Tom had no intention of waiting upon an unknown messenger's mercy and set [[Мусака|Musaka]] upon the trail. The dog led the heroes across the tavern's rear yard, past an incense shop, into the market and finally behind the same stall where [[Урист Китин|Urist]] had bargained for the tiger skin. There the trail ended. In a basket they found a scrap of blue thread and a scratched coin bearing a lotus. Their activity swiftly attracted the guard, but a little intimidation settled the matter before it became a true confrontation.

Seeking the coin's meaning, the heroes spoke with the young hide merchant and his father. The boy's father proved to be an [[Авгарцы|Avgar]], one of Urist's own people. Of the coin he knew only this: his wife, Savitri, had disappeared several weeks earlier after taking an identical token to the market. Urist agreed to help, and the grateful merchant promised to refund the price of the tiger skin if she was found. Their plan for the following day was simple: carry the coin through the market and watch for anyone who reacted.

Before returning to the lotus and the missing woman in the morning, the company attended to the affairs of its brothel network. The first problem concerned [[Бордель Халык-Бындыр|Halyk-Byndir]] and the [[Бордель Уй-Джан|Ui-Jan]]: someone was stealing from the former, while expensive goods sent from the latter never arrived. Through [[Чень Ляньши|Chen Lianshi]] and diplomatic channels they learnt that surviving nomads were blackmailing a customs worker and threatening his family in order to harm the heroes. The group wrote to [[Нефела|Nephele]], ruler of [[Город Бахара|Bahara]], asking her to remove the nomads from the affair. With the cold practicality increasingly common in their distant dealings, they hinted that prisoners might serve Bahara more darkly than free men ever could. Nephele's reply was brief: it had already been done, and the nomads would never leave the city's dungeons.

The second difficulty was more peaceful, but no simpler: staff were leaving Halyk-Byndir. Some of its women wished to move to the Ui-Jan for a supposedly better life, though the allure of its famous baths was easy to discern behind their ambitions. To distinguish serious intentions from passing fancy, the friends decided upon an entrance examination covering the Dju language, culture and related subjects. They also planned to add a women's bathhouse to Halyk-Byndir and recoup the cost through a new entertainment founded upon the guests' curiosity—strictly controlled so that curiosity remained part of the performance rather than descending into chaos.

The third nuisance grew from the heroes' own fame. Local Avgars, enthralled by tales of Urist and his friends, began imitating their most questionable exploits: drinking moonshine into oblivion and trying in the morning to discover what legend they had enacted during the night. To keep this devotion from becoming brawls and fires, the most unruly imitators were served moonshine laced with a sleeping draught, then told solemn and entirely invented stories of their nocturnal deeds.

After lunch, the company returned to the lotus. They entered the market carrying the scratched coin, hoping the sign itself would attract someone able to read it. A boy of perhaps twelve with a basket of limes caught their attention: he circled them too long and looked their way too often. When challenged, he ran, but could not escape Musaka. The heroes pretended to know more than they did and pressed him. Terrified, the boy betrayed the Black Lotus contact: [[Вирадж Сомани|Viraj Somani]], owner of a shop selling cheap incense.

Viraj's shop was closed when the heroes arrived. Its simple lock soon yielded. No one was inside, but everything suggested the proprietor had departed in haste. After searching the ground floor, Urist began climbing the stairs, saw that the upper storey was alive with snakes and wisely chose not to disturb them.

The ground floor proved far more rewarding. Beneath a carpet lay a concealed hatch. In the cellar, the heroes found many trays of black crystals—the same dangerous stones they had encountered before. They could not carry everything, yet leaving the cache unattended seemed too great a risk: by the time they returned, it might vanish as swiftly as Viraj himself.

Urist used the [[Чёрный Вестник|Black Harbinger]] to contact Mercate, taking care not to reveal too much in case someone listened. [[Мерката|Mercate]] reached Tom telepathically. The description astonished the Goddess of Night: there should not have been so many stones, and someone was plainly cultivating them. She advised the travellers to take what they could and await her in several days, when she would determine whether the crystals were genuine and whence they had come.

Knowing that the slightest scratch from such a crystal might prove fatal, Urist built a sealed chest from the shop's furniture. Five trays fitted inside. Carrying this perilous prize, the group went in search of another expert upon crystals, [[Велимир Сажин|Velimir]]. They dared not bring such a burden back to the tavern.
