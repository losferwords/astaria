# Wends

![[Assets/Images/Wends.jpg]]

## Main text

The **Wends** are a people of eastern [[Кельн|Keln]], descended from [[Вактары|Vaktars]] who left their homeland and settled among the rivers, forests and broad plains of what would become [[Обитель|Obitelj]]. From their northern ancestors they inherited sturdy frames, a love of songs beside the fire and the runes carved into weapons, ornaments and the beams of a family home. Under the protection of [[Велисса|Velissa]], however, martial severity slowly gave way to another kind of strength: the ability to cherish land, family and one another.

## From hatred to love

The Wends reckon their separate history from the first encounter with Velissa. At first, the Vaktars knew her only as the forest maiden of the [[Девичьи Холмы|Maiden Hills]], whose care and beauty caused hunters to abandon their families and search for her again. Frightened and angered, the women tried to lead their people east towards [[Сребролесье|Silverwood]], but men continued to fall beneath the goddess's influence there. At the same time, the country around the settlers grew ever more generous.

The women who went to the [[Река Красивая|Krasivaya River]] to avenge their broken marriages fell in love with Velissa themselves. Yet they managed to speak to her and learned that the goddess meant no harm: her gift was a curse to her as well. Peace was born from that conversation. The first Beregini became her voice and veil, while her blessings gave the settlers rich harvests, healthy children and magic to protect the home. Thus the Vaktar settlers gradually became the Wends.

## Home and community

Obitelj is seldom gentle with its children. A short summer must provide enough grain and firewood to carry every household through the long frost. Men and women work the fields, hunt, raise homes and defend their settlements together. The spring thaw is therefore welcomed as the world's return from oblivion: doors are thrown open, whole households step into the sunlight, wreaths are set upon the water, and the first fresh bread is shared among neighbours.

To a Wend, family means more than blood. A person is remembered for the care they gave their loved ones, the promises they kept and the travellers they welcomed to their hearth. Even a poor host will try to offer hot food, dry clothing and a place by the fire. Loyalty is not submission, but the daily choice to remain when winter, sickness or war tests a home to breaking.

An orphan or solitary elder is seldom left to inhabit an empty house. They are taken in by kin, neighbours or a family with whom they have long shared work and bread. Such bonds are not considered lesser. What matters is not the name of a common ancestor, but the place kept at the table and the duties willingly shared.

## Work, dress and song

Wends favour linen, heavy wool and fur, decorating the hems of shirts, cloaks and woven headbands with family signs. Beauty is found in health, an open gaze and confident strength; Wendish women are renowned for tirelessness and firm character no less than men. At evening, long-drawn songs rise above the villages. Some are begun by women returning from the fields, others taken up beside the fires, until the melody carries across dark water and the fringe of [[Веданский лес|Vedan Forest]].

Wendish homes are built of timber around a warm common room. The family's sign is carved upon a supporting beam, and an empty hook is left beside the entrance for a guest's cloak. People prize possessions able to serve more than one generation: a solid chest, a woollen coverlet, a knife or a clay pot. Damage seldom means the end of an object; a good artisan is respected for restoring both its usefulness and its memories.

Food is plain and sustaining: dark bread, grain porridge, roots, mushrooms, fish, honey and whatever can be preserved by drying, salting or cold. A feast is distinguished not by the rarity of its dishes, but by whether there was room enough for everyone. To refuse offered bread without reason is to reject not the food, but the peace of the household.

## Faith and dignity

Velissa is worshipped as Goddess of Earth, Love, Beauty and spring's renewal. The first grain of a new harvest, flowers and ribbons are left for her, but peace within a family and aid given to someone in distress are considered the finest offerings. To the Wends, forests and rivers are filled with ancient life. They are neither trifled with nor conquered, but treated with care, like silent elders.

The runes of their northern ancestors remain in daily life, though their meaning has changed. They mark field boundaries, wells, wedding belts and shields, while a sign upon a threshold more often asks for safe return than threatens a stranger. Important promises are spoken before the hearth or upon ancestral soil. To break such a word is to fail not only the listener, but the whole household that bore witness.

## Love, desire and vows

To the Wends, love possesses weight, warmth and magical force. Runes of mutual desire are placed upon ribbons, jewellery and headboards to heighten tenderness and attraction that already exist. During courtship such signs are given openly: a hidden rune intended to deprive another person of choice is regarded as a desecration of the goddess's gift.

Sexuality is honoured as the capacity to give life, joy and intimacy. Virginity is not a mark of greater worth, although a freely chosen vow of chastity can sustain protective and healing rites. Married lovers draw the same kind of power from fidelity and intimacy shared between them. The strength lies neither in abstinence nor passion alone, but in a promise freely made and faithfully kept.

For this reason, the Wends treat rune-bound marriage vows with care. Deliberate infidelity after such an oath may inflict longing, sleeplessness or the ‘cold hearth’, in which the fire of one's own home gives the offender no warmth. The curse does not punish love that has ended: it answers the betrayal of a freely given word and weakens after a truthful confession, reconciliation or an honest parting.

## After the long winter

The reign of [[Ивар Свирепый|Ivar the Fierce]] wounded a people whose faith places love above conquest. The rising of the [[Страж|Guardians]] reminded the Wends that kindness does not demand helplessness, and that protecting one's home may begin with defiance of anyone who names cruelty strength. The Wends know how to fight, but count their greatest victory as returning to a hearth where someone still waits for them.

After the war, many families took in refugees, the wounded and former soldiers who found returning to peaceful life more difficult than leaving for battle. Shared labour restores bridges, storehouses and burnt homes. The Wends therefore describe Obitelj's present renewal not as a new age of glory, but as the first spring after a winter that lasted too long: the earth is still cold, yet water has begun to move beneath the ice.
