# Chapter 086 — The Summoning of Shubhankari

*[[Сурадж Ка Гхар|Suraj Ka Ghar]], 106 NE, spring*

They brought [[Селина Эллиот|Selina]] out of the caves almost without incident, although [[Шаша|Shasha]] followed them. She was left behind with instructions to clear the cavern where they had first met her. On the surface, everyone began preparing the rite: [[Гилья Кангарлавехотри|Gilja]] drew the signs and assembled what was needed, [[Урист Китин|Urist]] and [[Томас Робинсон|Tom]] went to find blood for the summoning, and [[Мэй Ву|Mei]] called [[Мэйлун|Meilong]].

The dragon arrived exhausted. He now heard not only the sea monster's voice, but a multitude of human voices speaking endlessly to one another. Nor had the northern dragon goddess relented; she sent ever more emissaries after him.

By evening, the blood of a roe deer entered the rite, its carcass was given to Meilong, and each traveller added a measure of their own blood. [[Шубханкари|Shubhankari]] came.

She dealt first with Selina, remarking that this was probably another cunning design of [[Мерката|the Goddess of Night]]. Then Shubhankari filled a vial with her own blood and gave it to Tom, for it was he who had become a [[Тень|Shadow]].

She spoke of the nagas without gentleness. To her they remained monsters, however cultured or skilful they strove to become. Yet when the travellers mentioned the pyramid in the cavern and the strange device inside it, the goddess changed abruptly. The terrible Shubhankari became a frightened girl and began to weep.

Trying to soothe her, Urist offered red root infusion. The drink cast the goddess into a strange trance: she spoke to an invisible “Mama” and behaved like a little girl being led somewhere she did not wish to go. The travellers tried to halt the procession, but Shubhankari reached the pyramid nevertheless.

Inside a hidden chamber stood a spherical plinth fitted with restraints for six arms and two legs. The goddess stepped into it and screamed. Trying to help, Gilja entered her thoughts through the [[Рактапатр|Raktapatra]] and saw a little Shubhankari chained to the plinth. Blood was being drawn from her while a woman in the doorway operated the device without concern.

The feelings were strongest of all: anger, fury, bloodlust and hatred of the nagas. And one thought, childish and desperate: “I am not a monster like them.”
