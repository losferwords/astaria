# Chapter 065 — The Captive Empress

*[[Империя Ланг-Ан|Lang-An Empire]], 105 NE, spring*

[[Мэй Ву|Mei]] put on the monastic robes, and the group went to the [[Дом Белых Парусов|House of White Sails]] for the Ribbons of Memory. In a storeroom Mei found a casket containing an unsent letter from [[Ао Ву|Ao Wu]] to a man who had gone to the west of the country. She took the letter.

The plan was audacious: [[Томас Робинсон|Tom]] would lie inside a sarcophagus, [[Урист Китин|Urist]] would load it upon one of the rescued horses, and Mei, dressed as a monk, would lead them into the palace. During the changing of the guard, they succeeded in entering the inner courtyard of the [[Храм Дракона|Temple of the Dragon]].

Inside the temple, they were told to carry the ribbons around the courtyard and then descend to the storeroom. On the way they met a peculiar scholar who wished to be the first to study the ribbons, offering a jewel-filled casket in exchange. In the library, once Tom had finally climbed from the stifling sarcophagus, the scholar took possession of the ribbons and burnt them to ashes with a single sweep of his hand.

As the travellers climbed higher, they saw guards dragging a beaten young woman towards the harem. Urist knocked them unconscious, and the woman asked to be escorted to the seventh floor. The group then entered the dressmakers' house. Master Liao Shen recognised Mei and asked her to deliver dresses for the empress to try on.

Thus the travellers reached [[Сунь Ляньши|Sun Lianshi]]. The empress was angry at Mei's long absence, but her joy at seeing her friend again proved stronger. She listened to the tale of their journey and admitted that she had effectively become a prisoner within her own temple: the captain of the guard kept her under watch because of rumours of an attempt upon Lang-An's life.

Sun Lianshi asked Mei for help and ordered the charges against Mei and Urist withdrawn. Yet in a palace where even the empress spoke as though from within a cage, an order still had to be made to hold.
