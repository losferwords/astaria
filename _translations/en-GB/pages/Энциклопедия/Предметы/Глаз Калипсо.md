# Eye of Calypso

![[Assets/Images/Eye_of_Calypso.jpg]]

## Main text

The name **Eyes of Calypso** belongs to several Thalassian medallions of a common origin. Each is circular, set with a large blue sapphire at its centre and a halo of smaller stones around the rim. Their settings combine gold, silver and several rare alloys not ordinarily brought together in jewellery. When the medallion moves, light passes through the mount in such a way that the central stone seems alive, its gaze following the wearer.

The jewels are named for the goddess [[Калипсо|Calypso]]. According to the [[Наварх|Navarchs]], their sapphires precisely match her eyes: clear and brilliant blue, like a calm sea before dawn. Captains and gamblers wear cheap imitations in hopes of courting fortune, but a true Eye is distinguished by its unusual setting and a tracery of sea routes engraved with extraordinary delicacy. No one knows with certainty how many survive.

There is no reliable evidence that an Eye possesses supernatural power. Some owners claimed that the stone grew warm before a momentous choice; others dismissed the tale as a fiction invented to raise its price. In Thalassia, such uncertainty only makes the relic more alluring. Calypso never promised that good fortune would be easy to distinguish from a wise decision.

One such medallion belongs to [[Пирей Никос|Piraeus Nikos]]. To sailors, this makes it not merely a jewel, but the token of a tale that has yet to end. Anyone who sees the Eye at a gaming table or upon a captain's breast may well wonder whether the relic itself has been staked—or whether it is choosing the next player in its adventure.
