# Mirgrad

![[Assets/Images/Mirgrad_City.jpg]]

**Mirgrad** is the capital of [[Обитель|Obitelj]], standing upon a high bank of the [[Река Красивая|Krasivaya River]]. It was established in 1236 ChE upon the place where, according to tradition, the future Beregini first spoke to [[Велисса|Velissa]] and [[Основание Обители|made peace with her]]. The city grew beside a favourable crossing where the river route meets roads from the grain plains. Rafts bearing timber arrive in spring, boats with honey, flax and pottery in summer, and grain carts in autumn. In winter, the river falls quiet and life retreats into workshops, common halls and homes gathered around hot stoves.

The fortified royal court and its carved timber halls rise above the city. Below lie the artisans' quarters, quays and market square. Mirgrad's houses are built chiefly of wood upon stone foundations, protecting their lowest beams from damp. Broad streets and open squares are deliberate: they divide neighbourhoods during a fire, while also providing space for spring festivals, councils and shared labour.

## The city after the war

During the uprising of the [[Страж|Guardians]], Mirgrad became the last stronghold of [[Ивар Свирепый|Ivar the Fierce]]. The fighting damaged its gates, quays and part of the upper city, yet its people preserved the bridges and granaries. After the king's expulsion, generals, elders and Wendish nobles assembled here to decide the throne's fate. The Beregini interrupted their dispute by bringing forward the unknown [[Мафка|Mavka]] and proclaiming the girl chosen by [[Велисса|Velissa]].

Reconstruction now governs the capital's rhythm. Guardians reinforce the embankments and help carpenters clear ruins, craft yards accept apprentices from devastated villages, and the royal stores provide seed and materials for mending roads. Mavka's court is far less solemn than Ivar's: farmers, widows, merchants and children come to its gates with petitions, confident that the little queen will hear each of them.

Mirgrad still bears the marks of its recent war, yet its name sounds like a promise once more. At evening, the lights of the quays burn above the Krasivaya, working songs carry from the farther bank, and the city is slowly learning to be not the fortress of power, but the common home of a country.
