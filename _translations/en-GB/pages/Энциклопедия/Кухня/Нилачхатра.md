# Nilachhatra

## Blue parasols of the jungle

**Nilachhatra** are edible mushrooms with dark blue caps, growing upon damp timber in the jungles of [[Сурадж Ка Гхар|Suraj Ka Ghar]]. Their name means ‘blue parasol’ in the Rajati tongue. After a night of rain, the mushrooms open in dense tiers like tiny awnings raised above the moss.

Fresh nilachhatra taste bitter and may cause severe nausea. They are therefore boiled twice in fresh water before being fried with oil, soured milk and spices. The flesh remains firm and develops a deep flavour recalling both nuts and smoke. Poor villages add it to lentils and grain; wealthy households serve it upon thin flatbreads with a hot sauce.

Their blue colour has made nilachhatra a favourite of cooks. In an acidic dish, the flesh turns violet; in salted stock, almost black. An experienced cook can judge the acidity of a meal from its hue. Children are sometimes allowed to touch a raw cut surface with the tongue, which turns briefly blue, though swallowing an untreated mushroom is strictly forbidden.

Gatherers set out at dawn, before the heat softens the caps. In the [[Джунгли Шанкари|Shankari Jungle]], this work has become especially perilous because of the vetalas, so a basket of fresh mushrooms may cost more than meat. At market, such nilachhatra are sold together with the gatherer's name. The buyer pays not only for the food, but for a journey from which someone managed to return.
