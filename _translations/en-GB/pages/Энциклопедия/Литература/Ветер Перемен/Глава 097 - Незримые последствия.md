# Chapter 097 — Unseen Consequences

*[[Сурадж Ка Гхар|Suraj Ka Ghar]], 106 NE, autumn*

The Second Brew smelt of curry and hot tea. [[Томас Робинсон|Tom]] still sat near the counter where the trail of blood had ended. When a palace scribe with a battered face came down from the upper floor, the morning's enquiries finally began to make sense.

The scribe was visibly frightened by the broad-shouldered northerner in another man's trousers and black feathers. He knew almost nothing of what had happened to Tom during the night, but he could explain the blood and teeth in [[Бату Эклунд|Batu's]] shop.

The previous day, he had gone to question the leatherworker. The owner had grown angry and knocked out his teeth; the scribe had abandoned the servants accompanying him and fled. He plainly had no wish to return to Batu. Instead, he offered to take Tom to the palace of [[Город Ванпур|Vanpur's]] elder. Thus the hunter's morning search brought him to the palace gates, where he remained with Musaka while the scribe went inside.

Meanwhile, [[Урист Китин|Urist's]] clothes were returned to him, freshly cleaned. A servant invited him to breakfast and conveyed word that the woman would no longer be troubled. Which woman, and why this news was intended for him, Urist did not yet understand. He dressed and went to the master of the palace, hoping at last to learn something about the missing part of his evening.

The elder, [[Рахман Саркар|Rahman Sarkar]], waited at a table beside a channel filled with fish. He welcomed his guest as though they had already discussed the most important matters the previous day and immediately spoke of copper ore from [[Деревня Улехей|Ulehey]]. Rahman needed another shipment; all that remained was to establish when it would arrive.

Urist said two weeks. The elder found the time acceptable.

At that moment, the scribe entered the hall — [[Викрам Саркар|Vikram]], Rahman's son. At the sight of Urist, he recoiled, caught his foot upon the edge of the channel and fell into the water. The fish scattered. Rahman threw an empty sauce cup at his son, but lacked the strength even for such a short throw: the cup travelled about a metre and shattered.

The dripping Vikram recognised the guest and began apologising for the previous day's misunderstanding. His father immediately reminded him that this was Urist Kitin, elder of Ulehey and Herald of [[Хангор|Khangor]], and that such a man deserved different treatment. Urist was indeed a Herald, but had been made elder of his home village by mistake. He chose not to correct that part of the introduction.

Local custom obliged the scribe to bow forty times. About ten minutes passed while Vikram bent diligently before the guest. Only then did he venture to ask why he had been summoned. Rahman ordered him to draw up a trade agreement at once: copper ore from Ulehey was to reach Vanpur in two weeks.

The agreement was quite real, despite Urist's invented office. Earlier, such a swift delivery would have been beyond his power; now he had acquired the means to provide it, though he had yet to use them. He would have to leave the city within the next two weeks or find a way to contact Ulehey in time.

Urist asked why Vanpur needed so much copper: a large shipment had arrived only recently. Rahman replied that he was preparing for the Cycle of Creation. The city needed hoes, shovels, cookware, building work, trade and protection. One had to provide for the future in advance, especially when one's own future remained in [[Шубханкари|Shubhankari's]] hands.

He looked at his son. The elder plainly distrusted Vikram and asked bitterly who would inherit the city when the goddess took him.

Then the conversation returned to the woman mentioned by the servant. It was [[Савитри Эклунд|Savitri]]. Rahman admitted that he understood Avgar surnames poorly and ordered the records concerning her to be struck out, putting everything down to an incompetent scribe's mistake. Vikram was to amend the papers so that neither Urist nor his family would face further accusations in the city.

The reason for the battered face now emerged as well. It was precisely because of the records concerning Savitri that the scribe had gone to Batu. Rahman considered the lost teeth a deserved punishment. Urist offered no sympathy either: if the leatherworker had grown so angry, the questions had probably been insufficiently polite. Having received his orders, Vikram went to the infirmary.

The palace had a pigeon post. Urist decided to use it to contact Ulehey, both about the shipment and about other mutually beneficial affairs. Breakfast scarcely interested him: the food was spicy and greasy, and there was no meat. He never discovered how he himself had reached the palace or earned such lavish guest chambers.

Beneath the House of Red Silence, the lamplight had scarcely changed since the night. The scent of spices lingered in the stale air, and water remained the most desirable and unattainable thing of all. [[Гилья Кангарлавехотри|Gilja]] continued looking for it while Batu finished the remaining third of the [[Авгарский самогон|Avgar moonshine]]. In a hoarse voice, the trader asked where they were. Gilja had no answer. Four cells opened along the corridor, but he had found neither stairs nor a hatch leading out.

The bound girl lay beside the beds with a gag over her mouth. For the moment, Gilja merely prodded her with a finger to attract her attention; a proper interrogation had yet to begin. When the gag was lowered to her neck, she remained silent. The dark-haired Rajati watched the two men who had tied her up during the night and now could not remember why.

Ordinary threats changed nothing. Then Gilja spoke of the power of the [[Ракша|Rakshas]], the raktakataras and Shubhankari's bloody past. At last the girl grew frightened and gave her name as Gita.

According to her, the previous day she had helped [[Киран Варма|Kiran]] settle the patients in their beds. Leaving his quarters, she had encountered Gilja and Batu, who gave chase. She ran, leaned against a wall, and the wall opened, taking her below. Batu caught her and knocked her senseless with a single blow, after which the men tied her up and fell asleep. She had not drunk with them and had spent the whole night waiting for them to sober up.

Gilja did not believe the story about helping the sick. As far as he knew, people near Kiran disappeared rather than recovered. Gita did not argue. She considered [[Самир Тхакур|Samir]] just as useless a healer, though at least he was kind.

They could learn nothing more from her. Batu began feeling along the walls for a concealed lever, but drunken persistence revealed no new exit. Gilja untied their prisoner. While she rubbed her wrists, their attention returned to the statue of Destruction and the spices upon the floor.

The costly mixture had been scattered in a circle. Beside the statue, the line was smeared, as though disturbed by an opening door. To complete the rite, he needed only to close the pattern. Gilja still did not know which rite he had begun during the night. The image of Shubhankari holding a blade promised no harmless result.

Nevertheless, Gilja restored the circle. Then he remembered the abandoned temple in the forest between [[Город Танассар|Tanassar]] and [[Деревня Бадракали|Badrakali]]: there, changing the position of the statue's arms and its [[Рактапатр|raktapatra]] had opened a secret passage. He repeated the familiar movement.

Something clicked within the stone. The statue turned, and an alarming tingling ran through his body. Gilja expected blood or a scream, but no one died. Gradually, the sensation became familiar. Batu stepped into the passage first, with Gilja following. Gita remained on the other side. The passage closed, and the men found themselves in another corridor with four cells.

Above them, [[Мэй Ву|Mei]] was still sitting in the laundry. The women had left, making the stream sound especially clear. Morning light fell upon the folded arms of the statue of Creation — the very wall before which Mei had spent the night in vain.

At last, a click came from within. The statue's arms moved apart, stairs appeared behind the stone, and the familiar girl ran out. She pulled up her hood and walked calmly towards the exit. Mei followed.

The trail led to the elder's palace. The girl gave the guard some coins, received permission to enter and crossed the courtyard. The building stood about fifty metres away, its grounds surrounded by a bamboo fence. Tom waited at the gate with [[Мусака|Musaka]], still wearing work trousers and his feather garment.

Mei did not rush to him immediately. She wanted to discover how much it cost to pass the guards so freely. Then she called to Tom from the bushes and tried to draw him towards the fence so they could slip inside without unnecessary conversation.

To Tom, events looked entirely different. First, the bushes seemed to speak to him; then an unfamiliar woman began dragging him into them. He tried to resist, but the former spy proved quicker and more persuasive. Mei explained that they were companions and had been out on a job together the previous day. She even introduced him as her accomplice in theft. Tom did not remember her, but gradually began to believe her.

Before long, Urist emerged through the gates. Mei brought Tom over and explained that this man was one of them as well. Hearing of the gaps in his memory, Urist laughed: in his opinion, after the previous day's drinking, the northerner was simply playing a joke upon his friends.

But Tom was serious. He remembered the previous night with [[Мерката|Mercate]], yet did not know how he had ended up upon his sleeping place or why his belongings were missing. Batu's son had lent him the trousers. Tom had already visited the shop early that morning, but found only the boy, who remembered no more than he did. Gilja had vanished from his memory as well. Urist was certain that they would find the Raksha and that he would be able to explain everything.

At first, they decided to return to the leatherworker, but Tom reminded them that Batu had not been there. Then they thought of [[Лиарин Ветреная|Liarin]]. She had dealt with the wounds from the attack by invisible jaws; perhaps she could help now as well, especially with Tom. The travellers were about to return to the Second Brew when Mei noticed the hooded girl at the gates. Once more, she gave the guard a coin and came out.

This time, they did not let her go. Mei told the others of her pursuit during the night; the three approached quietly from different directions and surrounded the girl before she recognised the danger. Urist took her by the shoulder. Now they needed explanations: what the tiny golden cups meant and how the statue in the laundry opened.

They brought the girl back to the House of Red Silence. She showed them the movement of the statue's arms; the travellers repeated it, and the passage opened. They made her go down first.

Mei at last saw the rooms she had failed to reach during the night. In one cell lay an old woman and a younger one; in another, two men with bandaged elbows. A boy slept in the third. In the fourth they found an empty bottle of Avgar moonshine and the familiar spices. Such supplies pointed all too clearly to Gilja and Batu.

The girl said that it was a kind of hospital. She could only guess why it was hidden: probably to avoid disturbing the Rakshas treating the patients. Urist opened the second statue. He too felt a sharp tingling that soon ceased to trouble him. Voices sounded beyond the wall.

Gilja and Batu had not yet escaped the neighbouring corridor. Instead of an exit, they had found medical instruments, books and detailed notes on the symptoms of [[Ракта-Доша|Rakta-Dosha]]. In lists of Rajati, some names had been crossed out, others circled. The lamps were burning, and the fruit had been there only a day or two; the place was clearly still in use. There was water in a jug, and Batu drank it. The wooden door at the far end was locked, with a keyhole that ran all the way through.

Gilja was glad to see his friends, but regarded the girl beside them with his former distrust. That morning she had sat bound at his feet, insisting that she knew nothing; now she walked freely with Mei and Urist. Gilja suspected that her connection to Kiran was much closer than she admitted, and did not rule out services of a very different kind from tending the sick.

Gita was hurt by the way he spoke of her. Mei noticed her reaction and took a different approach. Beneath the silence lay the weariness of someone who had long wanted to leave but could see no safe way out.

The girl admitted that she had become involved with the [[Чёрный Лотос|Black Lotus]] when she was younger. She was a lowly messenger, delivering orders rather than deciding the fate of others, but she feared simply leaving the organisation: for that, they might put her to sleep for ever. Gita showed them a ring bearing the Lotus emblem, removed it and gave it to Mei.

Mei accepted the signet and agreed to help the girl escape Vanpur in such a way that the Lotus's people could not catch her later. For the first time, Gita had found someone amongst her recent pursuers with whom she could seek protection. Trust grew between them, mingled with a warmer feeling that neither had yet named aloud.

After that, the girl explained the cups. The tiny vessels served as a signal when orders for injections with a black needle were passed along. The Lotus's people first found a client, then used a cup to signal a meeting; a senior member of the network supplied a needle for the person who was to be put to sleep. The previous day, an order had come in for the woman in the neighbouring ward.

According to Gita, Nadila did not belong to the Lotus and did not know the cups' true purpose. The teahouse merely provided a place for the exchange. Messages had once been passed to the palace in the same fashion. The familiar object found in the guest house chest once again connected the investigation to Kiran — or to Savitri, who had stayed there before him.

The details of the night's pursuit emerged as well. After collecting the cup, Gita had gone through the laundry, crossed two underground halls and headed for the wooden door. Gilja and Batu were waiting upon its threshold. The men had shouted and rushed after her, forcing her to run back.

The door itself led to a waterfall. It was a back entrance, rather than a way into a study or palace. Neither the drunken Avgar nor the Raksha could remember how they had reached the waterfall. When the door was opened, the roar of falling water drowned their voices, and cool air swept away the staleness of the underground chambers. At last, Urist and Gilja could drink.

According to the girl, Kiran used the rooms for his work: people were brought there, while she merely carried messages, as did [[Джевахар Ашма|Djevahar's]] son. Gita did not know what exactly the instructor did with the patients. The books and instruments led Gilja to think that Kiran was attempting to practise medicine, though the Raksha saw no vocation for it in him. Mei offered a darker explanation: perhaps people were brought here for experiments.

The girl could confirm neither theory. They would have to ask Kiran himself. She suggested waiting for him here, provided he had not yet realised that strangers had entered. The waterfall might have masked their shouts, but someone lived nearby, and they could not be certain.

Batu asked to go home. They let him leave, and he went out through the waterfall, looked around and set off for his shop. The last third of the moonshine was still taking its toll. The four companions and Gita remained below.

Mei did not want to wait for Kiran underground. It would be better to return to the teahouse, eat, tend their wounds and arrange a meeting through another cup. Gita could pass on the signal, but she would need her ring back. The travellers now had two Lotus signets — hers and the one Urist had acquired earlier.

Urist took responsibility for arranging the meeting. Gilja also remembered Shubhankari's command: find Kiran and deal with him. If the instructor received his due, getting Gita out of the city would become easier. The girl herself had no wish to become involved in a religious dispute, but agreed to help.

She asked them not to leave her alone and promised to stay beside Urist. Then she clasped Mei's arm. Mei did not draw away.

Before leaving, Urist gathered up the spices and swept away the circle so that another Raksha would not notice the traces of the rite. By midday, the five had left the underground chambers and were heading for the Second Brew.

Tom walked behind. He now accepted that the three people he did not recognise were his companions, but explanations did not bring his memories back. He had certainly encountered the Black Lotus; only, in the scenes that remained, he had acted alone. Even the battle in the alley had been altered in his memory: Tom believed that he had brought down four opponents with a single arrow, without the help of Mei, Urist or Gilja.

Neither Liarin nor [[Велимир Сажин|Velimir]] was still at the Second Brew. The travellers supposed that they had gone out for mushrooms again and decided to eat and wait. They wanted greasy food, water and a few hours' sleep.

Their strength gradually returned at the table. Mei managed to overcome her apathy, but Gilja's longstanding troubles remained. The consequences of his encounter with the red crystal required the help of the [[Тхаги|Thuggee]] they knew, his right arm was still impaired, and the old curse likewise resisted ordinary treatment. Gilja nevertheless attended to Tom's haematoma, after which his friends helped him speak of what troubled him in turn.

The conversation returned to the vision of Shubhankari's childhood. Urist suggested that the other gods might have pasts just as sorrowful. Such memories brought them closer to people than the image of indifferent beings in the heavens, for whom the suffering of others cost nothing. If Shubhankari could change, then Gilja could change as well.

Urist proposed a modest beginning: stop making his pilaf quite so spicy.

During the meal, a raven settled upon the windowsill. He tapped upon the wood. Tom recognised Morogrim, Mercate's companion. The bird flew down from the window and headed west.

Tom followed. The raven chose no route convenient for a man, but the hunter overcame the obstacles and climbed over the city wall. Beyond it, Morogrim settled high in a tree, then moved lower and tapped upon the trunk.

Tom's experience as an animal trainer helped him understand the bird. Morogrim conveyed that the coming night's dream would be the absolute truth. Tom's connection to darkness had been lost for a time, and only he could restore it. He was to go west. The raven gave no further details.

Two coins fell from the bird's foot — a gift from Mercate. The darkness still did not answer, and the familiar abilities of a [[Тень|Shadow]] remained beyond his reach, but each coin could be used once to call upon that power.

Tom returned to his companions. He kept the westward direction and the promised dream in mind, with the two coins in his possession; at the Second Brew, lunch and rest still awaited. After eating, they all went to sleep.

The travellers woke towards midnight. Gilja was the first to stir and felt much better — until he looked at his right arm.

Part of it had become invisible. He could still feel the arm, and his fingers obeyed him; Gilja picked up a mug from the table and raised it, watching a movement he could no longer follow in full. The emptiness where flesh should have been reminded him of the invisible [[Ветал|vetalas]].

He woke Urist. Mei examined herself as well: her arms were still there. Urist was frightened at first, then decided that he ought to wash his face before anything else. Half asleep, a man might imagine stranger things.

Gilja pointed out that Urist's buttocks had disappeared.

Urist checked — and was horrified. He considered this part of his body one of his favourites and quite indispensable to everyday life. Unable to resist, Gilja jokingly touched the equally invisible spot with his invisible hand.

The joke did little good. Urist immediately asked what exactly had been scattered before the statue. Gilja admitted that, in his drunken state, he had used spices and apparently attempted some sort of rite. He could not remember its purpose. He had put the morning's strange sensations down to a hangover.

Urist demanded that they go to the temple, find books of rituals and discover what they had done. If the invisibility was spreading, the rest of them might disappear by morning.

Gilja suggested that, for the moment, they should stop fussing and put on some clothes. They had already arranged a meeting with Kiran that night and intended to capture him. As for the vanished parts of their bodies, he advised dealing with those in the morning — if there was still anything left to examine.
