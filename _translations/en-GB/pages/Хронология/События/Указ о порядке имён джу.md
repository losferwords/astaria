# Decree on Dju Name Order

> [!timeline] 208 ChE
> Empress Lan Tao, [[Дракон Ланг-Ан|the Dragon's]] chosen wife from a non-noble family, establishes the order "given name → family name" among the [[Джу|Dju]] and opens the bridal selection to young women of every social class. The reform preserves the memory that ancestry does not determine a person's worth.

## The Dragon's Chosen Bride

Lan Tao (蘭陶) was the daughter of a provincial scribe. Her family had little money and no connections at court, but she grew up among books and documents. Her education and eye for detail helped her understand the rules for admission to the bridal selection.

Young and ambitious, she entered the palace posing as a copyist. She gained access to the preparations for the bridal selection and secretly added her real name to the list of candidates. When her name was called, she stepped forward with the other young women.

Before the final choice, Lan Tao herself told the Dragon what she had done and explained that she had wanted a chance to be heard. Her candour helped her earn his trust.

Knowing the truth, the Dragon considered her the most worthy of all the candidates and chose her as his wife. She became empress, demonstrating that a worthy bride of the Dragon could come from any social class.

## The Decree of 208 ChE

In 208 ChE, the empress issued a decree changing name order and the rules for admission to the bridal selection. Before the reform, she herself had been known as Tao Lan (陶蘭). The Dju had previously placed the family name before the given name; from then on, the given name came first. Through this reform, she wanted to preserve the memory of her own path to the throne and of personal merit taking precedence over noble ancestry. [[Амато|Amato]] retained the earlier order: family name, then given name.

The same decree established the right of any young woman to put herself forward for the bridal selection, regardless of social class. It gave others a lawful opportunity to follow the path that she herself had begun through deception.

## The Court's Response

The noble families were divided over the reform. Some recognised the empress's merits and supported the decree; others sought exemptions and tried to retain control over admission to the bridal selection. After debate at court, both provisions of the decree remained in force. Lan Tao gained allies as well as opponents among the nobility.

## The Bridal Selection Today

Any young woman may now formally enter the selection. However, Lang-An values not only a pure heart but also a clear mind and purity of intent. Young women from poorer social classes often lack opportunities for education and preparation, as well as an environment that would help them develop these qualities. Formal eligibility therefore still leaves them with unequal opportunities to take part.
