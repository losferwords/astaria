# Vakumara

![[Assets/Images/Wakumara.jpg]]

## At a glance

- People: [[Ваку|Vaku]]
- Imithei: [[Шаман|Shaman]]
- Deity: [[Руфу|Rufu]]
- Ruler: [[Кинто Мулунгу|Kinto Mulungu]]
- Capital: [[Деревня Намибуа|Namibua]]

Vakumara is a union of [[Ваку|Vaku]] tribes in south-western [[Архея|Archaea]], spread across the [[Джунгли Аконда|Akonda jungles]], river valleys and sun-scorched savannah. It has no stone capital, courtly ceremony or single peacetime army. What binds the realm together is the memory of a slain god, the oaths of its lineages and the pilgrim roads leading to [[Деревня Намибуа|Namibua]].

The name *Vakumara* is translated as ‘the Vaku did not die’, or ‘the deathless Vaku’. It holds the central belief of the union: the death of the body is not the end of a person while the living remember their name, preserve ancestral knowledge and carry their work forward.

## A union born of loss

Before the death of [[Руфу|Rufu]], the Vaku tribes were independent communities, joined by faith and custom but subject to no single authority. In 854 ChE, the armies of [[Гиперион I|Hyperion]] entered their lands. For several years the God of Death defended his people, until he fell at the source of the [[Река Неда|Neda]] in 849 ChE. The heavily armed Ellian host then drove the Vaku south and west.

The [[Шаман|Shamans]] recovered Rufu's body, preserved it and carried it deep into Akonda. A vast necropolis arose near Namibua and became a common sanctuary for the scattered lineages. Pilgrimage, the shared defence of the god's remains and the memory of defeat gradually united the tribes more firmly than the power of any conqueror could have done.

## Land of the living and the ancestors

Vakumara's settlements are seldom large. Villages stand beside rivers, fertile reaches of savannah and paths known only to local guides. Homes are built for heat and heavy rain, medicinal gardens surround their courtyards, and carved posts bearing the names of the dead mark the borders of each settlement. The jungle offers food, timber, poisons and healing plants, but demands an intimate knowledge of the seasons and the ways of animals.

To the Vaku, the ancestral world lies beside that of the living. Hunters ask leave of dead kin before setting out, an important dispute may be delayed until a dream has been interpreted, and burial becomes a long rite of remembrance. Masks, tattoos and the cadence of drums are no mere ornament: every sign tells which lineage claimed a person and by what path their soul should depart the home.

Rufu's death has made faith more tangible, not less. His mummified body rests in Namibua, and the Shamans maintain that the Dead God still counsels them. Their authority rests upon more than fear. People come to them to treat fever, settle inheritance, seek protection before a journey and learn whether the ancestors have been angered.

## The tribal council

Each tribe retains its customs, chief and warriors. Matters concerning all are decided by a council at Rufu's necropolis, where an elder must be heard before weapons may be raised. The council is led by High Shaman [[Кинто Мулунгу|Kinto Mulungu]], yet even he cannot simply command every lineage. His power depends upon interpreting the god's will, reconciling chiefs and proving the worth of a decision.

When invasion comes, the tribes raise a common host. Their warriors use forest lore, ambush, poison and river paths upon which a foreigner's heavy formation loses its advantage. Vakumara's surest fortification remains Akonda itself—humid, dense and nearly impassable to anyone unable to read its signs.

## Neighbours and the present day

The memory of the war with [[Гилас|Gilas]] has not faded with the centuries. The Vaku hold Hyperion responsible for Rufu's death, and their stories of the fallen are told as though the battle ended yesterday. The northern and eastern reaches around the [[Река Мвиди|Mvidi River]] remain uneasy: a caravan may pass unharmed, but an armed company arriving unbidden will be met as a possible invasion.

Even so, Vakumara is not closed to the world. Medicinal infusions, rare resins, carved masks and the service of healers are prized far beyond the union. A foreigner may be welcomed if they respect local prohibitions and leave funerary objects untouched. Anyone who dismisses reverence for the Dead God as savagery soon learns that Vaku patience lasts only until the memory of their ancestors is insulted.
