# Ikaterra

## Main text

Ikaterra is the goddess of water, tranquillity and healing, patron of the [[Манаи|Manai]] people and the realm of [[Хамоа|Hamoa]]. She is worshipped not as a mistress of storms, but as the sea's quiet strength: the power to sustain the body, cleanse a wound and restore breath to one already thought lost.

In an age beyond memory, tradition says, the Sea Goddess saved the Manai from destruction and guided their ships to the shores of the [[Полуостров Туануку|Tuanuku Peninsula]]. Water is therefore called the first road and the first medicine in Hamoa. At a naming, a child receives a few drops of seawater; the dead are sent onward with a song whose rhythm follows the gentle surf.

Ikaterra has the form of a tall woman with blue scales, gills and webbing between her fingers. In sacred images, her long hair becomes seaweed and her trident is not an emblem of rule, but a staff upon which a weary traveller may lean. Shells, healing draughts and small carved boats are left in her shrines as thanks for deliverance.

To keep her will and care among mortals, Ikaterra raised seven [[Хранитель|Wardens]], whom the Manai call the Hiku. They tend the wounded, calm dangerous waters and defend villages, striving to preserve even an enemy's life. The mightiest of the seven becomes the First Warden and bears responsibility for the whole path of the goddess's servants.

For centuries Ikaterra appeared often before her people, but roughly a hundred years ago she all but vanished. On rare occasions she is glimpsed upon the shore, exhausted and diminished; after such encounters the Wardens leave their villages for long journeys whose purpose they will not disclose. Ordinary Manai do not know the cause of their goddess's silence, yet tend her shrines as carefully as the house of an ailing kinswoman.

Ikaterra's central teaching is simple: water's strength lies not only in the blow of a wave, but also in its power to fill a crack, pass around a stone and sustain life. Mercy is therefore no softness in Hamoa. One who can staunch blood, reconcile families or stay a weapon at the final moment shows no less strength than a victor in battle.
