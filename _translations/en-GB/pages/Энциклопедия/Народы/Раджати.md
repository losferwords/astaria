# Rajati

![[Assets/Images/Radjati.jpg]]

The **Rajati** are a people of the jungles and broad rivers of [[Сурадж Ка Гхар|Suraj Ka Ghar]]. Their cities are filled with incense, the music of ornaments and song, while villages hide among rice fields, gardens and dense tropical growth. The Rajati favour brilliant cloth, gold, garlands and intricate patterns in which beauty is never separated from sacred meaning.

Their culture rests upon an acceptance of extremes. A celebration may continue for days and mourning become a dance; a healer makes medicine from the same poison a hunter applies to a blade. Rajati love of life does not come from carelessness. They know the price of sudden sickness, a downpour, a predator or another Great Harvest, and so refuse to postpone joy until more peaceful times.

The extended family remains the foundation of society. Several generations may live around one courtyard, practise a craft together and raise children in common. Kin answer for one another before neighbours and temple, while a guest receives water and food before being asked their name. Family celebrations are accompanied by music, dancing and an exchange of incense whose scent may recall home for years afterwards.

The Rajati are skilled in herbcraft, gem-cutting, the preparation of spices and hazardous methods of healing. Their physicians study blood, poisons and the properties of tropical plants with close attention. Such knowledge may save a dying person, but often unsettles foreigners—especially when a healer asks for a cup of blood or offers a draught that first causes the patient to cease breathing.

## Memory and history

The Rajati do not preserve an unbroken history well: each new Cycle of Creation and Destruction changes cities, temples and even the means by which the past is remembered. Written chronicles often vanish, while songs, dances and ancestral jewellery survive the fall of palaces. A storyteller may not know the year of an event, yet reproduce precisely the hero's movements and the colour worn when they met their fate.

Ancient writings from other countries nevertheless suggest that the Rajati have inhabited southern [[Архея|Archaea]] for more than two thousand years, long before [[Империя Ланг-Ан|the neighbouring Lang-An Empire]] arose.

The Rajati attend closely to chance encounters, changes in weather and visions brought by ritual draughts, seeing in them possible signs from [[Шубханкари|Shubhankari]]. The [[Настой красного корня|red-root infusion]] is especially renowned, although even the Rakshas cannot always distinguish the goddess's voice from hallucination. Most believe that the souls of the dead return with Shubhankari in the next Cycle. Death therefore brings both grief and reverence: in bidding farewell to someone beloved, the Rajati mourn a completed life while leaving room beside sorrow for the hope of another.
