# Amrit Nair

![[Assets/Images/Amrit_Nair.png]]

Amrit Nair is a [[Тхаги|Thuggee]] field chronicler and translator from [[Город Награкшаса|Nagrakshasa]] who accompanies caravans along the roads of [[Сурадж Ка Гхар|Suraj Ka Ghar]] and far beyond. He gathers testimony, returns lost records to the archive and observes events that may one day become turning points for entire peoples. His travelling cloak has faded almost to grey, but his red sash and rich black turban still mark his allegiance to the brotherhood.

Amrit is not a hired killer. Like most travelling Thuggee, he knows how to handle a weapon, but memory, language and an understanding of people have always been his principal craft. The brotherhood's grim reputation leads foreign scribes to record any Thuggee as an assassin or poisoner without asking what they actually do. Just such an error once appeared in the register of foreign visitors to the [[Храм Дракона|Temple of the Dragon]].

## A Man of Many Languages

Amrit's gift for languages emerged in childhood. In Nagrakshasa, he spent hours listening to caravan travellers, comparing different translations of the same stories and remembering not only words but also pauses, gestures, forms of courtesy and ideas for which his own language had no exact equivalent. Later, he spent days and months among other peoples of Astaria, studying their songs, traditions, beliefs and everyday customs.

Amrit speaks several widely used languages fluently and can hold a conversation in many local dialects. Even by Thuggee standards, he is exceptionally well read: he knows ancient songs, temple interpretations, genealogies, travellers' accounts and conflicting chronicles from different lands. Yet he believes that learning another person's words is not enough. To understand someone, one must know what their people hold sacred, what brings them shame, how they mourn and how they express gratitude. His knowledge is therefore far more than a dry collection of facts. Behind every language, Amrit remembers the people from whom he learnt it.

Amrit's wisdom is quiet and free of arrogance. He readily admits when he does not know something, listens closely to an ordinary worker and does not treat another person's story as something to claim for himself. His nobility comes from neither birth nor title: he tries to keep his word, protect witnesses who have placed their trust in him and refrain from using another person's ignorance to secure an easy victory.

## Dju-Suo

Amrit arrived in [[Город Джу-Суо|Dju-Suo]] as a Thuggee observer when a crisis was already gathering around [[Сунь Ляньши|Empress Sun Lianshi]], the wounded [[Дракон Ланг-Ан|Lang-An]] and the imperial court. Court gossip interested him less than the ways in which the Empire's fate was being shaped by people who had not yet entered its official chronicles.

To hear the voices of foreign guests, warriors, servants and courtiers, Amrit took a job at the [[Таверна Красный Журавль|Red Crane Tavern]]. The work was real, rather than merely a cover: he welcomed guests, helped with translation and performed the ordinary duties of an inn worker. At the same time, he gathered accounts of the empress's captivity, Lang-An's plight and four travellers who would later be remembered as the Heroes of the Dragon.

It was there that he first met [[Урист Китин|Urist]], [[Томас Робинсон|Tom]], [[Мэй Ву|Mei]] and [[Гилья Кангарлавехотри|Gilja]]. Amrit remembered them long before their deeds formed a coherent legend. The heroes, however, retained only a vague impression of the inn worker; after a long journey and many subsequent events, they no longer recognised his face at all.

## Vanpur

In [[Город Ванпур|Vanpur]], Amrit immediately recognised the former guests of the Red Crane. That was why, at the Second Infusion, he approached Tom in particular and addressed him in flawless Lunaar, later speaking freely with Gilja as well. He knew what these people had come to mean to the Lang-An Empire and understood that their appearance amid lost relics, an unknown illness and disputing priests was unlikely to be a coincidence.

Amrit has still not introduced himself to them again. He is neither concealing his name nor pretending to be a stranger: he simply believes that he already gave his name in Dju-Suo. Their failure to remember him does not offend him. To a Thuggee, preserving another person's story matters more than remaining a prominent figure within it.

His offer to join a caravan bound for Nagrakshasa was deliberate. Amrit wants to bring the archive not only recovered records but also living witnesses to the events in Vanpur. He knows that even the most accurate scroll cannot answer a question its scribe never thought to ask.
