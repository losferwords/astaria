# Thuggee

![[Assets/Images/Thuggee.jpg]]

## Main text

The Thuggee are an organisation within [[Сурадж Ка Гхар|Suraj Ka Ghar]] that stands in opposition to the country's dominant faith. Unlike most [[Раджати|Rajati]], they do not regard [[Шубханкари|Shubhankari]] and her Cycles as an unqualified divine blessing. To them, the Cycles of Creation and Destruction are not merely a sacred order, but a recurring calamity that strips their people of their own memory time and again.

Throughout the kingdom, the Thuggee have earned a grim reputation as assassins, poisoners and disciplined fighters. They are spoken of warily: too quiet, too composed and far too capable of remembering what others would rather bury with the previous Cycle.

## Appearance

Against the bright clothing of the Rajati, the Thuggee are readily distinguished by an almost funereal union of black and blood-red. They wear long dark tunics and cloaks with red sashes, belts and embroidered cuffs. Black recalls burnt cities and forgotten names; red represents the price paid for every memory that survives.

Men commonly grow long, carefully tended beards, sometimes braiding them with metal rings or red cords. Their rich, many-layered turbans are adorned with clasps, chains and deep red stones. Thuggee women favour long braids, heavy earrings and lighter head coverings that do not hinder them upon the road or in battle. Their jewellery may appear costly, but it is seldom useless: a sturdy clasp, chain or weighty bracelet can become a final means of defence.

Even inside their archives, the Thuggee do not set aside their weapons. Curved sabres, daggers and short blades are worn openly, whilst needles and small vessels of poison remain concealed amongst the folds of their clothing. To them, reading an ancient manuscript and defending it are parts of the same craft. A venerable chronicler may therefore be an accomplished fighter, whilst a hired assassin may know several languages and the forgotten lineages of dead kings.

## Memory against the Cycle

After one Cycle gives way to another, most Rajati do not record the history and events of the preceding age. The new Cycle is regarded as the beginning of a new life, whilst troubling the past is likened to disturbing the dead.

The Thuggee have cast off this fear. They meticulously record Rajati history, gather testimony, preserve chronicles and compare events across successive ages. To them, memory is not sacrilege, but a weapon against recurring catastrophe.

The greatest repository of this memory is said to lie in [[Город Награкшаса|Nagrakshasa]]. There, Thuggee sages can provide valuable knowledge of Suraj Ka Ghar, previous Cycles, forgotten relics and events that the recognised priesthood would rather leave beneath the earth.

The brotherhood gathers knowledge beyond the borders of its own realm. Travelling Thuggee accompany caravans, hire themselves out as guards and interpreters, visit foreign archives and seek objects that survived the latest Harvest. Some return to Nagrakshasa with scrolls; others bring only a story committed to memory, which the scribes record before permitting the traveller to rest.

## Relations with society

Most inhabitants of Suraj Ka Ghar are devout and regard the Thuggee with fear and suspicion. The brotherhood is believed to settle its affairs through poison, steel and cold calculation.

Yet there is no open confrontation between the Thuggee and wider society. The Rajati are generally peaceful, tolerant and good-natured even towards those who deny Shubhankari's greatness. Thus the Thuggee endure beside temples, markets and ordinary homes: unloved but tolerated, unrecognised but far too useful simply to cast out.
