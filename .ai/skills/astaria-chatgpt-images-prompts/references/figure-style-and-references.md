## The common visual language

The gallery is a range of **dimensional, anime-influenced fantasy illustration**. The best match is not obtained by maximizing either anime exaggeration or photographic realism.

- **Drawing:** readable silhouettes; selective dark contours on eyelids, hair, garments and overlaps; softer edges within illuminated skin and distant scenery. Contours organize the image without outlining every plane equally.
- **Form:** faces, hands and bodies have coherent volume, recognizable joints and weight. Shadows include intermediate tones and reflected light rather than only two flat cel-shaded bands. Stylized facial proportions remain deliberate and subject-specific.
- **Faces:** eyelids, brows, nose planes, lips and jaw distinguish individuals. Chori's face is more structurally defined than Misty's; neither should be normalized into the other. Clear expressions can be warm, reserved, tired or threatening without a universal beauty pose.
- **Surface:** calm areas alternate with selected sharp detail. Embroidery, fastenings, scars, scales and hair edges receive detail where it identifies the subject. Fine detail does not require a grain layer over every surface.
- **Light:** one understandable lighting situation binds subject and background. Warm highlights and cooler shadows recur, but grey rain, blue moonlight, warm interiors and bright fields all belong to the series. Do not impose one orange–teal grade.
- **Composition:** a clear face, gesture or silhouette leads; costume, equipment and setting explain identity. The background is usually a recognizable place with atmospheric depth, not an unrelated photographic plate.

An adaptable English art-direction paragraph:

> Premium modern anime 2.5D character illustration matching the supplied style reference, with elegant selective contours, clearly modeled facial and anatomical forms, layered light and shadow, and softly blended intermediate tones. Preserve the subject's established facial proportions and a coherent expressive gaze. Differentiate softly shaded skin, grouped hair strands, fabric folds, worn leather and reflective metal, using highlights appropriate to each material. Concentrate fine detail on the face, silhouette and defining ornaments, with quieter surrounding surfaces and atmospheric depth. Keep the finish visibly drawn and illustrated.

Use the reference clause only when an image is actually attached. Adapt the material list to what exists in the scene; a bird does not need skin, hair or metal. This paragraph is a starting vocabulary, not an image-tested universal formula or a reason to repeat the same instructions later in the brief. Exclude waxy skin, glossy CGI or excessive eye enlargement only where those are relevant risks.

## Skin, age, gaze and organic materials

**Skin:** model broad cheek, brow, nose and mouth forms through soft transitions and subtle color variation. Keep small, purposeful highlights on lips, nose or a lit shoulder when appropriate. The successful art includes luminous skin; the disliked effect is a uniformly polished or waxy surface that erases structure. `Matte everywhere`, `perfect porcelain skin`, `photorealistic pores` and heavy subsurface-scattering terminology are poor blanket substitutes for the intended drawing treatment.

**Gaze:** specify a visible target when it matters: the manuscript, another person's face, the viewer, an approaching threat. Both eyes should plausibly address that target at the chosen head angle. Do not diagnose a rendering or optical cause from a vague impression of unfocused eyes. When eyes are blindfolded, convey expression through the lower face, head and hands instead of requesting a visible intense gaze.

**Age:** distinguish young adults, mature adults and children through structure, proportion, hair, posture and expression. Older faces need a few lines following brow, eyes and mouth, not repeated engraved furrows. Facial hair grows in directional groups with irregular edges; avoid a single smooth moustache-shaped mass. A role-bearing medium portrait can help, but the current Hyperion and Kenneth also show that a successful face need not always be hidden behind a task.

**Children:** keep age-appropriate proportions, a recognizable nose and jaw for that age, modest clothing and an unforced expression. Avoid compounding `kawaii`, giant eyes, a tiny mouth and a highly polished doll face. Do not correct Mafka by making her an adult or removing all softness from her expression. The critique of Mafka does not require shrinking the characteristic eyes of adult Misty or Losfy.

**Hair:** organize into locks and braids with directional highlights, overlapping strands and a few loose ends. Avoid turning every lock into a polished tube or drawing every strand with the same contrast.

**Fur:** describe coat structure and silhouette. A shaggy dog needs irregular coarse tufts at cheeks, neck, chest, elbows and haunches, a softer undercoat, broken contours and some stray hairs catching the light. Adding only `more detailed fur` may leave the same smooth, groomed surface. Preserve the animal's underlying anatomy; scruffy does not mean a shapeless cloud or an arbitrary new breed.

**Feathers:** Morogrim shows overlapping groups following the neck, folded wing and tail, restrained blue-violet sheen on black plumage, and a few lifted contour feathers. The eye and beak are sharper focal anchors. Transfer this organization and material separation without requiring every barb to be equally crisp or adding snow and sunset to unrelated animals.

**Scales and supernatural surfaces:** Shasha and Naga support bounded scale patches, directional plates and stronger local reflections. A scaled shoulder, bronze cuirass, moist eye or magical accent can be glossy while neighboring human skin remains softly shaded. Do not use an anti-plastic correction as a global ban on shine.

## Reference assignment and review

Use viewer-facing image numbers, not a local filename or a fictional name as an identity token. For example, when the corresponding two images are actually attached:

> Use image 1 for the intended character's facial identity, anatomy and costume. Use image 2 only for selective contours, dimensional shading and material treatment. Follow the scene, expression and lighting described below; do not borrow image 2's face, clothing, symbols or setting.

For a new character without identity art, assign the existing portrait as **style only** and describe the new face explicitly. If a reference contains the very defect being corrected, assign it only the successful layout/content and obtain rendering guidance from an appropriate positive reference. Do not ask to preserve its problematic surface finish.

Compare a result in this order: recognizable subject and anatomy; facial proportions and gaze; silhouette and pose; skin/organic materials against cloth/metal; light and background integration; overall compatibility with the selected reference. Preserve successful decisions and revise the few visible departures in the next complete prompt. Do not declare a style match from prompt wording alone.
