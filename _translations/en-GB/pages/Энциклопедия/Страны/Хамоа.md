## A kinship of villages

Hamoa is the ancient country of the [[Манаи|Manai]] upon the green [[Полуостров Туануку|Tu'anuku Peninsula]] in eastern [[Архея|Archaea]]. It bears little resemblance to an ordinary kingdom. There is no walled stone capital, no standing army and no court set apart from common life. The country exists as an extended kinship of coastal villages, joined by sea roads, shared traditions and the duty to come to one another's aid.

## Haven of Wanderers

Manai legend says that more than two and a half millennia ago, their ancestors fled an unknown catastrophe by sea. The surviving crews landed upon Tu'anuku, joined together and named themselves *Hamoa*, ‘the wanderers’. Their first settlement, [[Деревня Хамоа Хо|Hamoa Ho]], means ‘Haven of Wanderers’ and became the country's historic centre.

The memory of wandering made hospitality Hamoa's highest law. A shipwrecked stranger receives water, food and a dry place before being asked their name. Families often take in an orphaned child from another village as one of their own, while a boat left without a crew becomes the concern of the entire shore.

## A land of water and healing

Life upon Tu'anuku follows the rain, tides and rivers descending from [[Вулкан Ротонуи|Rotonui]]. The Manai fish, cultivate fruit and root crops, build light seagoing vessels, gather medicinal plants and know how to treat wounds where a foreign healer would see only hopelessness. Knowledge passes through families by practice, song and repetition: a child learns to read a wave before earning the right to sail into open water.

Water-dwelling creatures are more than prey or danger to Hamoa. Fishers recognise familiar inhabitants of the reefs, navigators follow the paths of great sea animals, and some families maintain a bond with the same species for generations. No animal is considered human property. The sea's aid must be earned through calm, care and the wisdom to take no more than is needed.

Homes are built so that air can move freely through their walls, with a common shelter broad enough to receive neighbours during a downpour. An evening meal seldom belongs to one family alone: the catch is shared, news spoken aloud, and a decision affecting the village debated until even the dissenting voice has at least been heard.

## Ikaterra and the seven Wardens

Hamoa's patron is [[Икатерра|Ikaterra]], goddess of water, serenity and healing. The Manai believe that she guided their ancestors away from disaster and bestowed a new home upon them. Her will is carried by seven [[Хранитель|Wardens]], known as Hiku, who protect the people from dangers of the sea and those approaching from the mainland. The strongest among them bears the title First Warden.

Hamoa's chief need not be an Imithei, although the present ruler, [[Атуа Та'Вай|Atua Ta'Wai]], is also First Warden. His authority rests neither upon wealth nor ancestry, but upon the trust of the villages. Even he cannot order a family to leave ancestral ground without explanation; yet at his call, dozens of boats will set out to seek one missing fisher.

The country's most vulnerable frontier is the [[Перешеек Мост Хранителей|Wardens' Bridge]], the isthmus joining Tu'anuku to the mainland and the lands of [[Сурадж Ка Гхар|Suraj Ka Ghar]]. Beyond it begin dangerous jungles and different laws. Hamoa's peaceable nature is therefore not helplessness: a people devoted to saving life understands especially well when life must be defended.
