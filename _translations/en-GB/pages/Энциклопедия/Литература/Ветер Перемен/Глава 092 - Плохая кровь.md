# Chapter 092 — Bad Blood

*[[Сурадж Ка Гхар|Suraj Ka Ghar]], 106 NE, summer*

By evening, the travellers reached the stables, hoping to find [[Велимир Сажин|Velimir]] and show him the black shards discovered beneath Viraj's shop. [[Гилья Кангарлавехотри|Gilja]] questioned the stableman, but the old man's toothless speech was almost impenetrable. Fortunately, his hands served him better than words: he drew a simple map showing that Velimir and the Druid [[Лиарин Ветреная|Liarin the Flighty]] had left by a neighbouring gate and gone north.

[[Мусака|Musaka]] took the trail. She led the friends beyond the city into the night growth, where the humid air of the [[Джунгли Шанкари|Shankari Jungle]] mingled the scents of earth, leaves and distant smoke. Near nightfall, the travellers saw a fire. Velimir and Liarin sat beside it in lively conversation. Before revealing themselves, the friends lingered in the darkness and listened.

Liarin spoke of elephants and the [[Первые|First Peoples]]—the Druid name for the humanlike peoples of nature, including elephant-folk, gnolls, maenads, harpies and many others. She also spoke of a mighty [[Реликты|Relict]] who had dwelt in these lands for ages and might hold knowledge needed by [[Церунна|Cerunna]]. Velimir answered with legends of his homeland, recalling its own Relicts, including a three-headed dragon from the distant tales of Obitelj.

Their present expedition was more mundane than these stories. Velimir and Liarin were searching for luminous mushrooms like those the travellers had encountered in the cave. Liarin hoped to use them to lure elephants more safely, for some unknown reason the animals greatly favoured the fungi.

Velimir and Liarin were wary when the travellers stepped into the firelight, but welcomed their friends once they recognised them. After a brief exchange of jokes, [[Урист Китин|Urist]] showed Velimir the shards. He examined them carefully and withheld hasty judgement. They resembled the earlier dangerous fragment, yet did not answer the powers of earth and sleep as the genuine needle had. The stones seemed too ordinary, though without Mercate's help Velimir would not call them counterfeit.

They spent the night beside the fire. Morning began in a less dignified fashion when a noise woke everyone. Tormented by itching horns, Liarin was rubbing them against the trees. Urist found this awakening deeply inappropriate, expressed his displeasure in the plainest fashion and returned to sleep. [[Мэй Ву|Mei]], however, followed the Druid into the forest.

In the thicket, Mei saw an invisible creature attack Liarin while she was occupied with another tree. The air shuddered, horns scraped bark and the Druid cried out. Mei rushed to her aid. Gilja and Velimir came at the sound, and the brief struggle soon revealed their enemy: a [[Ветал|vetal]], an almost invisible jungle creature swollen with blood.

Gilja chose to speak with the authority granted by his goddess and the divine blade, not merely to fight. He ordered the creature to retreat. To the travellers' astonishment, it obeyed. The vetal disgorged the blood it had gathered into the [[Рактапатр|Raktapatra]] and vanished into the jungle as abruptly as it had appeared.

Everyone returned to the fire after the battle. Liarin healed their wounds with the power of nature, but swiftly exhausted herself and fell asleep.

There was still time before their meeting with [[Мерката|Mercate]], so the group returned to the city to visit the hospital and confront one of the Rakshas. A queue had formed at Vanpur's gates: an immense shipment of ore and minerals was entering the city. Urist spoke with the Avgars escorting it and, with his customary mercantile generosity, recommended that they visit a renowned network of brothels.

The travellers stopped at the Second Infusion for food. There, [[Томас Робинсон|Tom]] received the second of Mercate's feathers with another identical note. They also met the [[Тхаги|Thuggee]] they had spoken with during their first days in Vanpur. Gilja conversed with him calmly, almost as a friend. They discussed the queue, the influx of visitors and the strange purchases of ore. The Thuggee thought the shipments might be connected to troubles in the south and said he intended to purchase weapon materials. If the heroes wished to visit his city, he would permit them to join his caravan.

At last, the friends reached the hospital and found far more patients than expected. Its wards overflowed with people slowly consumed by [[Ракта-Доша|Rakta-Dosha]], the very disease whose cure Gilja had once been sent to find.

The hospital was overseen by a weary Raksha named [[Самир Тхакур|Samir Thakur]]. He introduced himself courteously and asked how he might aid the great Raksha Gilja and his companions. Gilja came directly to the point: he accused Samir of neglecting his goddess's will and his own duties, blaming the city's misfortunes upon Rakshas abandoning the Harvest.

Samir did not deny his guilt. He confessed that he had believed one Raksha's refusal to conduct harvests would pass unnoticed amongst all the others. In this way, he hoped to gain time to study the disease and seek a cure. The bitter truth might be that many other Rakshas had reasoned much the same.

At Gilja's request, Samir described his latest observations. Contrary to city rumour, the loss of teeth was not amongst the principal signs of Rakta-Dosha. The illness manifested as growing weakness, fever towards evening, wasting and, at the end, a sleep from which the sufferer never woke. Careful examinations of the dead revealed an enlarged spleen and organ damage caused by bad blood. Stranger still, a substantial transfusion did more than briefly ease the symptoms: it could cure the patient.

Since Tom's blood had already been considered as a possible remedy for Rakta-Dosha, Gilja chose to test it there. The patient was an eight-year-old boy. Gilja mixed the child's blood with Tom's in a vial, but the truth became clear almost at once: the blood was incompatible and the mixture formed a sediment. To Gilja's profound disappointment, Tom's blood could not provide a universal cure.
