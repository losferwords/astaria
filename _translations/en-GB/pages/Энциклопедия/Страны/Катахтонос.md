## A country after catastrophe

Katachthonos is a young country on the shores of the [[Полуостров Таллас|Tallas Peninsula]], founded by descendants of the [[Хтониды|Chthonids]] who survived the destruction of their civilisation. Its name is not a claim to revive the former empire, but an acknowledgement of loss: this is a new home for a people who can never return to [[Остров Хтон|Chthon]].

The country began as a colony of [[Талассия|Thalassia]] and remains closely dependent upon its fleet, commerce and protection. Yet Katachthonos has its own court and laws. It is ruled by [[Арета Лефийская|Areta of Lepha]], daughter of the Thalassian king [[Телемах II|Telemachus II]] and heir to both realms. The bond provides security, while compelling the Chthonids to ask where protection ends and foreign rule begins.

Rebuilt [[Город Лефа|Lepha]] serves as the capital. Its pale streets, gardens and harbours are meant to prove that the Chthonids can build a future rather than merely preserve a vanished past. Farming, fishing and maritime trade are regarded as honest and comprehensible pursuits. Households especially prize objects made by hand, whose continued existence requires no hidden knowledge. Even after catastrophe, the Chthonids retain an exquisite taste: they favour fine cloth, quiet colours, deliberate interiors and long philosophical conversations in which the elegance of an argument matters almost as much as its precision.

The memory of the [[Падение Хтона|Fall of Chthon]] shapes society more strongly than any law. The study of magic, prophecy and many sciences inspires dread, while the worship of [[Тиресий|Tiresias]] is forbidden even here. A child bearing the signs of [[Хтонотема|Chthonotema]] brings a family not hope, but terror that the catastrophe might be repeated. Katachthonos therefore seems a quiet country: people speak carefully, distrust grand promises and know too well what a hunger for limitless knowledge may cost.

Yet they are not a people defined only by prohibition. Young Chthonids increasingly seek to separate learning from their ancestors' arrogance, and remembrance from perpetual punishment. The country's deepest divide runs between fear of the past and the desire to look towards the future once more.
