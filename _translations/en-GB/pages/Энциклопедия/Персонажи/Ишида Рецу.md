# Ishida Retsu

![[Assets/Images/Retsu_Makoto.jpg]]

## Main text

**Ishida Retsu** (石田 烈) was a priestess of [[Ицунэ|Itsune]] in ancient [[Амато|Amato]], whose life survives in family records as a succession of long absences and troubling returns. Her parents placed her in a temple as a child, where Retsu displayed uncommon strength of will and an affinity for spiritual rites. By the age of thirty, she had become the chief priestess of [[Остров Акасака|Tsukushi]].

Her contemporaries described Retsu as a direct and energetic woman, unwilling to mistake mystery for wisdom. She would challenge senior priests when she considered a rite meaningless, yet never refused anyone who came seeking aid. Her sudden departure for the west therefore astonished everyone. She told those closest to her only that she was following the will of Itsune.

Nothing was heard of the priestess for more than five years. One rainy day, she returned to her native [[Город Осуми|Osumi]] with two children, a girl and a boy. Retsu refused to name their father or explain where she had spent the intervening years. She entrusted the children to her family, passed a single night at the temple and sailed beyond the sea once more.

Five years later, Retsu came home utterly broken and exhausted. Almost nothing remained of her former assurance. Her family remembered a sorrowful figure with eyes concealed beneath a band: she wandered through the garden, stood in silence before the household altar and spoke of a duty she had failed to fulfil.

Ishida Retsu died a year later, never having recovered from what she endured overseas. In the temple archives, her name is surrounded by respectful silence. Her descendants preserved only a handful of records and a weapon that no one was advised to touch. To the priests of Amato, Retsu's story remains a warning: even one who can guide the souls of others may be unable to find the way for her own.
