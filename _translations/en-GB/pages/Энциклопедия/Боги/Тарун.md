# Tarun

## Main text

Tarun is the chief god of the [[Громовые Кланы|Thunder Clans]] and one of the great triad of the Dair pantheon, beside [[Мерката|Mercate]] and [[Церунна|Cerunna]]. He embodies storm, energy and freedom—not freedom from obligation, but the right to choose one's own duty and answer for the consequences.

[[Гойдаир|Goidair]] legend tells that, when the ancient Dair tribes spread westward from [[Иомар|Iomar]], they encountered giants of stone. The strength of the [[Друид|Druids]] was not enough to overcome the immense figures. Then a mysterious warrior clad in iron armour came to the Dair. He promised to help, but demanded in return that they defend those lands from ‘the creatures of night’.

The stranger raised his blade, caught a lightning strike upon it and went alone to face the colossi. The Dair heard thunder rolling and saw flashes behind black clouds. When the storm passed, the outlines of the mountains seemed to them like the bodies of fallen giants piled one upon another. Thus the tale explains the present shape of the [[Горный хребет Грань Тьмы|Edge of Darkness]], and names the warrior who gave the people these lands as Tarun.

Tarun rarely shows himself to mortals. The Goidair believe that after the battle with the giants he rests high in the heavens and briefly wakes during storms to speak with his people. In song, he never asks anyone to kneel, enters danger first and especially favours those who choose freely without leaving their companions to pay the price.

The Thunder Clans long sought a way to receive part of their celestial patron's power. This gave rise to [[Рунштоирм|Runstoirm]], a rite in which future [[Горец|Highlanders]] hold lightning within a shared circle. The trial measures not only endurance, but the willingness to share mortal danger without releasing one another's hands.

Tarun's blade is said to have shattered during the battle with the giants. In some images, lightning extends the missing part of the blade. The Highlanders treasure their [[Меч Де'лаим|De'laim swords]] and believe that, when Tarun returns, each warrior will be able to offer their own sword to the god.

Old tales sometimes place Tarun beside [[Хангор|Khangor]] and [[Калипсо|Calypso]]. In them, thunder gives a device its power, the smith gives it form, and the traveller gives it direction. Priests do not know how literally these stories of three gods united in friendship should be understood.
