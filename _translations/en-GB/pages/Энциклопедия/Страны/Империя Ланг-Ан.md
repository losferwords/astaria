## The Empire of Fire

The Empire of Lang-An, also known as the Empire of Fire, is one of the oldest and most populous realms of eastern [[Архея|Archaea]]. Its cities stand along the [[Река Тяо Хэ|Tiao He]], amid rice terraces, bamboo groves and the gardens of [[Долина Гармонии|He-Dji-Gu]]. To the [[Джу|Dju]], this country is not simply the possession of an empress, but a great order in which family, craft, water and flame must each occupy its appointed place.

## The Dragon's covenant

Tradition holds that the [[Дракон Ланг-Ан|Dragon-God Lang-An]] rescued a people from death in the western deserts and led the worthiest eastwards. In a valley rich with water and fertile earth, he founded a state bound to renounce slavery, endless war and the tyranny of its former rulers. The Dju repeat this covenant when swearing an oath, giving judgement or reconciling a family, although the empire's history has often shown how difficult it is to preserve harmony through force.

The Dragon's dominion once reached from the [[Горный хребет Шафар|Shafar Range]] to the [[Рассветное море|Dawn Sea]]. With the coming of the [[Авгарцы|Avgars]], the empire lost the [[Хангорская долина|Hangor Valley]] and part of the mountain passes. Ruined forts and settlements of blended custom still stand upon the north-western frontier, while old maps preserve lands the court regards as lost, but not forgotten.

## Dragon and empress

The Lang-An Empire is ruled by women whom the Dragon chooses as his brides. Tradition holds that a mortal empress must temper the god's ardour with reason, while the Dragon lends sacred weight and strength to her decisions. Whenever the deity does not appear before his people, his wife becomes his voice to officials, generals and heads of lineages. The present empress, [[Сунь Ляньши|Sun Lianshi]], came to the throne while still very young and has since been compelled to prove that the Dragon's choice matters more than age.

The [[Храм Дракона|Temple of the Dragon]] stands beside the palace, where monks prepare future [[Аватар|Avatars]]. They are regarded as the empire's living flame: guardians of its sanctuaries, defenders against disaster and a reminder that Lang-An's authority is more than a seal pressed into an edict.

## A land of rivers and craft

A gentle climate and plentiful water have made the Lang-An Empire the foremost agricultural and craft centre of the east. Rice, tea, medicinal herbs and fruit trees are cultivated here, together with paper, cloth, carved bamboo and delicate ceramics. Barges travel the Tiao He, binding the inland provinces to the sea ports, while caravans from the south bring spices and rare medicines.

Dju life is founded upon family, reverence for elders and mastery of the self. Even a festival rite becomes an exercise in harmony: the place of a cup, the colour of a ribbon and the order of greetings all matter. To breach custom through ignorance is forgivable. To scorn it knowingly is to reveal that one cannot govern even one's own nature.

Yet beneath the outward order lie many tensions. The houses of the capital contend for influence, frontier towns remember old wars, and remote villages mingle Dju practice with the customs of their neighbours. To a traveller, the Empire of Fire may be a land of exquisite beauty and generous hospitality—or an endless web of obligations in which every gift will one day require an answer.
