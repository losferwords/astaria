# Qadians

![[Assets/Images/Qadians.jpg]]

## Main text

The **Qadians** are a people of central [[Архея|Archaea]] who founded [[Кадир|Qadir]] amid the sands of the [[Пустыня Халисат|Khalisat]]. Their ancestors left the western deserts of [[Амон-Астат|Amon-Astat]] in the company of [[Альзаман|Alzaman]] and found a new homeland beside the Seven Gardens—the lakes around which cities, fields and trade roads grew.

Most Qadians have brown skin, dark eyes and dark hair. Their clothing favours light fabrics that cover the body from the sun, bright sashes and ornaments recalling one's lineage or native Garden. Even a modest house is arranged around a shaded courtyard, where water, a fruit tree and a place for a guest are held to be the signs of a worthy life.

Qadians are renowned as physicians, stargazers, alchemists, scribes and merchants. Learning is never separated from practical good: an observation should help choose a road, heal the sick, preserve water or recognise the proper moment for a decision. [[Альзаман|Alzaman]] has no priesthood of his own; the god favours anyone who seeks knowledge patiently and turns it towards creation. A young pupil may therefore sit beside an old sage in a turban, while an argument about the stars may end in an improved canal or a new remedy. Alzaman's philosophy teaches that time cannot be returned without consequence, and so a wise person guards both their own days and those of others.

Hospitality is a moral duty. Anyone who has crossed the desert receives water and shade before being asked their name. At table, however, Qadians delight in argument, testing not a speaker's station but the strength of their thought. A merchant with a good tale and the honesty to admit a weakness in their goods may achieve more than a haughty ambassador.

After the [[Раскол Кадира|Schism of Qadir]], the inhabitants of its three realms developed customs of their own, but continue to regard themselves as one people. The west looks towards the sea, the centre guards the sanctuaries of time, and the east studies the desert—each path understood as a necessary part of their common future.
