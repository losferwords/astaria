# Dju

![[Assets/Images/Dju.jpg]]

## Main text

The **Dju** (朱) are among the oldest peoples of eastern [[Архея|Archaea]] and form the heart of [[Империя Ланг-Ан|the Lang-An Empire]]. Tradition holds that [[Дракон Ланг-Ан|Lang-An]], the Great Dragon, led their ancestors out of the western deserts and into a fertile valley, where the waters of the [[Река Тяо Хэ|Tiao He]] allowed them to raise cities, gardens and fields.

Harmony and discipline are counted as the foremost Dju virtues. A person is expected to master anger, honour their elders, keep promises and understand their place within family and community. Harmony does not mean submission, however. A good adviser must oppose a ruler whose decision upsets the balance — but must do so respectfully, and at the proper moment.

Families preserve ancestral registers, keep the names of the dead and pass crafts from one generation to the next. The Dju are renowned for calligraphy, weaving, ceramics, work in bamboo and the art of turning an ordinary garden into a carefully composed space. Colour, pattern and the arrangement of objects often convey more than direct speech.

Worship of Lang-An unites the people, though the god's fiery temper may seem the very opposite of Dju restraint. They regard this contradiction as a lesson: fire is useful only within a hearth, forge or lantern, where it has been given form. Human strength likewise acquires dignity only when its bearer can govern it.

The Dju receive foreigners with courtesy, but also with close attention. A guest willing to learn will be forgiven an unfamiliarity with custom; deliberate rudeness is remembered for years. A patient traveller may discover lively humour, generosity and a profound love of home beneath their ceremonial reserve.
