# Vaku

![[Assets/Images/Waku.jpg]]

## Main text

The **Vaku** are a people of southern [[Архея|Archaea]], dwelling in the equatorial [[Джунгли Аконда|Akonda jungles]] and the savannah of [[Закатная долина|Sunset Valley]] and the [[Сфагийские поля|Sphagian Fields]].

Vaku history reaches back to the settlement of the southern continent. The first tribes lived near the border of the [[Пепельные земли|Ashlands]], where [[Руфу|Rufu]], then still a living god, guided them and helped them survive in exchange for their allegiance after death. Following a conflict with [[Аст|the Sun God]] and the pharaoh, the Vaku were driven westwards along the [[Река Мвиди|Mvidi]]. There they formed a tradition devoted solely to Rufu, God of Death. After Rufu was slain, the scattered tribes united beneath the common name [[Вакумара|Vakumara]], affirming their belief that life continues beyond death and their special closeness to the ancestral world.

The heart of spiritual life is the temple complex near [[Деревня Намибуа|Namibua]], where Rufu's mummy rests. The highest [[Шаман|Shamans]] claim to receive instruction through it from the realm of the dead. The Vaku preserve shamanic traditions: they perform sacrificial rites, commune with ancestral spirits, and prepare salves, infusions and potions for healing and cursing alike. Death is understood as a passage, while funerary rites employ dance, masks and song to strengthen the bond between lineage and departed kin.

A singular practice within Vaku culture is the ‘absorption of ancestral souls’ in order to inherit their knowledge and strength. Images of jackals, scythes and skulls are widespread in art and daily life, while masks and tattoos hold an important place in ritual and identity.

The Vaku are known for their martial spirit and endurance; harsh lands and the memory of a fallen god have tempered their character. Despite their sombre reputation, they possess a profound spirituality in which the worlds of living and dead form a single whole, and faith in Rufu's return continues to nourish hope and resistance to outside threats.
