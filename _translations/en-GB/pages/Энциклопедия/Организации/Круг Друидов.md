# Druid Circle

![[Assets/Images/Druidism.jpg]]

## Main text

The **Druid Circle** is the religious and governing community of [[Иомар|Iomar]], founded upon faith in humankind's intimate bond with nature and the duty to protect all living things. To the [[Надаир|Nadair]], the Circle serves at once as a temple, a council of keepers and an authority obliged to consider not only those who inhabit the country now, but those who will follow them.

The teaching was spread by [[Церунна|Cerunna]], goddess of life, after she met the first settlers beside [[Озеро Сильвиан|Lake Silvian]]. Her closest disciples became the first [[Друид|Druids]], while her other servants came to be known as the children of Cerunna. Since that age, the Circle has watched over forest, water and field, settled disputes between communities and judged what harm to the land may be permitted for human need.

Druids hold the foremost offices in the country's government, but their authority is not considered absolute. A decision must be explained before other keepers and reconciled with local custom. If a new road severs an animal trail or planned felling threatens a river, the Circle may halt even the ruler's decree. In return, it must help the community find another course, rather than merely forbid necessary work.

The Circle follows a matriarchal tradition: Cerunna herself takes only girls as her direct pupils. Men may also become Druids, but are instructed by mentors already initiated. Within the community, ancestry and wealth matter less than patience, the ability to hear the living world and the courage to answer for the consequences of one's choices.

Four times each year, at the solstices and equinoxes, representatives of the temples gather to honour Cerunna, exchange news and decide matters of state. They bring not only human petitions, but reports of dried streams, sick herds, strange behaviour among animals and plants appearing where none grew before.

The [[Храм Ишнаалар|Temple of Ishnalaar]], deep within [[Лес Иомар|Iomar Forest]], remains the Circle's greatest spiritual centre. Yet the faith is not confined to a single sanctuary. A ring of stones beside a village, a tree above a spring or a glade where the people leave part of their harvest for wild creatures may be no less sacred. In Iomar, it is said that a true temple begins wherever a person first asks what will remain after they are gone.
