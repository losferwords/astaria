# Red Root Infusion

## The heat of Suraj Ka Ghar

**Red root infusion** is a strong and fiercely spiced drink of [[Сурадж Ка Гхар|Suraj Ka Ghar]]. It is prepared from dried rhizomes with a crimson heart. Thin slices are first cured above smoke, then crushed with spices and steeped for weeks in a potent grain spirit. Every household keeps its own proportions, but a good infusion is known by its ruby colour, astringent scent and the heat that spreads through the body before the second sip.

It is not drunk to quench thirst. A small cup is offered after an arduous journey, before a night festival or to someone who wishes to speak of a matter for which sober courage has failed them. The drink intoxicates swiftly, heightens the senses and summons vivid, indistinct visions. After an immoderate evening come a dry mouth, trembling, a heavy head and memories that cannot always be trusted.

## A deity's voice—or a hallucination

For the [[Ракша|Raksha]], the infusion has ritual significance. Their singular bond with [[Шубханкари|Shubhankari]] allows them to behold the goddess and, on rare occasions, hear her voice. Even an experienced servant, however, cannot always distinguish a true encounter from hallucination. Important words received in such a state are therefore tested through deeds and omens, rather than proclaimed as commandments after a single vision.

Other [[Имитей|Imithei]] may also sense their patron's presence if the bond between them is sufficiently strong. No god appears to an ordinary mortal: they receive only heat, intoxication and strange images. For that reason, many people treat the infusion with respect while preferring to remain sober witnesses to another person's revelation.

Beyond Suraj Ka Ghar, red root is sold in small sealed bottles. Counterfeits are diluted with berry juice and hot pepper, but a connoisseur tests one drop upon white cloth. The true infusion leaves a copper-coloured trace that darkens only by morning.
