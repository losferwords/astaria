# The Great Drought of Amon-Astat

![[Assets/Images/Amon-Astat_Drought.png]]

The Great Drought was a calamity that gripped [[Амон-Астат|Amon-Astat]] from 94 to 100 NE. Within a few years, the loss of water ruined farming settlements, filled the cities with starving refugees and undermined the authority of the [[Хат-Астат|Sun faith]]. The disaster gave rise to the [[Кризис веры в Амон-Астате|crisis of faith]] and, eventually, the [[Война Жаждущих|War of the Thirsty]].

## A Land Without Water

Rivers and irrigation canals sustained life in the Empire of the Sun. As the water receded, fields and reed farms lost their harvests. Even the [[Река Хапи|Hapi]], upon which countless households depended, almost dried up. Wells and grain stores followed; poor families lost their means of survival long before hunger reached palaces and wealthy homes.

Rural families fled to the cities in search of work, food and temple relief. Coastal [[Город Уасет|Waset]] could still draw upon its fishing grounds, but refugees and the shortage of fresh water made life harsh even there. The authorities seized a share of merchants' goods, while temple attendants increasingly tended the dying rather than those who could still be healed.

## Famine and Revolt

In a country that revered [[Аст|the Sun]] as the source of life, the relentless heat took on a terrible meaning. People demanded to know why their god would not save their children and why help failed to reach distant settlements. By 98 NE, mistrust had become attacks upon temples, the expulsion of priests and open rebellion by the [[Жаждущие|Thirsty]].

Pharaoh [[Рамхотеп II|Ramhotep II]] protected Ast's servants and tried to prevent war with neighbouring states. Some rebels nevertheless sought salvation across the border and [[Вторжение Амон-Астата в город Ахей|seized Achaeus]]. The counterattack by [[Гилас|Gilas]] brought war into lands already devastated by drought.

## The Northern Storm

In 100 NE, during the siege of [[Город Атафет|Atafet]], a powerful storm front arrived from the north. Rain swept across Amon-Astat and turned the assault in the defenders' favour. The army of Gilas withdrew; two months later, the pharaoh concluded a peace that secured the release of the occupied lands.

The cause of the drought remains unknown. The anger directed at Ast during the famine does not in itself explain the disaster's origin. Rain ended the drought, but lost harvests, broken families and mistrust of the old authorities left a legacy that a single storm could not wash away.
