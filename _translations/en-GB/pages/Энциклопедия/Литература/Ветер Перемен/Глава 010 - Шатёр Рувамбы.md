# Chapter 010 — Ruwamba's Tent

*Amon-Astat, 103 NE, winter*

The road to [[Закатная долина|Sunset Valley]] led ever farther from the familiar [[Амон-Астат|Amon-Astat]] of temples, markets and pharaonic roads. The land changed: desert aridity yielded to places where the air grew thicker, the vegetation darker, and the proximity of [[Вакумара|Vakumara]] could be felt in every wary glance from the locals.

Not far from the road, the travellers met [[Ошу Мати|Oshu Mati]], an enigmatic young woman of the [[Ваку|Vaku]]. She had made camp and offered to show them the way into the valley. Something in her composure remained guarded, as though she herself did not know whom she feared more: the strangers or those who might find her trail.

Somewhere ahead, across the valley of the [[Река Мвиди|Mvidi]], her mother [[Рувамба Мати|Ruwamba Mati]], High [[Шаман|Shaman]] of the Vaku, was making her way home. She had halted near [[Деревня Дабу|Dabu]], a border village where the authority of the Empire of the Sun already contended with the older fears of the western lands.

During the journey, [[Хонсу-Намун|Khonsu-Namun]] contacted the company—or a voice far too like his did. He asked where they were going, and the connection ended so abruptly that an unpleasant sense of another presence lingered in the air. Since finding the [[Чёрный Вестник|Black Harbinger]], such oddities could no longer be dismissed as chance.

That night, a nightmare seized Oshu. Her body convulsed, her breathing came in ragged bursts, and the face concealed by the memory of terrible wounds seemed to suffer claws, shadows and torn flesh anew. Then the dream broke: a hand covered her lips, a gentle fragrance from childhood filled the air, and Oshu fell asleep once more—peacefully this time.

Morning brought no safety. Ruwamba Mati overtook the travellers during a halt and took them prisoner. They were led to a tent near Dabu, heavy with the scents of herbs, incense, smoke and something bitterly medicinal. Ruwamba questioned them without gentleness. She wished to know whether any [[Имитей|Imithei]] stood among her captives, testing them with potions, smoke and hypnosis as a physician probes a wound: without mercy, but with precision.

Unlike the others, Oshu was not bound. Her mother spoke to her with restraint, almost calmly, though years of longing lay behind that composure. Ruwamba had missed her daughter and wanted to understand why she had left. From fragments of speech, old wounds and nocturnal fits, she gradually drew a dreadful conclusion: Oshu had become Mara—“not dead”. Among the Vaku this was more than an illness; it was a mark, a terror and a sentence bound to [[Руфу|the God of Death]].

Ruwamba advised her daughter to seek someone capable of entering the depths of the mind and healing nightmares. She had heard that, on the far side of the world in [[Хамоа|Hamoa]], the finest healers could treat even such afflictions. It was also far from Rufu.

The shaman's attention then turned to [[Данис ибн Синай|Danis ibn Sinai]]. A dagger protruded from his side, and Ruwamba considered whether to prolong his suffering or end it. Oshu asked her to save him, and the request surprised her mother. Ruwamba called Danis all but dead, yet agreed to “read” him.

She recognised a [[Кадийцы|Qadian]], one of a people bound to sand, time and the secrets of the desert. Ruwamba turned his head towards an hourglass and commanded him to watch. The sand fell until the air itself began to thicken. Each grain slowed, slower still, until one hung motionless.

Danis realised that time had stopped. He could not move; even his gaze refused him. Only the pain in his side remained alive. It seemed to take an eternity for the dagger to be drawn. Then the grain fell, the world resumed its course, and oblivion swallowed him.

When Urist regained consciousness, he found Ruwamba's unbroken stare, Danis's bloodied side bound with herbs, and Oshu cleaning her dagger. The shaman said that [[Урист Китин|Urist]] had raised a hand against her daughter, and his fate therefore rested with Oshu. The young woman spared him.

Ruwamba regarded the Avgar's strength with almost predatory respect. She spoke of his shoulders, hands and blood as though she saw beyond the craftsman to a warrior whose family had tethered him to the forge to silence the drums within his heart. If Urist found a worthy weapon, she said, he might awaken the power of his wild blood and set foot upon the path of an Imithei. For now, however, another interested her more.

Her attention shifted to [[Томас Робинсон|Tom]].

When Tom awoke, Ruwamba recoiled as though she had seen someone other than the man she expected. She demanded his name and told him that during the night he had fought as though death itself guided his hand. The wound from his arrow upon her shoulder had almost closed already. The shaman set a dagger to his throat and looked deeper. For an instant, darkness filled Tom's eyes and his face became vacant and remote. Ruwamba leapt back again, wary and furious. Then Tom returned to himself.

Something outside distracted the shaman. Mei had followed the travellers' trail to the tent. The tension could easily have become another battle, but a discussion of their purpose changed Ruwamba's judgement. Learning that they needed a remedy for the journey into the Ashlands, she agreed to help.

According to her, the travellers would perish there less from the heat than from the guardian of the [[Сердце Огня|Heart of Fire]]. A particular poison might overcome it. Ruwamba did not reveal all she knew, but her aid was as perilous as her refusal: the gifts of a Vaku shaman seldom carried the scent of simple kindness.

She said, “So be it,” gathered a strange powder into her palms and snapped her fingers—and everyone awoke the next morning in their own camp, as though the night in the tent had never happened. Yet Mei was beside them, Danis lived, and among their supplies lay a large flask of ice-grip salve.

The expedition into the Ashlands was no longer a mad fancy. It had become a matter of preparation.
