# Rashaad al-Sakhar

![[Assets/Images/Rashaad al-Sakhar.jpg]]

## Main text

**Rashaad al-Sakhar** is a young [[Кадийцы|Qadian]] from [[Город Самаан|Samaan]], a pupil of the Way of the Blade and a fugitive wanted by the Avengers. His father died in unexplained circumstances, leaving the boy to grow up upon the streets of the port city. There he learnt to notice danger, make decisions quickly and disappear before an adult could demand an explanation.

[[Малик Аль-Азвар|Malik al-Azwar]] noticed the youth and recognised his uncommon agility and speed of reaction. Rashaad completed his training in the Way of the Blade and might have become one of the finest warriors in western Qadir, but mastery of the sword was not enough for him. He refused to accept that his father's murder should remain no more than an old entry in a city archive.

Rashaad turned to the study of the Way of Times and unlawfully drew upon the [[Часы Времён|Clock of Time]], hoping to witness the past and name the killer. He gained the opportunity he sought, but in doing so stole a measure of power from the sacred source and broke one of [[Альзаман|Alzaman's]] strictest prohibitions.

The Avengers now pursue him in order to recover the stolen time. Rashaad does not consider himself an enemy of Qadir. He believes that a law which guards a murderer's secret more faithfully than a victim's memory deserves to be broken. The conviction makes him both understandable and dangerous—particularly if the power of the Clock continues to alter the man who carries it within him.

While fleeing, Rashaad met [[Авеста Кронос|Avesta Kronos]], who was hiding from a different guard and from her own past. Their path through the [[Пустыня Халисат|Khalisat Desert]] transformed a simple escape into a search for answers about the fates of their fathers. Rashaad trusts facts and swift decisions, whereas Avesta perceives signs whose meaning has yet to reveal itself. Each therefore notices what the other might overlook.

To some, Rashaad is a criminal. To others, he is evidence that even a wise tradition may leave an ordinary person alone before injustice.
