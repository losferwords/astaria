# Chen Lianshi

![[Assets/Images/Chen_Lianshi.jpg]]

## Main text

Born in the shadow of an ancient lineage, the inquisitive and cheerful **Chen Lianshi** never sought either power or wealth. The Lianshi family regarded him as a peculiar shoot upon the ancestral tree—endearing, but useless. When his younger sister [[Сунь Ляньши|Sun Lianshi]] unexpectedly ascended the throne, however, fate changed its course. Chen found himself within the golden walls of the palace at the [[Храм Дракона|Temple of the Dragon]], where every step was accompanied by a bow, an intrigue or a suspicious glance.

Court life did him little good. Flighty, contemplative and disorganised by nature, he could not conform to the rigid cadence of ceremony. He arrived late to audiences, brought tea liberally laced with baijiu and recited poetry at full voice in the palace gardens, alarming court ladies instructed to avoid such “unpredictable elements”. His drinking and free thought eventually endangered both himself and the honour of his house. The family condemned him, and the empress was unable to protect him. In a moment perhaps of insight—or perhaps of cowardice—Chen voluntarily took monastic vows.

Eager to be rid of the troublesome monk, his family sent him to [[Город Чанг-Ша|Chang-Sha]], where [[Шень Ву|Shen Wu]], one of the most controversial [[Аватар|Avatars]], became his mentor. Once a warrior and sage and now a living legend, Shen was by then scarcely a conventional master of martial arts. Instead of training, teacher and pupil spent hours beneath a blossoming peach tree discussing harmony and adding another drop of “purifying tea” to their cups. Chen never mastered the martial arts, never became an Avatar and was barred from the Temple. Yet something within him changed: he ceased to desire power and began to seek truth—or, at the very least, a reason to drink with an agreeable companion.

He now drifts through the streets of [[Город Джу-Суо|Dju-Suo]], most often through its taverns, in search of a free cup, a compelling story or a lost soul whom he might help with a few words. No one quite understands why. Some call him idle; others, an eccentric saint. Yet anyone who has spoken with Chen beneath the rain or the stars departs feeling that they met a man of unclouded spirit, even if that spirit was always just a little merry with drink.

Chen still loves his sister, Empress Sun Lianshi, and though they seldom meet, he never speaks of her without quiet reverence. He does not believe himself worthy of the palace. Perhaps, however, his gentle simplicity is precisely what the throne needs most.
