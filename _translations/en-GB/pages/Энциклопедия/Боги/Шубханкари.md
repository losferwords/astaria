# Shubhankari

Shubhankari is the supreme goddess of [[Сурадж Ка Гхар|Suraj Ka Ghar]] and the [[Раджати|Rajati]], embodying rebirth and the indivisibility of creation from destruction. In her faith, birth cannot be parted from death, celebration from grief, and blood both carries life and marks its ending.

The goddess passes through two great Cycles, each ordinarily lasting close to a century. The transition follows no calendar. It is recognised through changes in her servants' conduct, omens in the temples and visions the Raksha seek through an infusion of red root. Such visions are unreliable: even an experienced Raksha cannot always know whether the goddess spoke or the draught merely fashioned an illusion. For the whole country, each change in Shubhankari's aspect transforms law, ritual and the fabric of daily life.

## Cycle of Creation

In the Cycle of Creation, Shubhankari appears as patron of celebration, music, dance, birth and craft. She grants her followers vitality and inspiration, helps them survive illness and restores what has been destroyed. Temple courtyards open to those in need, while cities vie in the beauty of their processions and the generosity of their feasts.

During this time the [[Ракша|Raksha]] care for children, the sick and the old, study the arts of healing and help raise new sanctuaries. Their blades remain at their sides, but serve as a warning: one day the life accumulated in this season will be demanded back.

## Cycle of Destruction

In the Cycle of Destruction, Shubhankari becomes the goddess of blood and rebirth. The season is called the Great Harvest, and the Raksha become gatherers of human vitality. Their Raktakatars are grown from their own blood and bound to their wielders as intimately as a hand or heart.

The goddess demands sacrifice so that she may be sated and turn once more towards Creation. Her faithful believe that the dead do not vanish, but return with Shubhankari in a renewed world. To neighbouring peoples, the doctrine sounds like an excuse for horror; to many Rajati, it remains the only hope by which the inevitability of the Harvest can be endured.

## Appearance

The Raksha who claim to have seen the goddess during the Great Harvest describe six arms, blue-tinged skin and teeth as sharp as blades. She wears magnificent red garments and a spiked crown set with rubies. In her gentler aspect, her images soften: flowers, bowls of grain and musical instruments replace the weapons, though the six-armed figure remains unchanged.

The present Cycle of Destruction has lasted far beyond its accustomed span. Priests offer different explanations, but agree upon one truth: the balance has been broken, and the country cannot live for ever beneath an upraised blade. Until Shubhankari turns again towards Creation, every festival in Suraj Ka Ghar will sound like a defiant promise of spring to come.
