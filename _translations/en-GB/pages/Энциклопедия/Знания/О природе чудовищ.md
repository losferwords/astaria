# On the Nature of Monsters

## Death Remains Death

In the tales of [[Астария|Astaria]], the dead sometimes rise from their graves, empty suits of armour wander by night and scattered bones gather themselves into the likeness of a person. Such stories may frighten a child beside the hearth, but they do not describe the workings of the world. There are no undead in Astaria: the bodies of the dead do not become zombies, skeletons or any other walking corpses.

Stories of restless souls and possession do not mean that an abandoned body has returned to life. A knowledgeable investigator therefore begins by discovering what a frightened witness truly saw, rather than declaring every shadow to be the dead arisen.

## Those Called Demons

The word *demon* seldom means what a foreign traveller expects. Horns alone are no mark of evil: [[Сатир|satyrs]], for example, belong to the [[Первые|Firstborn]], avoid humankind and are usually harmless. In [[Амато|Amato]], the name is given to the [[О́ни|Oni]]—possessed people whose minds have been divided between two wills. They are not horned fiends from the infernal depths, but sick and dangerous people who, above all, require the aid of an [[Онмёдзи|Onmyoji]].

## Lupine Traits and Mutable Forms

Lycanthropy does not exist in Astaria. A person does not become a wolf beneath a full moon, nor do [[Друид|Druids]] assume the shape of beasts. Their horns and other signs of kinship with the forest emerge gradually, but the body remains human.

The [[Вервольф|Wolven]] are a distinct kind of Firstborn, born with permanent lupine traits; they are not humans afflicted by a curse. [[Нереида|Nereids]], likewise, do not transform themselves into other beings by magic. They rely upon mimicry, adaptation of the body and deception of the senses. Outward resemblance does not alter their true nature.

These distinctions matter to more than scholars. A hunter who mistakes a frightened satyr for a fiend, an Oni for a monster from the underworld or a Wolven for a cursed human may themselves become the cause of a tragedy that did not exist before.
