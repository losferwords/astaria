# Vaktars

![[Assets/Images/Vaktars.jpg]]

## Main text

The **Vaktars** are a northern people of central [[Кельн|Keln]], seafarers, hunters and warriors of the Icebound Kingdom of [[Вактар-Йорден|Vaktar-Jorden]]. Their culture arose amid long winters, frozen lakes and cold seas, where survival depends upon people's willingness to trust one another. A Vaktar may argue with a companion all evening, yet still share their last dry cloak when the blizzard comes.

In 1637 ChE, an expedition of [[Друид|Druids]] travelled east to explore the ancient [[Лес Сольвид|Solvid Forest]]. During their journey the [[Надаир|Nadair]] reached the frozen [[Озеро Нордавеллир|Lake Nordavellir]] and found the body of an immense she-dragon beneath its ice. The Druids leading the expedition sensed a faint warmth of life within her, but could not immediately raise her from the depths. For two years the settlers broke through the ice and endured bitter cold, until in 1635 ChE they drew [[Винтра|Wintra]] ashore. In gratitude, the goddess protected the expedition, and her rescuers gradually embraced her as their patron. Thus arose the Vaktars, whose name in the Dair language means ‘protectors’.

The worship of Vintra changed the descendants of the Nadair. They retained their reverence for living land and ancestral memory, but placed truth, fortitude and righteous vengeance beside them. A lie is considered dangerous not only to the speaker: aboard a ship or in winter quarters it may kill everyone. An honest admission of error therefore often earns greater respect than any attempt to save face at all costs.

The Vaktars are equally at ease upon a snowbound road and on the waters of the [[Хтоническое море|Chthonic Sea]]. Their ships have stout hulls, a shallow draught and ample room for winter stores. Helmsmen read the ice by colour and sound, while hunters recognise an approaching storm in the behaviour of animals. A long voyage marks a young Vaktar's coming of age, but undertaking it unprepared is regarded not as courage, but as forcing others to pay for one's pride.

The common hearth occupies the heart of a home. Through winter, families gather around it to mend equipment, carve runes, tell sagas and settle disputes. Feasts are loud, but the place of a fallen companion remains empty until the evening ends, with a cup and a portion of the meal set beside it.

Many warriors dream of falling gloriously and earning Wintra's favour. The goddess may return the worthy as [[Вознесённый|Ascended]], yet the Vaktars do not deem every death heroic. One who abandons their companions for a splendid feat is unlikely to enter the goddess's halls; a defender who remains standing so that others may escape has already fulfilled the meaning of their people's name.
