# Ur-Blyat

## The pot to which everyone brought something

**Ur-Blyat** is a thick Avgar stew without a single correct recipe. It is made at the end of a long crossing, after shared labour or whenever the guests have suddenly grown more numerous than the food. Into a great pot go scraps of meat, grain, roots, dried mushrooms, onions, leftover stock and anything edible that those around the fire are willing to share.

The name is associated with an old Avgar expression uttered by a cook who discovers that the customary provisions have run out. Foreigners often mistake it for a curse. Their hosts reply that Ur-Blyat becomes a curse only when someone arrives empty-handed and is the first to demand a second helping.

The dish has one essential rule: every adult must put at least something into the pot. A wealthy guest may bring meat and spices, a child a handful of herbs, a traveller a pinch of salt. Its flavour can never be known beforehand, but a fortunate mixture gives the evening a name worth remembering. ‘That Ur-Blyat by the ford’ may enter family history as surely as victory in a duel.

The stew simmers for hours over a low fire, until the grain thickens the broth and its many scents become one. The first bowl is given to the person who worked hardest; the last is kept for a traveller who has yet to arrive. City taverns serve deliberately extravagant versions of Ur-Blyat, but Avgars maintain that the dish loses its meaning when the cook knows every ingredient in advance.
